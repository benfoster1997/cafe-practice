# Channel Operating System

The complete system for running the channel, in seven modules. Everything
here is grounded in the verified research (`docs/YOUTUBE_AI_MONETISATION_STUDY.md`);
where popular "YouTube strategy" advice conflicts with the evidence, the
evidence wins and the deviation is noted. Companion docs: the step-by-step
routine lives in `docs/YOUTUBE_BEGINNER_GUIDE.md`; milestones and review
gates in `docs/YOUTUBE_ACTION_PLAN.md`.

## Module 1 — Positioning & channel identity

**Niche (locked):** finance/business storytelling — true stories of frauds,
collapses, and unlikely fortunes. Never investment advice.

**Positioning statement:** *Documentary-grade tellings of business stories
the internet forgot, built from court records and primary documents, told
by a calm British narrator.* The competitors are either huge (MagnatesMedia
— we out-niche them on forgotten stories) or slop (we out-research them by
default).

**What makes it defensible:** every video contains details competitors
don't have, because scripts are built from judgments, filings and archives
(free), not Wikipedia. This is also exactly what YouTube's inauthentic-
content policy rewards.

**Channel name (DECIDED 11 Aug 2026): Cooked Books.**
Chosen after collision checks: "Paper Empires" clashes with existing
channels and a music label; "Cooked Books" is unique in the finance space,
instantly signals the fraud/collapse niche, and is short and brandable.
Runner-up kept in reserve: The Money Graveyard.

**Format (locked):** 9–12 min narrated documentaries; Kokoro "George"
voice; three-act structure; cold open ≤45s; one video/week, same day/time.

**Content pillars** (each video belongs to exactly one):
- **P1 — The Frauds:** cons, Ponzis, accounting scandals (Video 1 lives here)
- **P2 — The Collapses:** companies and empires that died, and why
- **P3 — Stranger Than Fiction:** bizarre schemes, unlikely fortunes, weird
  monopolies
- **P4 — How It Really Works:** the hidden mechanics of familiar money
  things (used sparingly — highest competition)

Cadence across pillars: roughly P1 40%, P2 30%, P3 20%, P4 10%. The
pillars share one audience (curious adults, 25–55, US/UK-heavy — the
high-RPM demographic) so every video feeds the same viewer pool.

## Module 3 — Packaging & retention playbook (the video creation engine)

**Idea → package, in that order.** Title and thumbnail are decided BEFORE
the script is written. If we can't state the hook in one line, the story
isn't ready.

**Title formulas that fit the channel** (pick per video, keep honest):
- The [impossible thing]: *The Computer That Invented 64,000 People*
- Number + irony: *IPO to Bankruptcy in 68 Days*
- The [person] who [audacious act]: *The Man Who Printed Real Money*
- Question the viewer can't answer: *Why Is Trading Onions a Federal Crime?*
Rules: ≤60 characters where possible; no clickbait the video doesn't pay
off (policy + trust); front-load the hook words.

**Thumbnail rules:** one idea, one image, ≤5 words, high contrast, no
faces needed (we have none) — favour object+text irony (VOID stamp,
dissolving silhouettes, burning banknote). Build 2 per video; A/B test via
Studio's Test & Compare once impressions allow (~1,000+/variant).

**Cold open (0:00–0:45) — the retention gate.** Structure: (1) drop the
viewer mid-scene at the most impossible moment; (2) the jaw-drop number;
(3) the dark twist line; (4) "this is the story of…" — title card, music
in. Never start with "welcome back" or channel branding. The study's
verified benchmark: typical CTR is 2–10% and packaging + first 30 seconds
decide whether impressions grow — everything else is downstream.

**Body retention techniques** (used in Video 1, standard from now on):
- Three acts with an escalation at each act break ("but that's not the
  crazy part")
- Open loops: plant the twist early ("the man who exposed it would pay the
  highest price"), pay off in Act 3
- Visual change every 3–6 seconds; documents on screen when quoted
- Re-hook every ~90 seconds (a question, a reversal, a number)
- Music drops to silence at the two biggest beats
- No mid-video CTA before the climax; one calm subscribe line after it
- End screen: tease the next story specifically ("a Victorian fraudster
  built a ballroom under a lake") → next video + subscribe elements

**Full scripts:** drafted by Claude from the fact pack, edited by Ben
(authorship pass), narrated by George. Per-video packaging (5 titles, 2
thumbnails, description, tags) ships in each video's PRODUCTION_KIT.

## Module 4 — Algorithm & growth playbook

How the machine actually works (from YouTube's own documentation and the
study's verified data — not folklore): YouTube offers impressions; **CTR**
decides how many become views; **average view duration / percentage**
decides whether YouTube offers more impressions; **session time** (does
the viewer keep watching anything after your video) compounds it;
**returning viewers** build browse-feed distribution. There is no
"algorithm trick" — there is packaging, retention, and consistency.

**Traffic-source strategy by stage:**
- Months 1–6: search + suggested carry new channels → titles include
  searchable story names; descriptions name people/companies; end screens
  chain to our own adjacent videos to build session time.
- After ~10 videos: browse/home grows as returning viewers accumulate →
  consistency of format and voice is what makes viewers "returning".
- Shorts (from Phase 2): 2–3/week cut from long-form best moments,
  pointing at the long video. Discovery only — Shorts revenue is ~nothing
  (verified: $32 per million views in the study's test case).

**Testing system:**
- Thumbnails: Test & Compare on every video once impressions support it
- Titles: if a video underperforms its pillar median CTR after 2–4 weeks,
  swap to the next title candidate (one change at a time)
- Format experiments: maximum one variable per video (length, structure,
  pillar) — never redesign everything after one flop

**Subscriber conversion:** consistent narrator + consistent format + a
specific next-video tease convert casual viewers into returning ones;
subscribe ask once per video, post-climax, benefit-framed ("one forgotten
money story every week").

**Posting frequency:** one long-form/week, same slot. The verified winner
pattern is 1–2 quality uploads/week, not volume — and volume without
variation is literally the demonetisation profile.

**Realistic benchmarks** (from the study; use in reviews, not as vanity):
CTR 2–10% (4–6% healthy); 40%+ average retention on 10-min videos is
solid early, 50%+ is strong; tens-of-views videos for months 1–3 is
normal, not failure.

- [ ] Attributions kept ("Fortune reported…", "the court found…")

- [ ] Narration listened through once (names/numbers correct)

- [ ] "Not made for kids" + "Altered content: Yes" set

channel's asset is long-form watch time, and a solo operator's hours are
the scarcest resource. Shorts-on-YouTube only (they at least feed the
same channel), starting Phase 2. Revisit other platforms at Gate 2 only
if a specific sponsor/audience case appears.

| 3 Scale | ~25–50k subs | Sponsorships: listing on marketplaces + inbound; rate card ~£200–£1,000+/integration; 1–2/month max, always disclosed, never crypto/gambling/finance-advice sponsors (policy + trust) | This closes the £2k gap: ads + sponsors + affiliate at ~350k views/mo |

financial advice; selling a course about YouTube (the study's finding on
who really profits from that is a warning, not a playbook).

## Module 7 — Analytics & optimisation system

**Weekly review (15 min, every Monday, in YouTube Studio):**
1. Last video: impressions, CTR vs channel median, AVD, retention graph —
   note the timestamp of the biggest drop and what was on screen
2. Traffic sources: what's growing (search terms → future title ideas)
3. One sentence in a running log: "what I'd do differently"
4. Any Test & Compare verdicts → apply the winner's lesson forward

**Monthly review (60 min, first Monday):**
1. Rank all videos by AVD and by CTR; find the shared traits of the top
   25% (pillar? hook style? story era?) → reorder the idea bank
2. Returning vs new viewers trend — is the audience compounding?
3. Subs per 1,000 views by video — which stories convert watchers to
   subscribers?
4. Re-check: retention graphs of the 2 worst videos — diagnose (packaging
   mismatch? slow open? confusing middle?) and write the fix into the next
   script
5. Update `STORY_BACKLOG.md` and refill Tier A if below 6

**Decision rules (mechanical, so feelings don't decide):**
- CTR <3% after 1,000+ impressions → test second thumbnail, then title
- >45% of viewers gone by 0:45 → cold-open problem; next two scripts get
  restructured opens
- AVD rising but views flat after 10+ videos → packaging is the
  constraint, not content; double down on Module 3
- Any video >2× channel-median views → make its closest sibling story
  from the idea bank within 3 weeks (strike while the topic is proven)
- Quarterly: score against the Gates in `docs/YOUTUBE_ACTION_PLAN.md`

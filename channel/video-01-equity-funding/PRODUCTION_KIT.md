## Description (paste, then personalise)

In 1973, Wall Street darling Equity Funding Corporation of America was
caught carrying roughly 64,000 life-insurance policyholders who did not
exist — invented by its mainframe, sold to reinsurers for cash, and, when
the company needed more, "killed off" for the death benefits. This is the
story of the first great fraud of the computer age, the whistleblower who
didn't trust the regulator, the journalist who didn't believe it, and the
analyst the government punished for exposing it — all the way to the
Supreme Court.

Sources include the US Supreme Court's opinion in Dirks v. SEC (463 U.S.
646), the D.C. Circuit record (681 F.2d 824), the SEC's contemporaneous
1973 enforcement digest, court records in Goldblum v. NBC, Fortune's 1973
reporting, and published fraud case studies. Key sources:
- https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/646/
- https://www.sec.gov/news/digest/1973/dig110273.pdf
- https://fortune.com/article/those-daring-young-con-men-of-equity-funding
- https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/584/904/155289/

This video uses AI-assisted research and production. All named individuals'
conduct is described per court records and published reporting.

# Strategic position after eight kills — the decision document

17 Aug 2026. Written for the owner. Plain English. The honest-expectations
rule applies: nothing here is softened.

Backing research: `reports/strategy-P1..P5-*.md` (five paths, each argued
and attacked) and `reports/strategy-CRITIC.md` (a critic whose only job was
to find what the other five got wrong). Kill evidence: `KILL_LEDGER.md`.

---

## 1. What happened

Eight product concepts have now been validated adversarially and **all
eight were killed on evidence**. That is no longer bad luck. Four
mechanisms repeat, and together they close the entire category we were
searching:

1. **The swarm.** Cheap AI building means anything findable by keyword
   search gets cloned within months — often before the law even bites. We
   found 10+ waste-tracking products 14 months before the mandate, and a
   Martyn's Law clone 8 months before enforcement.
2. **The state prices at zero.** In UK compliance, the government ships
   free portals and associations ship free member tools.
3. **Liability inversion.** Where paperwork carries legal liability,
   buyers get *more* cautious about who they buy from, not less. Our
   supposed moat was a wall. Worse, government approved-vendor lists and
   association supplier slots hand that trust to whoever arrived first.
4. **Distribution was always the real constraint** — not ideas, not build
   quality.

**And the deepest problem: the search method itself.** Every concept came
from asking "what UK regulation is coming?" — which is exactly the query
every AI-clone builder also runs. We were fishing the most-fished pond by
construction, and doing it very thoroughly.

## 2. The honest odds, path by path

Five strategic paths were analysed independently, then a critic attacked
all five. Probability = reaching £2,335/month within 24 months.

| Path | Odds | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Productised service (6-8 clients at £395-800, sold by phone) | **10-12%** | Best of the set — but it is a service business, not the software business in the brief |
| Buy an existing revenue stream (~£1,000-1,400/mo book, funded by a Start Up Loan) | **9%** | Only path with revenue on day one — and the only one that can end at −£25k of personal debt |
| Bespoke systems (£6-10k build + £300-500/mo retainer, 5-6 clients) | **8%** | Best revenue per client; hardest first sale |
| Partner/channel-led (accountants, franchisors, associations) | **7%** | Needs 4-12 productive partners, not 1-3; 9-18 month lag |
| Audience-first (build a following, then sell) | **5-6%** | Only works if the audience is *inside one trade* |
| Non-regulatory pain mining (better ideas, same method) | **3-5%** | Fixes idea generation; leaves distribution untouched |
| **The current plan** — build SaaS, sell to 24-30 strangers at £79-99 | **2-4%** | Dead as specified |
| Civic buyers (parish councils, clubs, churches) | **2%** | Price ceiling £12-30/mo means you'd need 80-195 customers |

**Aggregate, and this is the number that matters:**

- ~**15%** of hitting the target by *some* route within 24 months — **if
  you start selling or buying within 30 days**.
- ~**4-6%** if it must be software sold to 24-30 strangers.
- ~**2%, and falling**, if the next three months look like the last five:
  more desk research, no customer conversations, no money committed.

### Three things that killed the old assumptions

- **The parked YouTube channel is not a distribution asset for a B2B
  product.** The arithmetic is brutal and settles it: even at the
  channel's optimistic 18-36 month scale (~15,000 UK subscribers), the
  expected number of subscribers inside a 5,000-firm target trade is about
  **1.4 people** — and you couldn't identify them anyway. Cooked Books may
  be worth doing on its own terms, but not as a route to software
  customers. It also eats exactly the same evenings.
- **"One accountant with 80 clients ≈ your whole target" is false.**
  Partners take 10-60% margin and compress price to roughly £8/client/month
  — not £79-99. You need 4-12 productive partners out of 20-60
  relationships.
- **Nobody has ever tested the one variable that dominates every
  calculation:** whether you will actually hold conversations with
  strangers, repeatedly, when it is unpleasant, for a year. The critic
  puts it at 35-45% and notes we have six months of evidence that you
  persist at *research* and zero observations of you persisting at
  *selling*. Because this term multiplies into every path, **trying three
  paths does not triple your odds** — it gives you roughly the best single
  path plus a few points.

## 3. My recommendation

**Stop researching. Test whether you will sell, before choosing what to
sell.**

Eight kills answered a question that was never the binding one. A ninth
concept-validation round would be repeating a disproven experiment. The
binding question is not "which niche?" — it is "will this founder do
outbound?", and it can be answered in 14 days for about £35.

**Do this, in this order:**

**Tonight, free, two hours.** Three questions the whole plan hangs on:
1. Can you free up any *daytime* hours (a four-day week, occasional
   contract days)? If yes, straightforward contracting at UK rates
   (~£390-470/day) hits £2,335/month with roughly six billed days a month
   — more reliably than anything else in this document. It deserves
   pricing properly before you read further.
2. Does your employment contract contain IP-assignment or moonlighting
   clauses? An IP clause capturing work you create during employment would
   kill the software path outright and leave a service path untouched.
   Twenty minutes with the contract.
3. Are you genuinely willing to phone strangers in the evening for a year?
   An honest "no" here is not a failure — it redirects to the acquisition
   path, where the binding skill is diligence, which you have actually
   demonstrated.

**Days 1-14 — the conversation test (~£35).** Pick one trade of UK limited
companies. Send 20 plain-text emails a day for ten days from a real
mailbox, and phone the ones you can reach. No pitch, no product, one
question: *"How do you currently handle [specific monthly chore]? I'm not
selling anything and I'll send you what I learn."*

Log three numbers only: conversations held, hours spent, and one honest
sentence about how it felt on day 10 versus day 1. That third one predicts
month 14 better than the other two.

**The gate — write it down before you start:**
- Under 5 conversations from ~200 approaches → the constraint is
  execution, not niche choice. Divide every probability above by three and
  stop hunting for a ninth idea.
- 5-15 → the channel works. Pick the most-repeated chore you heard, sell it
  as a done-by-hand service at £395-495/month, deliver it manually with
  Claude behind the curtain, and only automate once someone has paid twice.
- Over 15 → we have all been too pessimistic; go straight to the
  higher-priced bespoke option.

**Whatever the result: no production code until someone has paid.** The
brief's stage order (research → spec → build → then customers) produced
five months and eight kills without a single buyer conversation. That
ordering should be reversed, and the first milestone should become **£500/
month from under 10 customers**, not £2,000 from 24-48.

## 4. What I am NOT recommending, and why

- **Not the £25k Start Up Loan acquisition.** It scores well (9%) and it
  is the only path with revenue on day one, but it is unsecured *personal*
  debt against an adversely-selected pool of businesses for sale. That is
  a decision only you can make, with your own money at risk, and it should
  never be taken on a Claude recommendation. If you want to explore it,
  the first step is free: one phone call to a Start Up Loans delivery
  partner asking whether they fund acquisitions at all (unverified, and
  the scheme is framed for businesses *trading*, not buying).
- **Not the US cold-calling plan the critic ranked first.** Its logic is
  elegant — US Eastern business hours fall inside UK evenings, roughly 2.5×
  the usable selling time. But it rests on my own unverified assumption
  that you work a UK day job (see §5), and it walks into US telemarketing
  law (TCPA, do-not-call rules, stricter state statutes) that no agent in
  this session verified properly. Do not dial a US number on the strength
  of this document.
- **Not resuming YouTube as a route to software customers.** The
  arithmetic above kills that specific idea. As a business in its own
  right it remains what the original study said: £0 for months, £2k/month
  a top-few-percent 18-36 month outcome. It competes for the same evenings.

## 5. Where I was wrong, and what still needs checking

**My error.** I told the research agents you have "a day job and
evenings/weekends" and "limited capital". Neither is recorded anywhere in
this repo — I inferred them. Several conclusions, including the critic's
top-ranked path, lean on that assumption. If your actual availability is
different, tell me and the ranking changes.

**Needs your browser (the proxy blocks these for me):**
- The ONS business-population split. Two figures in our research look
  contradictory but are both right about different groups: **~63% of *all*
  UK businesses** (5.7m, including 3.2m tiny sole traders) can't lawfully
  be cold-emailed — but of **VAT/PAYE-registered** businesses (~2.7m, the
  only ones big enough to pay £400/month), roughly three-quarters are
  limited companies and *can* be emailed. Practical upshot: cold email to
  real target customers is largely open to you. Worth confirming.
- Whether Start Up Loans fund acquisitions (one phone call).
- Any vendor price in the kill ledger you want to act on — all of them
  come from search extracts, not live pages.

**The honest bottom line.** The £2,335/month goal is not absurd — it is six
clients at £400, or three consultancy retainers. What is dead is the method
of finding an un-swarmed niche by desk research and building software for
strangers before speaking to any of them. Your odds go from ~2% to ~15% the
moment you start talking to potential customers, and not before. Everything
else in this repo is preparation for a conversation that hasn't happened
yet.

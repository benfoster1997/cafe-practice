# Concept R1 Validation: Party Wall / RICS-AI Report Workflow — VERDICT: KILL

## 1. Saturation check — FAILED, and worse than the three killed concepts

The niche is not "about to be swarmed." It is already swarmed, and the swarm includes the exact product described in R1:

- **SurveyorSuite** (surveyorsuite.co.uk) — from **£35/month (£30 annual)**, 14-day free trial. AI-drafts reports from site notes across **15 templates including Party Wall, Schedule of Condition, Dilapidations, Scott Schedule, Damp & Timber**, Levels 1–3, EWS1, HHSRS, Reinstatement Cost. Explicitly "built around the RICS Professional Standard on AI (effective 9 March 2026)": every report auto-includes **AI disclosure, named surveyor sign-off, immutable timestamps, and a complete audit trail**, with a dedicated "RICS AI compliance for surveyors" page (surveyorsuite.co.uk/ai). It uses Anthropic's Claude for drafting. This is concept R1 — including all three proposed niches (party wall, dilapidations, damp/timber) — shipped, at roughly half the proposed floor price.
- **SurveyorPro** (softomatesolutions.com/surveyorpro) — **£79/month**, "all-in-one party wall software": notices, awards, deadlines, CRM, file management, Xero + Outlook + Land Registry integrations, "RICS-style award templates with AI clause library for Schedule of Condition, Section 10 and Section 11 provisions, with a surveyor review gate enforced."
- **Party Wall PRO** (partywallpro.com) — from **£69/month**, party-wall-specific practice management: automated notice creation, deadline tracking, dated file per matter kept "for appeals and professional indemnity purposes."
- **SurvPal** (survpal.co.uk) — party-wall-specific document generation: "perfectly formatted party wall notices, awards and documents in seconds," editable templates, automated reminders, company-wide pricing.
- **PocketSurvey** — a party wall survey app line; plus horizontal incumbents **GoReport** (Schedule of Condition, dilapidations — used widely; pricing on request), **Imfuna Surveyor** and **Kykloud** ("nine of the top 10 UK surveying firms").
- **go-legal.ai** already sells an AI-powered party wall agreement template to consumers — weekend-clone evidence at the document level.
- Secondary swarm signal: party wall keywords are carpeted with near-identical AI-generated SEO blogs (partywallsurveyorlondon.uk, nottinghillsurveyors.com, wimbledonsurveyors.com, kingstonsurveyors.com, princesurveyors.co.uk) all churning "2026 RICS AI standard / construction boom" content. The cheap-AI swarm has demonstrably found this niche — exactly the dynamic that killed the previous three concepts, arrived here first.

Four to five party-wall-specific tools competing for a ~1,000-person profession is saturation by any measure.

## 2. Population & enumerability — passes on paper, but the pond is tiny and contested

- Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors: **~900 members** (fpws.org.uk/about-us), with a public members directory (enumerable — good).
- Pyramus & Thisbe Society: **~1,000 members**, but that includes solicitors, barristers, structural engineers and architects, and overlaps heavily with FPWS (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramus_and_Thisbe_Club; pyramusandthisbesociety.org).
- Realistic universe: perhaps 500–1,500 practising individuals, many doing party wall as one service line inside a general practice. 24–30 customers = 2–5% penetration — arithmetically possible, but you would be the **fifth-or-later vendor** into that pond, with the closest substitute at £35/month. Every marginal customer is a competitive displacement sale, not a greenfield sale.

## 3. Buyer economics — the time-saving argument is real, which is precisely why it's already been made

- Awards typically bill **£700–£1,500** (agreed-surveyor matters £900–£2,700; two-surveyor £1,800–£5,400); hourly rates £150–£300; notices £20–£150; Schedules of Condition £300–£600 per adjoining property (getpine.co.uk, hoa.org.uk, checkatrade.com cost guides, 2026). £79/month against hours of Word formatting per job is an easy ROI story — but SurveyorPro's own marketing already makes exactly this stack-cost pitch, and SurveyorSuite undercuts it at £35. The concept has no pricing room: £49–99 is above the incumbent AI product and level with the practice-management tools.

## 4. Current workflow — Word templates, yes, but the free-association-tool criterion triggers

- FPWS gives members a free **'Draft Standard Award Template' Word document** (fpws.org.uk document search).
- The RICS party wall professional standard (7th edition, reissued 2023) ships appendices with **draft letters, draft notices and a suggested draft award** (rics.org PDF); isurv hosts the download set; firms like Collier Stevens give away free notice templates.
- So the core statutory document set is served free by the professional bodies themselves — the "no free government/association tool doing the same job" test fails at the template layer. What the free templates don't do (assembly, photos, tracking) is what the five paid tools above do.

## 5. RICS AI standard (9 March 2026) — real, but a weak and already-claimed wedge

Confirmed: RICS "Responsible use of artificial intelligence in surveying practice," in force 9 March 2026, mandatory for RICS members and regulated firms (rics.org; cms.law; beale-law.com; brodies.com). Requirements: written client disclosure before work commences (in engagement letters), risk register and due-diligence documentation, baseline staff AI knowledge, named surveyor accountability for AI outputs, documented judgment overrides. Three fatal problems for R1's moat claim:
1. **It only applies to AI use with "material impact"** — a small practice can comply by simply not using AI. The compliance need is created by adopting the product, not by the regulation itself. That is a much weaker forcing function than, say, a filing deadline.
2. **Party wall surveying is unregulated** — anyone can act as a party wall surveyor under the 1996 Act, and many FPWS members are not RICS-regulated, so the standard doesn't even bind a chunk of the target niche.
3. **The compliance-artefact feature is already SurveyorSuite's headline positioning** (AI disclosure block + audit trail on every award), and the surrounding artefacts (policies, registers, engagement-letter clauses) are Word documents that law firms (Beale & Co, CMS, Brodies), PI brokers (Forte) and RICS itself are distributing guidance for. A compliance pack is a one-off template purchase, not a £79/month recurring duty.

## 6. Legal sensitivity — active liability headwind, not just friction

A party wall award is a quasi-judicial statutory document, appealable in county court under s10(17). Practitioner commentary is explicit: the surveyor is "personally responsible for every word in the Award regardless of how it was produced"; a negligently drafted award can run to six-figure damages; issuing awards containing unreviewed AI-generated opinions risks RICS conduct proceedings; the "assistance is not authorship" debate is live in the community (wimbledonsurveyors.com, partywallservice.com, kingstonsurveyors.com). PI insurers are actively scrutinising AI use (fortebrokers.com). For a no-name solo-founder vendor with Claude doing the drafting, this means: hard trust barrier, contractual liability-exclusion drafting, likely vendor PI/tech E&O cost, and a buyer whose personal statutory liability makes them conservative about whose tool touches an award.

## 7. Demand-side — the "2026 boom" is SEO fiction; real data says the market is shrinking

The "40% surge in party wall notices" and "6–8 week backlogs" claims come exclusively from the AI content-farm blogs above — unverifiable and self-serving. Actual data: **CPA summer 2026 forecast has construction activity falling ~3.3% in 2026, with private housing RMI "weakening considerably" in H2 2026** (constructuk.com); ONS shows Q1 2026 output +0.4% only because of repair & maintenance, new work −1.9%, **new orders −10.5%**, and private housing R&M −5.0% month-on-month in May 2026 (ons.gov.uk; lexisnexis.com; pbctoday.co.uk). Party wall work tracks domestic extensions/lofts/basements — squarely inside the weak discretionary-RMI segment. You would be launching a fifth-to-market tool into a contracting demand pool.

## Why this fails the founder's own surviving criterion

The proposed moat — "RICS 2026 AI-standard compliance artefacts built in" — is a shipped feature of a £35/month incumbent, not a moat. There is no maintained dataset, no fulfilment component, and the niche is demonstrably not "too small and unglamorous to attract the swarm": it has attracted at least five vertical tools and an AI SEO content industry within roughly a year of the RICS standard being announced. This is the regulation-triggered-keyword-category pattern (RICS AI standard → swarm) that killed the previous three concepts, observed mid-commoditisation.

## What would have to be true to revisit (none currently evidenced)

- SurveyorSuite/SurveyorPro/Party Wall PRO all failing on quality with documented practitioner complaints (no forum evidence found either way — a gap, but the burden is on the concept); or
- a genuinely un-served adjacent statutory workflow with a harder artefact than templated documents. Not found in this pass.

## Research gaps / verify-in-browser list

Vendor sites (surveyorsuite.co.uk, softomatesolutions.com, partywallpro.com, beale-law.com) were egress-blocked for direct fetch, so all pricing/feature claims come from search-result snippets and summaries rather than the live pages — SurveyorSuite's £35/month price and full feature list should be re-verified by loading the site directly before treating this as final. Competitor traction is unknown: SurveyorSuite, SurveyorPro and Party Wall PRO appear to be recent (2025-26) launches and may have few customers, meaning the niche is commoditising rather than fully commoditised — but that cuts against R1 either way, since it proves clone speed. Exact FPWS/P&T membership overlap and the count of RICS members with a party wall specialism were not found. No direct forum/community evidence of tooling complaints or satisfaction was located (FPWS forums are members-only; no Reddit/LinkedIn threads surfaced within budget) — this is the one place counter-evidence could exist. The '40% notice surge' and backlog statistics could not be traced to any primary source and are almost certainly AI-generated SEO content. Launch dates for SurveyorPro and Party Wall PRO were not pinned down precisely.

## Verdict

KILL — the proposed moat is already a shipped feature of an incumbent: SurveyorSuite sells AI-drafted party wall/schedule-of-condition/dilapidations/damp reports with the full RICS-AI compliance artefact set (disclosure, named sign-off, audit trail) built in at £35/month, with four more party-wall-specific tools at £69-79/month competing for a ~1,000-practitioner niche whose underlying demand (private housing RMI) is forecast to shrink through 2026.

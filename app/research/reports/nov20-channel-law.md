# Can the 14-day conversation test be done lawfully, and will it produce conversations? (20 November 2026)

Scope: this is not a new idea search. It checks one thing: whether one person in the UK can lawfully run the conversation test in `app/STATUS.md` (top section) and `STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3, and the selling in `strategy-P3-service-first.md` ("First 90 days"), and whether the test is likely to produce conversations.

This is research, not legal advice. I am not a lawyer.

## Bottom line

1. Phoning small trade firms cold is lawful for one person, for sole traders and companies alike, if every number is first checked against both the TPS and CTPS lists, his number is not withheld, and he gives his name.
2. Cold email is lawful only to limited companies and LLPs. Cold email, text, WhatsApp and social media messages to sole traders need consent that he does not have.
3. The script line "I'm not selling anything", followed by selling to the same people, is what the ICO calls sugging. The ICO treats it as direct marketing. The wording must change.
4. Honest estimate: 0 to 5 conversations from 200 emails alone, 5 to 25 from phoning 200 firms. No one has measured this for UK tradespeople, so these are rough. The "under 5 = stop" gate is fair only if the test is led by phone.
5. Verdict: **LAWFUL_WITH_CHANGES**. The changes add up to about £60 and are listed at the end.

## Q1. The law, channel by channel

The rules come from the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR) and UK GDPR. PECR applies to "a person", so it covers one man with a phone as much as a call centre.

One distinction decides almost everything. PECR splits businesses in two:

- **Corporate subscribers**: companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships and other bodies that are "a legal person distinct from its members". Source: [PECR regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2) (primary).
- **Individual subscribers**: everyone else. The ICO says "sole traders and other types of partnerships are classed as 'individual subscribers'" and "are treated the same as individuals and have greater protections under PECR". Source: [ICO business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) (primary).

The ICO's advice if he is not sure which a firm is: treat it as an individual (same page).

| Channel | Limited company or LLP | Sole trader or ordinary partnership |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Live phone call | Allowed without consent if the number is not on TPS or CTPS | Same |
| (b) Email | Allowed without consent. Must show who he is and give an opt-out address | Not allowed without prior consent |
| (c) Text and WhatsApp | Text allowed in law, but he cannot tell who owns a mobile. WhatsApp's own rules forbid it | Not allowed without prior consent |
| (d) Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn messages | Treat as not allowed | Not allowed without prior consent |
| (e) Website contact form | Not covered by any guidance I found. Treat like email | Treat like email: avoid |
| (f) Post | Allowed. PECR does not apply | Allowed. UK GDPR applies |

### (a) Live phone calls

- He must not call a number on the register (TPS or CTPS), or anyone who has told him not to call. Source: [PECR regulation 21(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/21) (primary).
- An unanswered dial counts. "A reference to making a call includes a reference to attempting to establish such a connection." Source: [PECR regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2) (primary).
- He must not withhold his number. He must either not prevent his number showing, or show a number he can be reached on. Source: [regulation 21(A1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/21) (primary). An ordinary mobile with the number showing meets this (inference from the wording). A paid VoIP line is not needed for the test.
- He must give his name on every call, and a contact address or a free-to-call number if asked. Source: [PECR regulation 24](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/24) (primary); [gov.uk direct marketing](https://www.gov.uk/marketing-advertising-law/direct-marketing) (primary).
- For business calls he must check both lists. "Some businesses (sole traders and some partnerships) register with the TPS." The ICO says: "before you make live B2B calls, you need to screen against both the CTPS and TPS registers", plus his own do-not-call list. Sources: [ICO telephone marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/) and [ICO business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) (primary).
- The 28-day rule: it is not a breach to call a number that has been on the register for less than 28 days. So a screening result is good for 28 days. Source: [regulation 21(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/21) (primary).
- Regulation 21 sets no calling hours (my reading of its text; inference). The plan's 7am calls are a question of manners, not of this law. Industry codes on calling hours: not checked.
- A sole trader's name and number are personal data, so UK GDPR also applies. See Q3.

### (b) Email

- The consent rule applies only to "individual subscribers". Source: [PECR regulation 22(1) and (2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22) (primary).
- The ICO: "You can email or text any corporate body." For sole traders and some partnerships, only "if they have specifically consented, or the 'soft opt-in' applies". The soft opt-in "does not apply to prospective customers or new contacts". Sources: [ICO electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) and the ICO business-to-business page above (primary).
- Every marketing email, to anyone, must not hide who sent it and must give a valid address for opting out. Source: [PECR regulation 23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/23) (primary).
- Second pass: regulation 23 also bans a marketing email that would break regulation 7 of the Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002. That rule says a commercial message must "be clearly identifiable as a commercial communication" and must "clearly identify the person on whose behalf the commercial communication is made". Source: [Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002, regulation 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/7) (primary). An email that says "I'm not selling anything" while it is the first step in selling does not look "clearly identifiable" as commercial (inference; no case found).
- If the address names a person (dave@firm.co.uk), UK GDPR applies as well. The right to object to marketing "is absolute" (ICO business-to-business page, primary).
- So the standing plan's wording, "pick one trade of UK limited companies" for the emails, is correct in law. It only works if each firm is checked on Companies House first.
- How much of the market this closes (second pass, newest figures): at the start of 2026, 55% of UK private businesses were sole proprietorships (3.1 million), 6% were ordinary partnerships (343,000) and 39% were companies (2.2 million). 74% had no employees. Source: [Business population estimates 2026, published 5 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2026/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2026-statistical-release) (primary). So about 6 in 10 businesses cannot be cold-emailed. The year before it was 57%, 6% and 37% ([2025 release](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release), primary).
- Construction has more small firms than any other sector: 846,000, which is 15% of all small and medium firms (2026 release, primary). The split between sole traders and companies for construction alone: not found in either pass. It is in the detailed tables, which I could not open.

### (c) SMS and WhatsApp

- "Electronic mail" in PECR "includes messages sent using a short message service". Source: [PECR regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2) (primary). So texts follow the email rule: no cold texts to sole traders.
- A tradesman's mobile may be on a personal contract even when the firm is a limited company. He cannot tell from outside (inference). Treat cold texts as off limits.
- WhatsApp is not named by the ICO. Its definition covers "any similar message that is stored electronically", so I read it as covered (inference).
- WhatsApp's own rules settle it anyway: "You may only contact people on WhatsApp if" they gave their number and "you have received opt-in permission". Source: [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy, updated 23 September 2026](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) (primary).

### (d) Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn messages

- The ICO says electronic mail includes "direct messages via social media". On professional networking sites, people are "unlikely to be on the sites exclusively in their business capacity", and "sending them direct marketing messages is not considered B2B marketing". Source: ICO business-to-business page (primary). In plain words: cold sales messages to a person's account need consent.
- LinkedIn's rules: "Do not use our invitation feature to send promotional messages to people you don't know." Source: [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies) (primary).
- A message to a limited company's Facebook business page: no ICO statement found. Meta's own rules: not checked.

### (e) The contact form on a firm's website

Not found in any ICO page I opened. A form usually sends a stored message to the firm, which fits the PECR definition of electronic mail (inference). Treat it like email: acceptable for a verified limited company, avoid for a sole trader.

### (f) Post

- "If you are intending to send marketing by post then PECR does not apply." Source: ICO business-to-business page (primary).
- UK GDPR still applies where the letter goes to a sole trader or a named person. He needs a lawful basis (see Q3) and must stop if asked.
- gov.uk also says to check the Mail Preference Service. Source: [gov.uk direct marketing](https://www.gov.uk/marketing-advertising-law/direct-marketing) (primary).
- Cost of a stamp today: not found (Royal Mail's site refused to open).

### What changed in 2025 and 2026

- **Fines.** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 replaced the PECR enforcement rules on 5 February 2026. Breaches of the marketing regulations (19 to 24) now carry the "higher maximum amount": £17.5 million, or for a business 4% of worldwide turnover if higher. The old cap was £500,000. Second pass: the Schedule's own note reads "Sch. 13 in force at 5.2.2026 by S.I. 2026/82". These are ceilings. The real fines listed below are far smaller (inference: no fine has yet been seen under the new ceiling). Sources: [the Act, section 115](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/115), [Schedule 13, paragraph 18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/schedule/13), [Data Protection Act 2018 section 157](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/157) (all primary); [Clifford Chance, February 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html) (independent, search extract only).
- The gov.uk business page still shows the old figure ("up to £500,000 for each unsolicited phonecall"). It is out of date.
- **Legitimate interests.** Since 5 February 2026, UK GDPR lists "processing that is necessary for the purposes of direct marketing" as an example of a possible legitimate interest. Source: [the Act, section 70](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/70) (primary). This helps a little with calls and post. It does not remove the PECR consent rule for email to sole traders.
- **The email rule itself did not change for businesses.** The only change to regulation 22 was a new soft opt-in for charities (regulation 22 page above, primary).
- **ICO guidance.** The main direct marketing guidance was updated on 28 April 2026 "to reflect the six month commencement schedule" of the Act. Source: [ICO direct marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/) (primary). The page that defines sugging shows "Last updated: 20 August 2025" (second pass). The ICO's pages on B2B marketing, telephone marketing, email marketing and the data protection fee each still carry this notice: "Due to changes made by the Data (Use and Access) Act, this guidance is under review and may be subject to change."
- **What fines look like in practice.** The 2026 ICO fines I found for calls to TPS numbers ran from £100,000 to £240,000, for 230,000 to 760,000 calls each (ICO news items; all but the example below were seen as search extracts only). The example cites the old penalty power, so these cases were decided under the old £500,000 cap (inference). Example: [Energy Prices Direct, £160,000, over 700,000 calls to TPS and CTPS numbers, 20 May 2026](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/05/glasgow-based-energy-company-fined-160-000-for-making-unsolicited-marketing-calls/) (primary). In 2023 the ICO fined two firms £180,000 for business-to-business calls, one of which could not prove it had screened ([ICO, May 2023](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2023/05/ico-fines-two-businesses-180-000-for-making-unlawful-marketing-calls), primary, search extract only). I found no ICO action over a few hundred calls. The law still applies at any volume.

## Q2. Is "I'm not selling anything" sugging?

Yes, as the plan is written.

- The ICO: "Contacting people to conduct genuine market research is not direct marketing." But "if the research is ultimately being carried out for you or others to send direct marketing to the people involved, then this is direct marketing. This is sometimes referred to as 'sugging' (selling under the guise of research)." Source: [ICO, Identify direct marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/identify-direct-marketing/) (primary).
- The same page lists "generating leads for advertising purposes (eg cold calling people)" as a direct marketing purpose. It adds: "The focus is on why you are doing something rather than the activity itself."
- The plan's gate says that 5 to 15 conversations means "sell the service". The people he spoke to are the obvious first prospects. So the test is lead generation. All the marketing rules in Q1 apply to the test itself, including the ban on cold email to sole traders.
- The Market Research Society's Code, rule 4: members "must never undertake any activities, under the guise of research, which aim to manipulate, mislead or coerce individuals", with the example "Sell or market under the guise of research ('sugging')". Source: [MRS Code of Conduct 2023](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/MRS-code-of-conduct-2023.pdf) (primary). The Code binds MRS members and partner companies only. The owner is not one, so it does not bind him. It shows what the research trade thinks of the practice.
- A second risk: "Advertising which is misleading is prohibited" when aimed at traders. Advertising means "any form of representation" made "in order to promote the supply" of a product. Source: Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008, [regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/2) and [regulation 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/3) (primary). Whether a "research" call that hides a sales aim falls under this is my inference. I found no case.
- A third risk, for email only (second pass): a marketing email must be "clearly identifiable as a commercial communication" (see Q1(b)). A sales-led email that says "I'm not selling anything" is hard to square with that (inference).

**There are two honest ways to run the test.**

1. Real research: never sell to anyone he approached in the test. Lawful, but it throws away his warmest contacts, and one follow-up sales call turns it into sugging after the fact. Not recommended.
2. Open marketing with a truthful script. Recommended. He says who he is and why he is asking, and asks permission to come back.

**Suggested phone wording (inference, built on the rules above):**

> "Hello, is that [name]? My name is [first name and surname]. I'm setting up a small service that writes up quotes for trade firms. Before I go further I'm asking contractors how they do it now. I'm not asking you to buy anything today. Have you got two minutes for one question?"
>
> Then the question: "How do you get a quote out at the moment, from the site visit to sending it?"
>
> To close: "Thank you. If I do launch this, are you happy for me to ring or email you about it? No problem if not."

Write down the answer, with the date. A "yes" lets him call that person again even if the number later goes on the TPS (regulation 21(4)). A "yes" that clearly covers email is the consent he needs to email a sole trader (my reading of the ICO's "specifically consented"; inference). A "no" goes on his do-not-contact list.

**Suggested email wording (limited companies only):**

> "Hi [name]. I'm [full name], based in [town]. I'm setting up a small service that writes up quotes for trade firms. Before I build it I'm asking contractors one question: how do you get a quote out at the moment, and what is the slowest part? I'm not asking you to buy anything. A one-line reply would help, and I'll send you a short summary of what I learn. I found your details on your website. If you'd rather I didn't contact you again, reply 'no thanks' and I won't. [Full name, contact address, phone number]"

This names him, states the commercial reason, says where he got the details, and gives the opt-out that regulation 23 requires.

## Q3. Practicalities for one person

**Screening 200 to 500 numbers.**

- He uploads his list of numbers to a licensed checking service and gets back the ones he must not call.
- One reseller, TPS Services Ltd, publishes one price list for "TPS, CTPS and FPS Screening": up to 250 numbers £5.00 plus VAT (£6.00); 500 numbers £7.00 plus VAT (£8.40); 1,000 numbers £13.00 plus VAT (£15.60). It also advertises "250 FREE credits" for a new account. Source: [TPS Services pricing page](https://www.tpsservices.co.uk/tps-services-for-businesses/tps-services-pricing.aspx) (primary for its own prices; it is a seller).
- Second pass: paying for one list at a time has a minimum charge of £4.25 plus VAT (£5.10) for 250 numbers or fewer (same page). So expect to pay about £5 to £9 in all, or nothing if the free credits are honoured.
- The page does not say plainly that one check covers both TPS and CTPS. Confirm before paying.
- The official TPS licence price: not found.
- Screen no more than 28 days before the last call. If the test runs over, screen again.

**Are mobiles covered?** Yes. The ICO tells the public they can "register your landline or mobile number with the Telephone Preference Service" free of charge. Source: [ICO nuisance calls](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/nuisance-calls/) (primary). A tradesman's mobile can be on the list. The seller above says the TPS holds "around 23 million registered numbers" (vendor_or_seo). How many belong to tradespeople: not found.

**The ICO data protection fee.**

- "Most companies will only need to pay £52 or £78 a year", with £5 off for Direct Debit. Sole traders are included. Source: [ICO, data protection fee: what you need to do](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-fee-what-you-need-to-do/) (primary). A one-person business is in the lowest band (inference; the page does not define the bands).
- The law: a data controller must pay "unless all of the processing of personal data they undertake is exempt processing". Exempt purposes include "advertising, marketing and public relations in respect of the data controller's business", keeping accounts and records, and staff administration. Sources: [Data Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018, regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/2) and [Schedule, paragraph 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/schedule) (primary).
- My reading (inference): during the test he probably does not have to pay, because a prospect list used to market his own business is exempt. Once he writes quotes that contain his clients' customers' names and addresses, the answer is less clear.
- What to do: run the ICO's free checker at ico.org.uk/fee-checker before the first call. If the answer is unclear, pay. £47 to £52 is cheap next to the doubt. The ICO says: "If you need to pay and don't pay, you could be fined."

**UK GDPR basis and records.**

- For calls and post the ICO says "in many cases it is likely that legitimate interests will be the appropriate lawful basis" (business-to-business page, primary).
- "Legitimate interests is made up of a three-part test (known as a legitimate interests assessment or LIA)." Source: [ICO, Plan direct marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/plan-direct-marketing/) (primary). "You should record your LIA and the outcome" and "keep an audit trail". Source: [ICO, legitimate interests in practice](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/how-do-we-apply-legitimate-interests-in-practice/) (primary).
- When details come from somewhere other than the person: "You must give people your privacy information within a reasonable period and at the latest within a month", or at first contact if sooner. Source: [ICO, Collect information and generate leads](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/collect-information-and-generate-leads/) (primary).

Records to keep (my list, built from the ICO pages above; inference):

1. One page: why he is contacting these firms, why he needs their details, and why that is fair to them. This is the LIA.
2. For each firm: where and when he found the details, and whether it is a company or a sole trader.
3. Proof of screening: date, service used, the file sent and the file returned, the receipt.
4. A log of every call and email: date, time, result.
5. A do-not-contact list, checked before every session.
6. Every "yes, get back to me": who, when, and whether for calls, email or both.
7. A five-line privacy note he can read out or send: who he is, what he holds, where he got it, why, how long he keeps it, and that they can tell him to stop or delete at any time.

## Q4. Realistic response

**No measured response rate was found for UK tradespeople, in either pass.** The benchmark figures below come from sales-software firms measuring professional sales teams, mostly in the US. Treat them as rough.

Phone:

- 9.9% of dials are answered by a person. 24.5% of prospects are reached at least once after about three tries. 58% of answered calls get past the greeting. UK dials were answered 14.7% of the time against 9.0% in the US, on a small UK sample. Source: [Belkins, 175,000 dials in 2025](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks) (vendor_or_seo).
- Average cold call length is 82 seconds. Best hours are 10 to 11am and 2 to 3pm. Before 9am, after 4pm and lunchtime are weak. Source: [Cognism cold calling report 2026](https://www.cognism.com/reports/cold-calling-report-2026) (vendor_or_seo). Belkins found the opposite for 6pm, its best hour. Neither studied trades.
- The P3 report's claim that contractors answer at 7am, lunch, 6pm and Saturday morning: no evidence found for or against. The test will show it if he logs the time of each call.
- Second pass, UK trades, direction only. A Fix Radio survey of over 220 UK tradespeople found 60% "regularly struggle to take calls while on site" and 34% had lost work because they did not answer. The survey date is not given and it is quoted by a firm that sells call answering. Source: [Callout AI blog, 24 May 2026](https://callout-ai.pages.dev/blog/cost-of-missed-calls) (vendor_or_seo). An Everreach survey of over 250 UK trade firms found 72% worry about losing work if they cannot answer the phone. Source: [Arthur Online, 22 April 2024](https://www.arthuronline.co.uk/blog/property-news/contractors-make-missed-calls-and-missed-opportunities-a-thing-of-the-past) (vendor_or_seo).
- What those two surveys mean (inference): tradespeople miss many calls during site hours, so calls outside site hours and a second try matter. They also have a reason to pick up an unknown number, because it may be a customer. Neither survey measures how many cold calls get answered.

Email:

- Counted per email sent: 0.45% reply on average, 0.72% for firms of 0 to 10 staff, about 0.6% for construction. For the UK: 0.48%, from over 370,000 emails (second pass). Source: [Belkins, 7.5 million emails in 2025](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates) (vendor_or_seo).
- Counted per campaign with follow-ups: 3.43% ([Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)) and 4.5% ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)) (vendor_or_seo, search extracts only).

First-hand accounts about trades (second pass; none is a UK measurement):

- Over 800 cold emails to plumbers offering a free trial of a phone-answering tool: "400+ opens" and "0 replies". Country not stated. Source: [Substack post, 14 July 2025](https://itteba.substack.com/p/i-emailed-800-plumbers-and-got-0) (independent; the author sells the tool).
- A US buyer of home service firms: one week of phoning for 1 to 3 hours a day gave 12 leads. 200 cold emails gave 3 responses. Source: [Searchfunder post, about three years old](https://www.searchfunder.com/post/i-started-marketing-to-home-service-companies-last-week-and-got-14-leads) (independent, US, old). He was offering to buy their firms, which is a more welcome call than most.
- A UK forum thread on marketing to tradespeople (2023, opinions, no numbers): "all the tradesmen I know are to busy to be bothered with social media". One poster's plumber joined LinkedIn, was flooded with sales pitches and left. One poster wrote that "emails sent via bought lists go straight to the bin". Another disagreed: "Email or SMS is the best way to get the attention of a plumber." Nobody in the thread discussed phoning. Source: [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/goto/post?id=3206729) (independent).
- The two accounts with numbers point the same way: cold email to trades gets little or nothing, and the phone does better. The forum thread is opinion and is split on email.

Other first-hand accounts (first pass; none UK, none about trades):

- 212 cold emails to lawn care firms from Brisbane: 47% opened, 6.4% clicked, replies were "thin", one sign-up. Source: [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-212-cold-emails-to-lawn-care-businesses-heres-what-actually-worked-e69b5309a6) (independent, search extract only, Australia).
- 200 cold LinkedIn messages asking for research calls: response "abysmal", "zero calls booked". Source: [DEV post, November 2025](https://dev.to/meysam81/i-sent-200-cold-messages-and-got-zero-calls-my-customer-discovery-reality-check-3m7p) (independent).
- 50 emails to small service firms: 16 replies. The author sells a kit on the subject and nothing is verified. Source: [DEV post](https://dev.to/henryknight_dev/i-cold-emailed-50-local-businesses-about-ai-chatbots-heres-what-happened-4c64) (vendor_or_seo).
- Response to "research only" requests to small firms: no measured rate found.

**My estimate from 200 approaches (inference on the figures above):**

| How the 200 are approached | People who respond | Real conversations |
|---|---|---|
| Email only, limited companies, with one follow-up | 0 to 9 replies | 0 to 5 |
| Phone, one dial each | 20 to 30 pick up | 5 to 15 |
| Phone, up to three tries each | 30 to 70 pick up | 8 to 25 |

I assumed a quarter to a half of people who pick up, and about half of those who reply by email, give a real answer. That assumption is mine and unmeasured.

**Is the gate sensible?**

- "Under 5 = stop" is fair for a phone-led test, but only just. With one dial each, my range starts at 5, so a full effort could still land under the line by bad luck. With a second try at the unanswered numbers the range starts at about 8. That is why the second try belongs in the test.
- It is not fair for the test as section 3 words it (20 emails a day, phoning only "the ones you can reach"). An email-led test can score under 5 with perfect effort. The plan would then blame the owner for a weak channel.
- "Over 15 = we were too pessimistic" is set too low if he phones all 200 with redials. That result would be ordinary.
- The gate does not say what counts as a conversation. It should: the contractor gave a real answer to the quoting question, in about two minutes or more on the phone or in a written reply.
- Time check (inference): at the P3 report's 20 dials an hour, 200 first dials is 10 hours, a second try at the unanswered ones is about 9 more, and building the list by hand is 10 to 13 hours. That is about 30 hours in 14 days. It is tight for evenings and weekends. Three tries each will not fit.

## Q5. Where the list can come from

| Source | Cost | Shows company or sole trader? | Terms on copying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Companies House | Free | Every entry is a company or LLP. Sole traders are not on it | Free monthly data file and a search by SIC code, status and address. No licence limits stated on the pages I opened |
| Google Maps | Free | No | Bans copying the content and mass downloads |
| Which? Trusted Traders | Free to view | Not verified | Personal use only. No commercial use |
| TrustMark | Free to view | Not verified | Any copying or other use needs written permission, except personal, non-commercial use |
| Checkatrade | Free to view | Not verified | Could not open the terms |

Details:

- **Companies House.** The free data product is "basic company data of live companies on the register", "provided free of charge", updated monthly. Source: [Companies House data download](https://download.companieshouse.gov.uk/en_output.html) (primary). The [advanced search](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/advanced-search) filters by "Nature of business" (SIC code), status, company type and registered office address (primary). SIC codes 41, 42 and 43 cover building, civil engineering and specialist trades such as electrics, plumbing and roofing (from the standard code list; not re-checked today). I found no phone number or email field. The registered office is often an accountant's address, so a letter there may not reach the owner (inference).
- **Google Maps.** Terms dated 27 January 2026 say users may not "copy the content" or "mass download or create bulk feeds of the content". Source: [Google Maps additional terms](https://www.google.com/intl/en_uk/help/terms_maps/) (primary). Looking up one firm's number in order to ring it is ordinary use (inference). A scraping tool is not. Second pass: the same terms ban using Google Maps "to create or augment any other mapping-related dataset (including a mapping or navigation dataset, business listings database, mailing list, or telemarketing list) for use in a service that is a substitute for, or a substantially similar service to, Google Maps". That clause is aimed at people building a rival directory. The plain ban on copying and mass downloading is the one that bites here.
- **Which? Trusted Traders.** "You must not use or encourage others to use our website (or its contents) for their or your commercial use." Source: [Which? Trusted Traders terms](https://trustedtraders.which.co.uk/terms-and-conditions/) (primary). Building a sales list from it breaks those terms.
- **TrustMark.** "TrustMark owns all copyright, database rights and all similar rights in this site." Use beyond printing or downloading extracts "for personal, non-commercial use" needs "prior written permission". Source: [TrustMark terms](https://www.trustmark.org.uk/about/terms-and-conditions) (primary).
- **Checkatrade.** Its terms pages refused to open in both passes. A search shows it has "Platform Terms of Use" (effective 28 October 2026) and an "Acceptable Use Policy", but the search did not show any clause on copying (search summary only). Tools that scrape it are sold openly, and one tells buyers they are "responsible for complying with Checkatrade's Terms of Service". Source: [Apify listing](https://apify.com/studio-amba/checkatrade-scraper) (vendor_or_seo). Do not treat it as a list source until the terms are read.
- **Any list copied from someone else** needs checks. The ICO: "It is not enough to simply accept a third party's assurances." Source: ICO, Collect information and generate leads (primary).

**How to tell a company from a sole trader (second pass).** Every company must show "the company's registered number" and "the address of the company's registered office" on its websites, business letters and order forms. Source: [Company, Limited Liability Partnership and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015, regulation 25](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17/regulation/25) (primary). So a trade firm's website with a company number at the bottom is a company. A website with no number is probably a sole trader or partnership, though some small companies forget the rule (inference). Confirm either way with a free search on Companies House. A company can trade under a different name from its registered one, so search by the number where there is one. If the firm cannot be found, treat it as a sole trader: phone only.

**The clean route (inference):** search Companies House by SIC code and county to get limited companies. Look up each firm's own website for its phone number and email. Note the source. Screen the numbers. Email and phone those firms. For sole traders, take the number from the firm's own website or advert, screen it, and phone only.

- Any measured response rate for "research only" requests to small firms.

## Verdict

**LAWFUL_WITH_CHANGES**

Decisive reason: phoning screened numbers is open to one person for both sole traders and companies, and on rough evidence should give enough conversations to pass the gate. But the script's "I'm not selling anything" line is sugging under the ICO's own definition, and the plan does not yet keep cold email away from sole traders.

The exact changes:

1. **Script.** Remove "I'm not selling anything and I'll send you what I learn". Use the wording in Q2: his full name, that he is setting up a quote-writing service, that he is not asking them to buy today, the one question, then a request for permission to get back in touch. Record every yes and no.
2. **Treat the test as marketing from day one.** Screen every number against both TPS and CTPS before the first dial. Call within 28 days of the screen. Keep the proof. Do not withhold his number. Give his name on every call and have a contact address ready if asked.
3. **Email only firms confirmed on Companies House as a limited company or LLP.** A company number on the firm's own website is the quick sign. Never cold-email a sole trader or ordinary partnership. Every email says plainly that he is setting up a paid service, and carries his real name, a contact address, where he found them, and a one-line opt-out.
4. **No cold texts, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram or LinkedIn messages.** No contact forms on sole traders' sites.
5. **Make the phone the main channel and restate the gate.** Count 200 firms approached, at least 150 of them by phone with up to two tries. Under 5 conversations means stop only if those calls were made. An email-only result under 5 says nothing about him. Read "over 25", not "over 15", as the surprisingly good result. Define a conversation as a real answer to the question. If the hours do not fit into 14 days, run the test over 21 days. Do not cut the calls.
6. **Build the list by hand** from Companies House and each firm's own website. Do not copy or scrape Which? Trusted Traders, TrustMark, Checkatrade or Google Maps.
7. **Keep the seven records in Q3.** Run the ICO fee checker before the first call and pay the fee if the answer is unclear.
8. **Budget.** Add £0 to £8.40 for screening and £47 to £52 if the fee is due. The test costs about £35 to £95, not a flat £35.

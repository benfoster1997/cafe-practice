# Renters' Rights Act 2025 — Regulatory Ground Truth as of 17 August 2026 (Timing Dimension)

**Verification caveat up front:** the network proxy blocked gov.uk, assets.publishing.service.gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, property118.com, landlordzone.co.uk, theindependentlandlord.com and hoganlovells.com, so I could not read the Act text or the official roadmap directly. Everything below is triangulated from multiple independent secondary sources (law firms, Shelter, NRLA-adjacent, trade press) that consistently cite the official documents — the official roadmap is at gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap and the government "Information Sheet 2026" at gov.uk/government/publications/the-renters-rights-act-information-sheet-2026. Treat exact penalty figures and portal features as high-confidence-but-unverified-against-primary-text.

## 1. Commencement: what is in force NOW

- **Phase 1 commenced 1 May 2026**: Section 21 "no-fault" evictions abolished; all assured shorthold tenancies converted to open-ended assured monthly periodic tenancies; possession now runs exclusively through Section 8 grounds ([Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/private_renting/renters_rights_act_changes_for_private_renters), [RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/renters-rights-act-implementation-roadmap.html), [OTS Solicitors](https://www.otssolicitors.co.uk/news/renters-rights-act-section-21-section-8-notices-before-may-2026/)).
- **Transitional S21 window is CLOSED**: landlords had until 31 July 2026 to issue court proceedings on pre-1-May S21 notices ([Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/private_renting/renters_rights_act_changes_for_private_renters)). So as of today every English private tenancy is periodic and S8-only — peak landlord disorientation is happening right now.
- **Phase 2 (from late 2026)**: PRS Database regional rollout begins; Landlord Ombudsman development starts late 2026 but is **not expected open until 2028** ([RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/renters-rights-act-implementation-roadmap.html), [Charles Russell Speechlys](https://www.charlesrussellspeechlys.com/en/insights/quick-reads/102luu7-renters-rights-act-2025-what-is-the-governments-roadmap-for-implementation/)).
- **Phase 3 (consultations pending)**: Awaab's Law extension to PRS and a modernised Decent Homes Standard — DHS enforcement floated for **2035 or 2037** ([RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/renters-rights-act-implementation-roadmap.html)).

## 2. PRS Database: current official timeline and what it will do

- **No confirmed go-live date — "from late 2026", regional first.** The GOV.UK service "Register your rental property" rolls out **region by region starting late 2026, widening through 2027** as it beds in; agent/portal duty to show registration numbers on adverts applies area-by-area once live there ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/understanding-the-private-rented-sector-database), [The Independent Landlord](https://theindependentlandlord.com/prs-database/), [landlordknowledge.co.uk](https://landlordknowledge.co.uk/renters-rights-act-phase-two-late-2026/)). As of July–August 2026 **no delay has been announced**, but equally no region order or hard date has been published — everything hangs on secondary legislation still to be made.
- **Beta testing is real and recent**: the government tested the service with real landlords in mid-2026, issuing Landlord Registration Numbers and Property Registration Numbers, and tweaked the service after testing ([Property118](https://www.property118.com/the-prs-database-has-been-tested-on-real-landlords-what-happens-next-will-separate-the-professionals-from-everyone-else/), [LandlordZone](https://www.landlordzone.co.uk/news/government-tweaks-prs-database-after-landlord-testing), [NRLA tester call](https://www.nrla.org.uk/news/landlords-needed-test-new-database)).
- **What landlords must record**: contact details (incl. joint landlords), each property's address/type/bedrooms/occupancy, and **core safety records — gas safety certificate, EICR and EPC — plus HMO licence details** ([roadmap via RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/renters-rights-act-implementation-roadmap.html), [mydeposits](https://www.mydeposits.co.uk/content-hub/renters-rights-act-the-new-private-rented-sector-database/)). **This is the product-shrink finding: the free mandatory government portal will itself hold the certificate records the proposed register tracks.** Whether the government portal proactively sends expiry reminders is NOT confirmed in any source I could verify — vendor copy ("expiry alerts", "Smart Document Capture") kept bleeding into search extracts and must not be mistaken for portal features — but once government holds cert dates, adding reminders is trivial for them.
- **Fees**: annual, **per property**, cost-recovery basis; amount unconfirmed; consultation chatter suggests £10–£50/property/year ([tenancypilot](https://tenancypilot.co.uk/guides/private-rented-sector-database-landlord-registration-2026/), [mydeposits](https://www.mydeposits.co.uk/content-hub/renters-rights-act-the-new-private-rented-sector-database/)). Landlords will therefore already be paying a recurring government fee per property — fee fatigue works against a £49–99/mo private add-on.
- **Agent/API route**: government has **confirmed agents can submit database information on landlords' behalf** ([Property118](https://www.property118.com/government-confirms-agents-can-submit-database-information-on-behalf-of-landlords/)); bulk upload/API for software is discussed but not officially confirmed ([The Independent Landlord](https://theindependentlandlord.com/prs-database/)). Mainstream agent platforms (Goodlord, Reapit, Arthur) are already planning integrations — meaning agent-managed portfolios get this handled inside software they already pay for.
- **Access**: tenants/prospective tenants can check registration status and some standards info; councils get comprehensive cross-referencing against licensing records ([tenancypilot](https://tenancypilot.co.uk/guides/private-rented-sector-database-landlord-registration-2026/)).

## 3. Penalties (consistent across sources; Act text unverified due to proxy blocks)

- Letting an unregistered property: civil penalty **up to £7,000**, rising to **up to £40,000 or criminal prosecution for serious/repeat breaches** ([rentalize](https://rentalize.com/prs-database-registration-guide/), [tenancypilot](https://tenancypilot.co.uk/guides/private-rented-sector-database-landlord-registration-2026/), multiple concurring). This matches the RRA's general two-tier civil penalty structure.
- **Possession consequence**: an unregistered landlord generally **cannot obtain a possession order** under Section 8, except on antisocial-behaviour grounds (7A/14) ([tenancypilot](https://tenancypilot.co.uk/guides/private-rented-sector-database-landlord-registration-2026/)). Since S21 is gone, this makes registration existential for landlords — a genuine fear-driven sales hook, but one the government's own comms will also hammer.

## 4. Adjacent recurring duties the register would track

- **Gas safety**: annual CP12, unchanged for 2026-27 ([LetSorted](https://letsorted.co.uk/landlord-checklist/gas-safety-certificate-landlord-obligations-2026)).
- **EICR**: 5-yearly, unchanged ([Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/professional-standards/consumer-guides/landlords/smoke-alarm-and-safety-checks.html)).
- **EPC/MEES — the big confirmed change**: government response published **21 January 2026** under the Warm Homes Plan: **EPC C required for ALL private lettings (new and existing) from a single date, 1 October 2030** (the old 2028/2030 phasing dropped); new HEM-based dual-metric EPCs launch **October 2026**, legacy ratings discontinued October 2029; cost cap raised to **£15,000/property** with an exemptions register ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/03/epc-c-by-2030-what-real-estate-investors-need-to-know-about-the-uks-new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards), [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030), [CLA](https://www.cla.org.uk/news/government-announces-plan-for-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector-prs-mees/)). Current minimum remains E until then. This is a real multi-year planning duty a register could own.
- **Smoke/CO alarms**: 2022 regulations regime stable — alarm per storey, CO alarm per fixed-combustion-appliance room, repair on report ([lettingaproperty.com](https://www.lettingaproperty.com/landlord/blog/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-regulations-for-landlords/)).
- **Deposits**: 30-day protection + prescribed information, 1–3x penalties, unchanged by RRA ([landlordknowledge](https://landlordknowledge.co.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection-rules-schemes-penalties-2026-guide/)).
- **Awaab's Law**: in force for social housing since 27 October 2025; **does NOT apply to private landlords as of mid-2026**; PRS extension is Phase 3, subject to a future consultation, earliest realistic commencement 2027, no confirmed date ([awaabslawguide.co.uk](https://awaabslawguide.co.uk/blog/awaabs-law-private-landlords), [Kenwood](https://www.kenwoodplc.co.uk/blog/awaabs-law-and-the-private-sector-what-private-landlords-need-to-know-in-2026/)). Do NOT market Awaab's Law compliance to private landlords today — it would be a false claim.

## 5. Addressable market (portfolio landlords)

English Private Landlord Survey 2024: **45% of landlords own one property (21% of tenancies); 83% own 1–4 (51% of tenancies); 17% own 5+ properties, controlling 49% of tenancies** ([EMPO summary](https://empo.co.uk/english-private-landlord-survey-2024/), [OpenRent summary](https://blog.openrent.co.uk/english-private-landlord-survey-summary/)). Against the commonly cited ~2.3m landlords in England (EPLS sampling frame is the ~1.28m TDP-registered landlord records, so scale is fuzzy), that's roughly **220k–390k landlords with 5+ properties**. A "4+" cut is not published. Market size is emphatically not the binding constraint for 24–48 customers — reachability and willingness to pay are.

## 6. Timing risk assessment — the pessimist's case

**The bind: the same event that creates the demand spike delivers the free substitute.** The database's arrival (late 2026 regionally, through 2027 nationally) is what makes landlords anxious enough to pay — and it is a mandatory government system that will hold their gas/EICR/EPC records per property. The prior-research constraint "no free government tool covering the same job" is not violated today but is on a **6–18 month countdown**, region by region.

**If the database slips to mid-2027**: certificate-tracking alone does not carry the product at £49–99/month. Gas/EICR/alarm/deposit duties are decades old and have never sustained a standalone paid reminder register — compliance reminders are a free/cheap bundled feature of existing landlord apps and NRLA membership (~£100/YEAR, not month), and agent-managed portfolios (most 5+ landlords use agents for at least some properties) get it from Goodlord-class software. A slip extends the anxiety window slightly but removes the only novel deadline, and the sources show the content/SEO space is already carpet-bombed by 2025-26 micro-SaaS entrants built on exactly this thesis: CertNudge, LLCR, LetCompliance, LandlordOS, August, Remindlo, PRS Shield, Rentalize, TenancyPilot, SelfLandlord, LetSorted, Locastica and more surfaced in almost every search. First-mover on the "RRA compliance register" keyword set was gone a year ago.

**What survives the database**: multi-duty deadline orchestration the portal won't do (deposit clocks, licensing renewals, alarm logs, EPC-2030 retrofit planning per property, future Awaab's response-time tracking), and agent-side workflow. A product scoped as "the government database holds your certificates; we make sure you never breach anything across all duties" has a defensible post-2027 story; a product scoped as "your compliance certificate register" does not.

**Marketing-claim precision required today**: no confirmed database date, fee, or region order exists — any copy stating "register by [date] or face £7,000" is currently unverifiable and will need weekly maintenance as SIs land. S21-abolition and EPC-C-2030 claims ARE safe to make now.

VERDICT: CAUTION — the timing bind is structural: the event creating the demand spike (PRS Database regional rollout from late 2026, completing through 2027) is itself a free, mandatory government system that will hold landlords' gas/EICR/EPC records, so a certificate-register product has a genuine but closing 6-18 month window and must be scoped from day one around what the database won't do (multi-duty deadline orchestration, EPC-C-2030 retrofit planning, deposit/licensing clocks) rather than certificate storage — and if the database slips to mid-2027, certificate-tracking alone does not sell at £49-99/month against free bundled reminders in NRLA membership and existing landlord apps.

GAPS: Primary sources were proxy-blocked (gov.uk, assets.publishing.service.gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, property118.com, landlordzone.co.uk, theindependentlandlord.com, hoganlovells.com), so the Act text, the official implementation roadmap, and the Information Sheet 2026 were verified only via consistent secondary reporting — the £7,000/£40,000 figures and the S8 possession-bar exception (grounds 7A/14) should be checked against the Act before any marketing copy uses them. Unresolved facts: whether the government portal itself sends certificate-expiry reminders (vendor marketing copy contaminated search extracts; unconfirmed either way — decisive for how much of the product survives); the exact database fee and charging basis (secondary legislation pending); the regional rollout order and first pilot regions; whether an official API/bulk-upload route for software vendors will exist; the '4+ properties' landlord count (EPLS only publishes a 5+ cut, and its sampling frame of ~1.28m deposit-registered landlords vs the cited ~2.3m total makes absolute numbers fuzzy).

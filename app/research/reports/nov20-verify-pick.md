# Fact-check of the two reports on the quote-writing pick (20 November 2026)

Scope: this is a check, not a search for ideas. I took the claims that the two reports lean on most and tried to prove them wrong, using my own searches and by opening the pages myself. The two reports are `nov20-pick-supply.md` (verdict PICK_FAILS) and `nov20-pick-demand.md` (verdict DEMAND_WEAK_OR_UNPROVEN).

Source labels: **primary** (official statistics, a seller's own page or price list, an app store listing), **independent** (trade body survey, press, forum posts by real users), **vendor_or_seo** (marketing material, or a survey paid for by someone selling something: direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).

## Bottom line

1. Both verdicts stand. Neither report invented anything that matters. The official counts and the trade body survey check out exactly.
2. Both reports are firmer in places than their evidence. I found six errors or overstatements. None of them reverses a verdict. They are listed below.
3. The single most important finding of this check: **nobody in either report is a confirmed working tradesperson.** The forum thread that both reports call "first-hand" is mostly app sellers. I opened it. No poster clearly says they work in a trade.
4. So the honest position is this. The reason the quote-writing service was chosen has gone. Whether any contractor would still pay for it is not known. Desk research has now been tried twice and cannot answer it.

## Errors and overstatements I found

1. **ServiceM8 prices are wrong in the supply report.** It gives "29, 79, 149 and 349" with the currency unclear. The UK page shows £0, £25, £59, £119 and £269 a month. [ServiceM8 UK](https://www.servicem8.com/uk/) (primary). The direction is unchanged.
2. **ServiceM8 and Fergus are overstated as rivals.** See row 2. ServiceM8's tool needs a history of 100 jobs. Fergus's own notes do not show voice to quote.
3. **The Wickes line is stretched.** The supply report says Wickes tells trades to use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini to draft from "a brief voice note or bullet points". The page says that about replies to customers, risk assessments and terms. It does not say it about quotes. [Wickes](https://www.wickes.co.uk/trade/ai-tools-for-tradespeople-summer-26) (vendor_or_seo).
4. **The top of the price range is the wrong app.** "Nothing to about £44 a month" uses Tradify's top plan, and Tradify does not do voice to quote. The confirmed voice-to-quote apps cost about £10 to £15 a month. So £395 is about 26 to 40 times their price (395 / 14.99 and 395 / 10), not 9 to 40 times (inference from the prices in row 1). This makes the supply finding stronger, not weaker.
5. **The two reports disagree with each other on two prices.** VoxTrade: £14.99 (supply) against "from £9.99" (demand). The UK App Store shows £14.99. Fergus: "pound price not found" (supply) against "from £39" (demand). The Fergus UK page shows from £39 and from £45. [Fergus UK pricing](https://fergus.com/uk/pricing/) (primary).
6. **The demand report quietly rebuilds the offer.** Its closing list suggests a new buyer (limited companies of 3 to 10 people sending 6 or more quotes a week) and a new price (£135 to £255). Both are its own reasoning. The price rests on the placeholder-looking site in row 9. Neither has any more proof than £395 has.

Smaller points. The Powered Now survey dates differ: the trade press says June 2026 and Powered Now's own page says April to May 2026. The demand report says the number of small apps "shows that many developers believe the need is there". This project's own kill ledger shows that app builders pile into almost any niche, so the number of apps is weak evidence of demand. A count of apps on sale also hides the ones that have died. VoxTrade's App Store page shows its last update as 10 April, and it has no ratings.

## Does each verdict follow from its evidence?

**Supply: PICK_FAILS.** Yes, in the narrow sense the report itself gives. The earlier strategy report set the rule that "the service must sell an outcome no shipped product currently sells". It answered the "apps exist" objection by saying those apps "are templates and you are writing". I checked both quotes in `strategy-P3-service-first.md`. They are there. The answer is now out of date: at least three UK-aimed apps write the quote from speech, and a third of small building firms say they already use AI for quotes, estimates or tenders. A general chatbot can also do the write-up for nothing (inference). The survey figure is the strongest point, because it is about actual use, not just apps on sale. The report is slightly too firm about the big-name apps and slightly too soft about how cheap the small ones are. The two cancel out.

**Demand: DEMAND_WEAK_OR_UNPROVEN.** Yes, but "unproven" is the accurate half. The first part of its decisive reason (pricing is the slow part) is not shown by any survey. The second part (the write-up is the only part the owner can do, and it is now cheap or free) is well supported and is enough by itself. Extra support the report did not have: in two surveys, only about one tradesperson in five pays an accountant or bookkeeper, and about one in six relies on a partner or spouse. One sole trader in ten has an unpaid family member doing admin. Sources: [Tradesman Saver study, April 2026](https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2026/04/many-tradespeople-unprepared-for-new-digital-tax-reporting-rules-study-finds/) (vendor_or_seo, sample size not stated); [Screwfix Voice of the Trade 2024](https://media.screwfix.com/is/content/ae235/PDF_Download/Voice_of_The_Trade_2024.pdf) (vendor_or_seo, search extract only, 2024). If most trades will not pay for help with their accounts, which they must file by law, paying £395 a month for help with quotes is a hard sell. That is inference, and both surveys are weak.

## The closing question

**Should the owner still be told that the quote-writing service is the choice?**

No. Not as a settled choice at £395 a month.

- He should be told plainly that the service was picked because no app sold the same result, and that this is no longer true.
- He should be told that nobody has found a single contractor, or a single business, buying or selling quote writing for a monthly fee.
- He should also be told that this is not proof it cannot work. Every piece of evidence here is from a desk. None of it is a contractor speaking.
- What stays the same: the first step. The 14-day conversation test sells nothing. It answers the question that has been open since August, which is whether he will contact strangers. Neither report weakens it.
- What changes: what comes after the gate. `STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3 already says "Pick the most-repeated chore you heard". The 19 November wording in `app/STATUS.md` narrowed that to "sell the service". Going back to the section 3 wording is not a new idea. It is the standing plan as first written. Quote writing becomes one thing the test might support, not the thing already decided.
- One honest warning. Whatever chore comes out on top must pass the same check that quote writing just failed: is an app already selling it for a few pounds? It is possible that the test ends with nothing left to sell. He should hear that before he starts.

**Should the single question stay about quoting, or be widened?**

Widen it, but keep it to one question in the first message.

- A quoting-only question can only test an idea whose main reason has gone. It would spend all 200 approaches on that.
- The evidence does not say quoting is the worst chore. The surveys bundle "quotes, invoices and chasing payments" together. The one survey that ranks them puts invoicing and chasing first. That survey is weak (row 6), which is another reason to ask rather than assume.
- The demand report suggests four questions. Four questions in a cold message to a stranger will get fewer answers than one. The "one question, no pitch" rule was there for a reason.
- So: one opening question that names the paperwork jobs and asks which one costs the most evening time and how they handle it now. Quoting is one of the named jobs. The follow-up questions from both reports are for people who reply or pick up the phone: what do you use for quotes now, have you tried an app or ChatGPT, does anyone help you with paperwork and do you pay them, how many quotes do you send a week.
- Change the gate wording to match. Five to 15 conversations shows he can reach people. It does not show they want anything. Before selling, at least three of them should name the same chore, say it is not already fixed by an app, by AI or by family, and say they would pay to be rid of it. That number is a judgment, not a measured figure.

## What I could not check

- Tradespal, Kwikbill, PlutoPrice, Zipflow and BuildScope. I did not open their pages.
- Whether any of the small apps has real users. No ratings, reviews or user counts were found for any of them.
- How good an AI-written trade quote is. No independent test was found by me or by either report.
- Jobber's UK price in pounds and whether its voice feature works in the UK.
- The Checkatrade pages. They refused to open (403), so those claims rest on search extracts.
- Any statement from a confirmed working UK tradesperson about quoting in 2026. This is the largest gap in all three reports, including this one.

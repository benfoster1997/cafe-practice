# Check of one claim: do "no-selling" products pay a newcomer? (20 November 2026)

Scope: on 19 November the owner was told, from general knowledge, that templates, e-books and similar products do not pay for a newcomer. This report checks that claim against evidence. It does not look for a product to make and proposes none.

## Bottom line

1. The claim holds. In every family with real data, the middle seller earns a small fraction of £500 a month. No family shows a typical newcomer reaching £500 a month within 12 months without selling or adverts.
2. The best figures: the middle subscription app makes about £55 a month a year after launch. The middle Gumroad seller made about £54 in a whole year. The middle Chrome extension has 10 users. Redbubble paid its average selling artist about £22 for a whole year.
3. A minority is paid in every family. The biggest minority is in phone apps: about one subscription app in six reaches $1,000 a month within two years. That sample includes funded teams that buy adverts.
4. Stronger AI in 2026 makes it worse for a newcomer, not better. Making things got cheap for everyone at once. New iPhone apps doubled in the first half of 2026 while downloads rose 2 percent.
5. Platforms are tightening, and none pays for Claude skills. Etsy banned AI-made patterns, recipes and guides in October 2026. Anthropic's new Claude Marketplace publishes no payout terms for makers.

## How to read the numbers

- "Median" means the middle seller. Half do better, half do worse. It is the fair picture of a typical person. An "average" (mean) is pulled up by a few big winners, so it flatters.
- Most sources are in US dollars. I convert at about 1.3 dollars to the pound, and about 2 Australian dollars to the pound. Both rates are my rough figures and were not looked up. On that rate the £500 a month bar is about $650.
- Source labels: **primary** (the platform's own data, terms or filings), **independent** (press, academic work, first-hand surveys), **vendor_or_seo** (marketing content, direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).
- Almost all the data is global or American. The only UK-only figure I found is for professional authors. These are global marketplaces, so a UK seller fishes in the same pool (inference).
- About this version: an earlier pass of this same check wrote this file earlier today, using 16 web searches. This version re-opened its main sources one by one and used 12 more searches on the gaps. Almost everything checked out. Three things changed and are marked below: the content-site traffic figures (corrected), the print-on-demand figures (replaced with newer ones read directly from the company's report), and the Claude marketplace finding (it does exist; it launched in September). One figure on the number of self-published books could not be confirmed and was removed. A few marketing-blog links that I say I do not rely on (customcy, listifyai, cartmango, insightraider, FreshLearn) were carried over from the earlier pass and not re-opened.

Why even these numbers flatter a newcomer:

- **Apps.** The sample is apps that wired up paid subscriptions. It includes funded teams that buy adverts. Apps that never charge are left out.
- **Gumroad.** People who sold nothing are left out of the median. The data is from 2020, before the AI flood. A 2026 site claims a "$72 a month median", but [its own data page](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026) says it does not measure that (vendor_or_seo). I do not rely on it.
- **Books.** The people who answer are committed authors on a book-marketing mailing list. Two other surveys give higher medians, and both cover only professionals. The Alliance of Independent Authors found a median of $12,749 a year, but only people who spend at least half their working time on writing and publishing, or make half their income from it, could answer ([ALLi](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/2025-author-income-survey/), independent trade body; figure from its previous survey, described in a search extract as 2022 income). The UK authors' survey found a median of about £7,000 a year in 2022 for authors whose main job is writing, down 38.2 percent in real terms since 2018 ([CREATe, University of Glasgow, for ALCS](https://zenodo.org/records/7373314/files/2022_13_ALCS.pdf), independent academic work, search extract only). Neither is a newcomer figure.
- **Etsy.** "Active seller" leaves out shops that are open but dormant. [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/etsy-lost-14-million-active-sellers-in-a-year) (independent) describes it as a seller with at least one sale in the last year. From memory, Etsy's own filings define it by fees charged, which I did not check today. Marketing blogs quote "medians" from £100 to £800 a month that contradict each other and give no method ([customcy](https://customcy.com/?p=2008), [listifyai](https://www.listifyai.net/blog/how-much-do-etsy-sellers-make-2026), vendor_or_seo). One tool seller claims about 65 percent of Etsy shops never make a single sale, with no method shown ([SellerToolsHQ](https://sellertoolshq.com/guides/etsy-success-rate/), vendor_or_seo). I do not rely on any of these.
- **Print-on-demand.** Artists who sold nothing are not counted. This row was corrected: the earlier version used a 2024 figure from a search extract that I could not re-open.
- **Stock images.** A stock-photo site quotes a 2026 survey of 312 photographers with a median of $18 a month across all platforms ([PropelRC](https://propelrc.com/?p=25058), vendor_or_seo). I could not find the survey itself, so treat it as unverified.
- **Shopify.** This is the one family where a "middle" figure clears the bar, so read it with care. 4,438 apps were listed. Only 2,113 publish enough to be measured, and only 1,675 had price data. So the "middle app" is the middle of the best-documented 38 percent (inference). The money sits in old apps: the oldest fifth earn roughly 100 times what the youngest fifth earn.
- **Prompt marketplaces.** One guide puts the median prompt price at $5.99 ([cartmango](https://cartmango.com/sell-ai-prompts/), vendor_or_seo). Blogs agree PromptBase keeps 20 percent of each sale. No seller-income data exists.

The share of sellers who earn nothing at all is not published by any platform I could reach.

## Q2. What are the rules on AI-made content in 2026, and are they enforced?

- **Etsy.** New rules were published on 22 October 2026. There is a "categorical ban on AI-generated functional guides, including patterns, recipes or instructions". "Bot-generated listings without human involvement are explicitly prohibited and will result in listing removal." Sellers "must still disclose when an item is created using AI". AI art is still allowed "when sellers play a clear creative role". Etsy warns sellers when it detects AI images at listing and plans to extend this to existing listings. Buyers can flag AI listings, but at first reports only inform Etsy and do not trigger removal. [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/10/26/etsy-ai-policy/amp/) (independent). Repeat offences can lead to "additional enforcement action, including shop removal". Bundles of AI prompts were already banned in 2024. A 7 November update says proactive enforcement is planned for early 2027, with advance notice. No removal numbers have been published. [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-bans-ai-patterns/) (independent, updated 7 November 2026). So the rule is real, and enforcement is mostly still to come. Etsy's own rules page was blocked.
- **Amazon KDP.** "We require you to inform us of AI-generated content (text, images, or translations)" when you publish. Work you wrote yourself and tidied with AI need not be declared. "We'll reject or remove content that does not adhere to these guidelines." KDP also bars "content that's typically disappointing to customers". [KDP content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672410) (primary, read today). In September 2023 Amazon capped new titles at three a day per account and said "the number may be adjusted if necessary" ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/), independent). Whether the cap is still three in 2026: not found. Claims of a sweep of account bans in April 2026 come only from tool sellers and a law firm's blog ([example](https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/blog/increase-in-kdp-account-terminations-over-ai-assisted-books-and-poor-customer-experience-concerns), vendor_or_seo). I do not rely on them.
- **Apple App Store.** No rule against AI-built apps. Apple says "all apps are held to the same high standards for quality, privacy and security" ([9to5Mac, July 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/), independent). Apple's own count for 2024: over 320,000 submissions rejected as spam, copies or misleading ([TechCrunch, April 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/), independent, quoting Apple). In March 2026 Apple removed three AI app-building tools (Replit, Vibecode, Anything) for breaking its rules ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/apple-app-store-experiences-surge-in-new-apps-amid-vibe-coding-boom-2000742653), independent).
- **Google Play.** No AI rule found. But a new personal account must first run a closed test with "a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before it can publish. This applies to "personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023" ([Google Play help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465), primary). A newcomer with no audience has to find 12 people before launch day.
- **Chrome Web Store.** No AI rule found. The store does not take payments for sellers at all. Google's notice says the "Chrome Web Store payments system is now deprecated", and since 1 February 2021 developers can no longer charge through it ([Google developer notice](https://developer.chrome.com/webstore/cws-payments-deprecation), primary, search extract only). The seller must set up their own billing.
- **WordPress plugin directory.** No AI-specific rule found. The directory does not sell anything. "Plugins may not contain functionality that is restricted or locked, only to be made available by payment or upgrade" ([WordPress plugin guidelines](https://developer.wordpress.org/plugins/wordpress-org/detailed-plugin-guidelines/), primary). Since September 2026 each release is also analysed "by several AI models" ([WordPress plugin team](https://make.wordpress.org/plugins/), primary). So money has to come from a paid version sold somewhere else, which means finding buyers yourself (inference).
- **Google Search (for content sites).** Google's spam policy lists "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" as "scaled content abuse". Sites that break the policy "may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), primary, read today).
- **Claude Marketplace.** "Every submission is validated and safety-scanned as soon as it is sent", and a person reviews new plugin listings. Anyone on a paid Claude plan can submit. Fee, revenue share and payouts are not published ([Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/?p=34638), independent, 28 September 2026). One reviewer concludes: "For individual developers, nothing changes yet" ([dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/claude-marketplace/), independent, 15 October 2026).
- **Notion.** No AI rule found. Sellers "need to join the waitlist, get approved by the Notion team", and "it may take a few months to get reviewed". Notion takes "a 8% fee plus 40 cents per transaction", plus 1 percent for sellers outside the US ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace), primary).
- **Adobe Stock.** AI images are accepted and must be marked as generative AI when submitted ([Adobe help](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/generative-ai-photo-submission-guidelines.html), primary, search extract only).
- **Shutterstock, Udemy, Skillshare, Canva, Gumroad, Payhip, Redbubble, Shopify.** Current AI rules: not found.

## Q3. What happened after AI content flooded in (2023 to 2026)?

The same thing in every family with data: far more things for sale, about the same number of buyers.

- **Apps.** About 600,000 new iPhone apps in 2025, up 30 percent. About 560,000 in the first half of 2026 alone, double the year before. Downloads rose 3 percent in 2025 and 2 percent in the first half of 2026 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/), independent, reporting Sensor Tower figures from the New York Times). New subscription apps went from about 2,000 a month in January 2022 to over 14,700 a month in January 2026. Apps launched in 2025 or later take 3 percent of subscription revenue ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/), primary). "While 19% of all new apps crossed the $1,000/month recurring revenue threshold in 2024, that figure slipped to 17% in 2025" ([9to5Mac, March 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/04/new-report-looks-into-the-sustainability-of-the-subscription-app-market-for-2026/), independent).
- **WordPress plugins.** About 700 new plugins a week were submitted in May 2026: "an increase of 2.7 times compared to 2025, and 5 times compared to 2024" ([WordPress plugin team](https://make.wordpress.org/plugins/), primary).
- **Stock images.** A Stanford and UCLA study of a large image marketplace found "78% more images per month than in markets without AI images" and "an additional 23% drop in non-AI artists". Total sales rose 39 percent, so buyers gained and sellers who did not use AI lost ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/when-ai-generated-art-enters-market-consumers-win-artists-lose), independent, an academic study, May 2025).
- **3D models.** On CGTrader about one upload in six was AI-made, but AI items took "just 2.6% of sales" and $1 in every $90 of revenue between June 2025 and May 2026. Only 5 percent of customers who tried an AI model found it worked well ([Fortune, August 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/20/ai-product-fatigue-online-marketplace-ecommerce/), independent). Buyers can tell, and they mark AI goods down.
- **Books.** Low-earning authors in a 2026 survey (582 replies) describe "Fast buck AI books flooding the market making discoverability almost impossible" and "AI slop clogging the market" ([Written Word Media 2026 mid-year survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2026-mid-year-indie-author-survey/), independent, self-selected). A count of new self-published titles: not found (see below).
- **Etsy.** Buyers are shrinking, not growing: active buyers down 5.0 percent, active sellers down 10.9 percent, sales down 2.4 percent year on year ([Etsy](https://investors.etsy.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/213/etsy-inc-reports-third-quarter-2025-results), primary, third quarter 2025). Etsy then banned the main AI-made digital goods (see Q2). Etsy has not said what share of listings are AI-made.
- **Print-on-demand.** Redbubble's payments to artists fell from A$30.2 million to A$20.9 million in one year, and selling artists fell from 542,000 to 468,000 ([Articore FY26 annual report](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260828/pdf/073d18n6j8zx42.pdf), primary). The same report warns that AI search tools "may reduce or change organic search-driven traffic" to its sites. In June 2026 Amazon began letting US shoppers describe a design to Alexa and have it printed on shirts, hoodies and bottles, "posing an expanded challenge to online merch platforms like Redbubble" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/), independent). The buyer no longer needs the seller's design.
- **Shopify apps.** 8.8 apps for every thousand shops, up from 5.5 in July 2023. "App numbers grow 28% a year while shop numbers grow 12%" ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-app-store-saturation/), vendor_or_seo).
- **Content sites (corrected).** Search visits fell 60 percent over two years for small publishers (1,000 to 10,000 page views a day), 47 percent for medium ones and 22 percent for large ones. The data is from Chartbeat, given to Axios, and leans towards news sites ([Search Engine Journal, March 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/search-referral-traffic-down-60-for-small-publishers-data-shows/569959), independent). The small sites lost the most. When Google shows an AI summary, 8 percent of users click a normal result, against 15 percent when it does not (Pew Research, July 2025, in [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/publishers/the-ai-search-reckoning-is-dismantling-open-web-traffic-and-publishers-may-never-recover), independent). An informal poll of 50 bloggers found four whose income was steady or growing ([Blog Herald](https://blogherald.com/interviews-commentary/i-asked-50-bloggers-if-theyre-still-making-money-in-2026-the-answers-were-brutal/), independent, low grade, search extract only).
- **Udemy.** The instructor's share of subscription income was cut from 25 percent in 2023 to 15 percent from January 2026 ([FreshLearn](https://freshlearn.com/blog/udemy-good-for-instructors/), vendor_or_seo; carried over from the earlier pass and not re-opened). This is a pay cut, not proven to be caused by AI.
- **One wider study.** Unpublished research by a Washington University professor found "about a 160% increase in apps by April 2026" against two years earlier, and that "the number of apps with more than 10 reviews dropped significantly". The article says "these results were not causal" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/20/ai-product-fatigue-online-marketplace-ecommerce/), independent).

## Q4. How long until a newcomer's first pound?

Mostly not found. No platform publishes time to first sale. What exists:

- **Apps.** The apps that do reach $1,000 a month take a median of 58 days to get there. That counts winners only. The middle app is still at about $72 a month after a full year ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/), primary). On Android, a new personal account must first hold 12 testers for 14 days ([Google Play help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465), primary).
- **Stock images.** First-year contributors are "statistically unlikely to cross $100 per month". Most people with one to four years in are under about $200 a month ([Xpiks](https://xpiksapp-website.pages.dev/blog/microstock-survey-2024-earnings-analysis), vendor_or_seo survey).
- **Books.** About 80 percent of authors with one to four books earn under $100 a month ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-author-survey/), independent).
- **Shopify apps.** The newest fifth of apps earn about $190 a month, modelled ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-app-store-saturation/), vendor_or_seo).
- **Udemy.** People who joined since 2020 average $2,100 a year, against $5,400 for earlier joiners ([SellCoursesOnline](https://sellcoursesonline.com/udemy-earnings-study), vendor_or_seo). Arriving late pays less.
- **Notion.** Approval from the waiting list "may take a few months". Money is held 14 days and paid only above a $20 balance ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace), primary).
- **Chrome extensions and WordPress plugins.** The store brings users, not money. Neither store collects payment for the seller (see Q2), so the first pound waits until the seller has built their own way to charge and found people willing to pay (inference).
- **Etsy.** Blogs say anything from one week to six months, with no data behind it ([Out and Beyond](https://outandbeyond.com/?p=17220), vendor_or_seo, search extract only). Measured figure: not found.
- **GPT Store and Claude Marketplace.** Never, as things stand: neither pays a UK newcomer (see Q1).
- **Gumroad, Payhip, Canva, print-on-demand, prompt marketplaces, content sites.** Not found.

## Q5. Is there any family where the typical newcomer reaches £500 a month within 12 months, with no selling and no adverts?

No. I found none.

- Where a real median exists, it is nowhere near: about £55 a month for a subscription app after a year, about £54 a year on Gumroad, 10 users for a Chrome extension, under about £77 a month for four in five authors with one to four books.
- Where only an average exists, the average is already below the bar, and the middle seller sits below the average: Etsy about £120 a month in gross sales, Redbubble about £22 a year, TeePublic about £54 a year, Udemy late joiners about £135 a month.
- Five figures look like exceptions and are not:
  - **Shopify's middle app at $2,500 to $5,200 a month.** A model, for the best-documented 38 percent of apps, with a stated error of up to double. The money sits in old apps. The newest fifth earn about $190 a month.
  - **Self-published authors' median of $12,749 a year, and UK authors' median of about £7,000 a year.** Only people who already write for at least half their working life could answer.
  - **More than 35 percent of surveyed stock contributors earn over $500 a month.** Two thirds of that sample had five or more years in. The same survey says first-year sellers are unlikely to pass $100.
  - **28 percent of surveyed authors earn over $1,000 a month.** A self-selected sample of committed authors. The high earners mostly have many books.
  - **17.3 percent of subscription apps reach $1,000 a month.** True of apps serious enough to wire up subscriptions, within two years, including teams that buy adverts. For a first-time maker with no audience and no adverts the share will be lower. How much lower: not found.
- None of the sources separates out "newcomer, no audience, no adverts". Every sample includes people with followings, back catalogues and advert budgets. So the real figure for the owner's situation is below every number in this report (inference).
- Three of the families do not even supply paying buyers. The Chrome store and the WordPress directory hand out free software and collect no money. The Claude and GPT marketplaces pay a UK newcomer nothing. In those, "no selling" is not on offer at all.

A minority does get paid in every family. The evidence says who they are: people with many products, several years in, an audience or an advert budget. That is the opposite of "passive, easy, quick".

## Q6. Do the much stronger AI models of 2026 make this better or worse for a newcomer?

Worse.

- Stronger models cut the cost of making things for everyone at once. The evidence shows a flood of sellers, not a rise in typical earnings. iPhone apps doubled while downloads rose 2 percent. WordPress plugin submissions are five times the 2024 level. One image marketplace got 78 percent more images each month than markets without AI.
- Newer entrants earn less. Apps launched since 2025 take 3 percent of subscription revenue. The share of new apps reaching $1,000 a month fell from 19 to 17 percent in a year. New Shopify apps earn about a hundredth of the oldest ones. Udemy's later joiners earn under half of the early ones.
- Buyers mark AI-made goods down where they can tell: about one upload in six but 2.6 percent of sales on CGTrader.
- Platforms respond by restricting AI goods or adding hurdles (Etsy, Amazon KDP, Google Search, Google Play's tester rule).
- The channels that used to bring free buyers are weakening. Small content sites lost 60 percent of their search visits in two years. Redbubble's owner warns its investors of the same risk.
- The buyer can now make the simple things without a seller. Amazon lets shoppers generate their own printed designs (reported). A person who wants a planner or a prompt can ask an AI model for one (my inference).

Two honest points the other way:

- A strong model lets a non-coder ship a working app at all, which was not true in 2023. That lowers the cost of trying. The evidence above says it does not raise the odds of being paid, because it lowered the cost for hundreds of thousands of other people too.
- In the stock-image study total sales rose 39 percent, and the gains went to sellers who used AI. That was the early phase on one marketplace. I found no evidence that the typical AI-using seller there earns anything close to the bar.

## What I could not find

- A published median income, or a share earning nothing, for Etsy sellers. Etsy does not publish one, and nothing splits out digital-download shops.
- Any earnings distribution for Canva, Payhip, Skillshare, Notion, prompt marketplaces, WordPress plugins, Chrome extension revenue (only user counts) or niche content sites (only traffic).
- A sound median for stock-image sellers. One quoted 2026 figure ($18 a month) could not be traced to its survey.
- Any figure for sellers earning exactly nothing, on any platform.
- Measured time to first sale for newcomers on any platform.
- Any dataset that isolates newcomers with no audience and no adverts.
- UK-only data, apart from the 2022 authors' survey (read as a search extract; the ALCS site itself was blocked on the earlier pass).
- Earnings for people who mass-produce books or templates with AI. No source measures them as a group.
- The number of self-published books per year. The earlier version of this file quoted a Bowker figure (about 3.5 million titles in 2025). The page cited for it does not contain that figure, so I removed it.
- Whether Amazon's three-titles-a-day cap still stands in 2026, and any Amazon figure on AI books or accounts removed.
- Any count of Etsy listings removed under the October 2026 rules.
- Current AI rules for Shutterstock, Udemy, Skillshare, Canva, Gumroad, Payhip, Redbubble and Shopify.
- A published fee, revenue share or payout for individuals on Anthropic's Claude Marketplace. Anthropic's own launch post says nothing on it.
- The 2025 results of the Alliance of Independent Authors income survey. It opened in September 2025 and I found no published results.
- Pages that would not open: Etsy's rules, Google's Chrome payments notice, the Articore 2024 report, Windows Report, and (on the earlier pass) eMarketer, Neowin, Class Central and ALCS.

## Verdict

**NO_TYPICAL_PAYOFF**

The decisive reason: in every family with real data, the middle seller earns a small fraction of £500 a month (about £55 a month for an app after a year, about £54 a year on Gumroad, about £22 a year on Redbubble, 10 users for a Chrome extension). No source shows a typical newcomer with no audience and no adverts reaching the bar in any family. The 2026 models made this worse by multiplying the number of sellers while the number of buyers stayed flat.

What the check changes: the statement made on 19 November was right, and it now rests on evidence instead of general knowledge. A minority is paid in each family, so "it never pays anyone" would be wrong. What the evidence supports is: "it does not pay the typical newcomer, and it is not quick or passive for the few it does pay". The standing recommendation does not need to change because of this check.

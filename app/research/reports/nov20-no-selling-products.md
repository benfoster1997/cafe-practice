# Check of one claim: do "no-selling" products pay a newcomer? (20 November 2026)

Scope: on 19 November the owner was told, from general knowledge, that templates, e-books and similar products do not pay for a newcomer. This report checks that claim against evidence. It does not look for a product to make and proposes none.

How to read the numbers:

- "Median" means the middle seller. Half do better, half do worse. It is the fair picture of a typical person. An "average" (mean) is pulled up by a few big winners, so it flatters.
- Most sources are in US dollars. I convert at about 1.3 dollars to the pound. That rate is my rough figure and was not looked up. On that rate, the £500 a month bar is about $650.
- Source labels: **primary** (the platform's own data, terms or filings), **independent** (press, academic work, first-hand surveys), **vendor_or_seo** (marketing content, direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).
- Almost all the data is global or American. I found no UK-only earnings data for any family. These are global marketplaces, so a UK seller fishes in the same pool (inference).
- 16 web searches were used. Several pages were blocked and are marked "search extract only".

## Bottom line

1. The claim holds. In every family where real data exists, the middle seller earns far below £500 a month. Where a true median exists it is about £5 to £60 a month.
2. The best figures: the middle subscription app makes about $72 (about £55) a month one year after launch. The middle Gumroad seller made $70 in a whole year. The middle Chrome extension has 10 users. About four in five authors with one to four books earn under $100 a month.
3. A minority does get paid. About one subscription app in six reaches $1,000 a month within two years. But that sample is serious and often funded teams, not a newcomer with no audience and no adverts.
4. Stronger AI in 2026 makes it worse for a newcomer, not better. Supply exploded and demand did not. New iPhone apps doubled in the first half of 2026 while downloads rose 2 percent.
5. Platforms are tightening. Etsy banned AI-made patterns, recipes and guides in October 2026. Amazon requires AI disclosure on books. Google's rules name mass AI pages as spam.

## Q2. What are the rules on AI-made content in 2026, and are they enforced?

- **Etsy.** New rules were published on 22 October 2026. AI-made "functional guides" are banned: patterns, recipes and instructions must show "a real result made using the seller's own instructions". Listings made by bots with no human involvement are banned and will be removed. Sellers must disclose AI use. AI art is still allowed where the seller plays "a clear creative role". Etsy warns sellers when it detects AI images at listing and says it will extend this to existing listings. Buyers get a button to report AI listings. [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/10/26/etsy-ai-policy/amp/) (independent). Other coverage says repeat offenders can lose the whole shop ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-bans-ai-patterns/), independent, search extract only). Enforcement looks patchy so far: six days after the rules, a fresh AI copy of a viral crochet hat was on the site, and Etsy has published no removal numbers ([tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/au/etsy-ai-guidelines-copying-sellers-sceptical-2026/), independent, low grade, search extract only). Etsy's own rules page was blocked.
- **Amazon KDP.** "We require you to inform us of AI-generated content (text, images, or translations) when you publish a new book." AI-assisted work (you wrote it, AI tidied it) need not be declared. "We'll reject or remove content that does not adhere to these guidelines." It also bars "content that's typically disappointing to customers". [KDP content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672410) (primary, read today). In September 2023 Amazon capped new titles at three a day per account ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/), independent). I could not confirm the cap is still three in 2026. Claims that account bans rose in 2025 and 2026 come only from tool sellers' blogs, so I do not rely on them.
- **Apple App Store.** Apple says it has no rule against AI-built apps and that "all apps are held to the same high standards" ([9to5Mac, July 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/), independent). Apple's own count for 2024: over 320,000 submissions rejected as spam, copies or misleading ([TechCrunch, April 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/), independent, quoting Apple). Apple also removed three AI app-building tools (Replit, Vibecode, Anything) for breaking its rules ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/apple-app-store-experiences-surge-in-new-apps-amid-vibe-coding-boom-2000742653), independent, search extract only).
- **Google Search (for content sites).** Google's spam policy names "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" as "scaled content abuse". Sites that break the policy "may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all". [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) (primary, read today).
- **WordPress plugin directory.** No AI-specific rule found. Every plugin is reviewed by hand, and since September 2026 each release is also scanned by "several AI models" ([WordPress plugin team](https://make.wordpress.org/plugins/), primary).
- **Notion.** No AI rule found. Sellers must join a waiting list and be approved. Notion takes 8 percent plus 40 cents a sale ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace), primary).
- **Stock image sites, Udemy, Skillshare, Canva, Gumroad, Payhip, Redbubble, Chrome Web Store, Shopify, Google Play.** Current AI rules not found. The Adobe Stock and Chrome help pages were blocked.

## Q3. What happened after AI content flooded in (2023 to 2026)?

The same thing in every family with data: far more things for sale, about the same number of buyers.

- **Apps.** About 600,000 new iPhone apps in 2025, up 30 percent. About 560,000 in the first half of 2026 alone, double the year before. Downloads rose 3 percent in 2025 and 2 percent in the first half of 2026 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/), independent, reporting Sensor Tower estimates via the New York Times). New subscription apps went from about 2,000 a month in January 2022 to over 14,700 a month in January 2026. Apps launched in 2025 or later take "just 3%" of subscription revenue. The share of new apps reaching $1,000 a month slipped from 19 percent to 17 percent ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/), primary; [9to5Mac, March 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/04/new-report-looks-into-the-sustainability-of-the-subscription-app-market-for-2026/), independent).
- **WordPress plugins.** About 700 new plugins a week were submitted in May 2026: "an increase of 2.7 times compared to 2025, and 5 times compared to 2024" ([WordPress plugin team](https://make.wordpress.org/plugins/), primary).
- **Stock images.** A Stanford and UCLA study of a large image marketplace found 78 percent more images a month once AI images were allowed, "driven almost exclusively by generative AI production", and an extra 23 percent fall in non-AI artists. Total sales rose 39 percent, so buyers gained. Sellers who did not use AI lost ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/when-ai-generated-art-enters-market-consumers-win-artists-lose), independent, an academic study, May 2025). One older vendor analysis points the other way for early movers: AI images earned about 4.5 times more per image for a small group of its customers ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/AI-generated-images-found-to-make-more-money-on-Adobe-Stock-compared-to-real-counterparts.724535.0.html), vendor_or_seo, undated, search extract only).
- **3D models.** On CGTrader about one upload in six was AI-made between June 2025 and May 2026, but AI items took 2.6 percent of sales and $1 in every $90 of revenue. "AI is entering the catalog rapidly, but buyers aren't yet opening their wallets for it" ([Fortune, August 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/20/ai-product-fatigue-online-marketplace-ecommerce/), independent).
- **Books.** Self-published titles in the US were reported at about 3.5 million in 2025, up 38.7 percent on 2024 (Bowker figure repeated on self-publishing statistics pages such as [ALLi](https://www.allianceindependentauthors.org/?p=483); search extract only, not checked against Bowker). Low-earning authors in a 2026 survey (582 replies) describe "Fast buck AI books flooding the market making discoverability almost impossible" ([Written Word Media 2026 mid-year survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2026-mid-year-indie-author-survey/), independent, self-selected).
- **Etsy.** Buyers are shrinking, not growing: active buyers down 5.0 percent, active sellers down 10.9 percent, sales down 2.4 percent year on year ([Etsy](https://investors.etsy.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/213/etsy-inc-reports-third-quarter-2025-results), primary, third quarter 2025). Etsy then banned the main AI-made digital goods (see Q2). Etsy has not said what share of listings are AI-made.
- **Print-on-demand.** Redbubble paid artists A$51.2 million in financial year 2024, down from A$65.0 million a year earlier ([Articore](https://www.articore.com/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/VFV_6YfHZ0qKveaD4XGZZA/files/presentations/FY24_Annual_Report.pdf), primary, search extract only). In June 2026 Amazon began letting shoppers make their own AI designs and have them printed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/), independent, search extract only). The buyer no longer needs the seller's design.
- **Shopify apps.** 8.8 apps for every thousand shops, up from 5.5 in July 2023. App numbers grew by more than 28 percent a year while shop numbers grew 10 to 14 percent ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-app-store-saturation/), vendor_or_seo).
- **Content sites.** Google visits to small publishers fell nearly 60 percent between June 2025 and June 2026, against about 43 percent for large ones (Chartbeat data in [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ai-forces-publishers-rethink-search-dependent-revenue-models), independent, search extract only). When Google shows an AI summary, 8 percent of users click a normal result, against 15 percent when it does not (Pew Research, July 2025, in [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/publishers/the-ai-search-reckoning-is-dismantling-open-web-traffic-and-publishers-may-never-recover), independent). Google disputes outside measurements of this kind.
- **Udemy.** The instructor's share of subscription income was cut from 25 percent in 2023 to 15 percent from January 2026 (Udemy's own November 2023 announcement, as reported by [FreshLearn](https://freshlearn.com/blog/udemy-good-for-instructors/), vendor_or_seo). This is a pay cut, not proven to be caused by AI.
- **One wider study.** Unpublished research on an app marketplace found new apps up about 160 percent by April 2026 after AI coding tools spread, and fewer apps getting more than 10 reviews. The researcher says the result is not proof of cause ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/20/ai-product-fatigue-online-marketplace-ecommerce/), independent).

## Q4. How long until a newcomer's first pound?

Mostly not found. No platform publishes time to first sale. What exists:

- **Apps.** The apps that do reach $1,000 a month take a median of 58 days to get there. That counts winners only. The middle app is still at about $72 a month after a full year ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/), primary).
- **Books.** About 80 percent of authors with one to four books earn under $100 a month ([Written Word Media 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-author-survey/), independent).
- **Shopify apps.** The newest fifth of apps earn about $190 a month, modelled ([Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-app-store-saturation/), vendor_or_seo).
- **Udemy.** People who joined since 2020 average $2,100 a year, against $5,400 for earlier joiners ([SellCoursesOnline](https://sellcoursesonline.com/udemy-earnings-study), vendor_or_seo). Arriving late pays less.
- **Notion.** A seller must first be approved from a waiting list. Money is held 14 days and paid only above a $20 balance ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace), primary).
- **GPT Store.** Never, for a UK seller, as things stand (see Q1).
- **Etsy, Gumroad, print-on-demand, Chrome, WordPress, stock, prompts, content sites.** Not found.

## Q5. Is there any family where the typical newcomer reaches £500 a month within 12 months, with no selling and no adverts?

No. I found none.

- Where a real median exists, it is nowhere near: about £55 a month for a subscription app after a year, about £54 a year on Gumroad, 10 users for a Chrome extension, under $100 a month for four in five authors with one to four books.
- Where only an average exists (Etsy about £120 a month in gross sales, Redbubble under £50 a year, Udemy late joiners about £135 a month), the average is already below the bar, and the middle seller sits below the average.
- Three figures look like exceptions and are not:
  - **Shopify's "middle app" at $2,500 to $5,200 a month.** This is a model, for the 1,675 apps with enough data, with a stated error of up to double. The money sits in old apps. The newest fifth earn about $190 a month.
  - **Self-published authors' median of $12,749 a year.** Only people who spend half their working life on it could answer.
  - **"17.3 percent of apps reach $1,000 a month".** True of apps serious enough to wire up subscriptions, within two years, including teams that buy adverts. For a first-time maker with no audience and no adverts the share will be lower. How much lower: not found.
- None of the sources separates out "newcomer, no audience, no adverts". Every sample includes people with followings, back catalogues and advert budgets. So the real figure for the owner's situation is below every number in this report (inference).

A minority does get paid in every family. The evidence says who they are: people with many products, several years in, an audience or an advert budget. That is the opposite of "passive, easy, quick".

## Q6. Do the much stronger AI models of 2026 make this better or worse for a newcomer?

Worse.

- Stronger models cut the cost of making things for everyone at once. The evidence shows a flood of sellers, not a rise in typical earnings: iPhone apps doubled while downloads rose 2 percent; WordPress plugin submissions are five times the 2024 level; one image marketplace got 78 percent more images each month than it would have without AI.
- Newer entrants earn less. Apps launched since 2025 take 3 percent of subscription revenue. The share reaching $1,000 a month fell from 19 to 17 percent. New Shopify apps earn about a hundredth of the oldest ones. Udemy's later joiners earn under half of the early ones.
- Buyers mark AI-made goods down where they can tell: 17 percent of uploads but 2.6 percent of sales on CGTrader.
- Platforms respond by restricting AI goods (Etsy, Amazon KDP, Google Search).
- The buyer can now make the simple things without a seller. Amazon lets shoppers generate their own printed designs. A person who wants a planner or a prompt can ask an AI model for one. The second sentence is my inference; the first is reported.

One honest point the other way: a strong model lets a non-coder ship a working app at all, which was not true in 2023. That lowers the cost of trying. The evidence above says it does not raise the odds of being paid, because it lowered the cost for hundreds of thousands of other people too.

## Verdict

**NO_TYPICAL_PAYOFF**

The decisive reason: in every family with real data, the middle seller earns a small fraction of £500 a month (about £55 a month for an app after a year, about £54 a year on Gumroad, 10 users for a Chrome extension), and no source shows a typical newcomer with no audience and no adverts reaching the bar in any family. The 2026 models made this worse by doubling the number of sellers while the number of buyers stayed flat.

What the check changes: the statement made on 19 November was right, and it now rests on evidence instead of general knowledge. A small minority is paid in each family, so "it never pays anyone" would be wrong; "it does not pay the typical newcomer, and it is not quick or passive for the few it does pay" is what the evidence supports.

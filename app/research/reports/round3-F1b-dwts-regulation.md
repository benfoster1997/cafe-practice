# F1 "DWTS waste-carrier capture" — Regulation ground truth and the free-portal threat

## 1. Timeline: Phase 1 holds; the carrier date has ALREADY slipped once and is still "subject to confirmation"

- Phase 1 (permitted waste receiving sites) is real and on track: public beta opened 28 April 2026 (EA blog, environmentagency.blog.gov.uk, 30 Apr 2026: "Digital Waste Tracking goes live"), regulations laid in Parliament May 2026 (legislation.gov.uk — The Digital Waste Tracking (England) Regulations 2026; Wales SI 2026/105; Hansard Lords debate 15 June 2026), mandatory 1 Oct 2026 England/Wales/NI, Scotland 1 Jan 2027. The inherited premise holds.
- Phase 2 (carriers, brokers, dealers, exporters — the concept's entire market) was **delayed six months in February 2026: April 2027 → October 2027** (circularonline.co.uk "Mandatory Digital Waste Tracking rollout delayed by six months"; atfpro.co.uk 9 Feb 2026). Multiple sources (Burges Salmon, cellcomply) still describe carriers as joining Oct 2027 "subject to confirmation". Programme history is one of serial slippage (originally 2023/24, then April 2026, then receipt-only descope, then the Feb 2026 slip). Phase 2 private beta is scheduled autumn 2026 (invited operators and software providers), public beta spring 2027. A product "timed for Oct 2027" is timed to a date the department has already moved once.

## 2. The free government offer: NOT a comprehensive portal — and that is worse news than a good portal would be

- Fee confirmed: **£26/year per legal entity that creates or edits records**, rolling 12-month access, designed "to remove any financial barrier to compliance for smaller operators" (wastematrix.co.uk guide; gov.uk guidance "Report receipt of waste").
- Defra's own stated strategy (defradigital.blog.gov.uk, 28 May 2026, "why the market is our primary user"): the service is **API-first; operators are expected to comply through commercial software**; the free route is a **temporary spreadsheet upload**, explicitly a bridge "until at least October 2027", not a permanent portal. Manual entry/CSV concepts were deprioritised after user research. So yes — a 10-15 load/day carrier will not live in a free spreadsheet.
- But the consequence is fatal for the concept's positioning: Defra deliberately cultivates a commercial software market and **publishes an approved-provider directory on GOV.UK** ("Report receipt of waste: choose a software provider"). **~25 software companies were Defra-listed by June 2026 — for Phase 1 alone, 16 months before any carrier is mandated** (letsrecycle.com, which also reported a convicted waste exporter among the listed providers — showing how low the listing barrier is). Being absent from that list is a cold-outreach objection no unknown solo founder can answer; being on it requires only the free PAT process every competitor also passes.

## 3. API access: open, free — and the carrier-side spec is NOT published yet

- The Receipt of Waste API is published with full developer docs (defra.github.io/waste-tracking-service; source repo github.com/DEFRA/waste-tracking-service). Onboarding is open and free: register via a Qualtrics form, receive test client ID/secret, pass 14 Gherkin PAT scenarios, receive production credentials and per-organisation API Codes, get GOV.UK-listed. Third-party software can submit on an operator's behalf. There is no gatekept recognition scheme — anyone, including a weekend AI build, clears it.
- **The carrier/movement-creation API — the one this product needs — does not exist yet.** The developer guide's nav covers "Phase 1 Receipt of Waste" only; the README (updated Jan 2026) says Defra is "exploring the development of new APIs" for carriers, and the carrier flow exists only as a storyboard. The real spec arrives via Phase 2 private beta (autumn 2026) and public beta (spring 2027). So in Aug 2026 the product is **not buildable against a real spec**, and the day the spec drops, all 25+ listed Phase 1 providers get it simultaneously — with existing customer bases and GOV.UK listings.

## 4. What actually changes for a small carrier — and what it's worth

- Today: paper/PDF waste transfer notes for non-hazardous waste; **season tickets (annual WTNs) cover repeat collections for up to 12 months** (netregs.org.uk; wastebolt.app explainers); hazardous movements use consignment notes with existing EA electronic submission. Duty-of-care records kept 2 years. Phase 1 does not change carrier paperwork — WTN rules continue for them until Oct 2027.
- From Oct 2027: per-movement digital records (EWC codes, haz/POPs flags, weights/containers, carrier/receiver details, WT ID) replacing the paper regime. **The season ticket's fate is unresolved — CIWM's position statement explicitly lobbies to retain it** to protect SME operators from per-load burden (circularonline.co.uk CIWM position statement). If Defra keeps a digital season-ticket equivalent, the per-load pain the product monetises largely evaporates for exactly the repeat-collection small carriers being targeted.
- Willingness to pay is already price-anchored: **WasteBolt sells "DWT 2026 Ready" carrier software — driver app, unlimited WTNs, season tickets, e-signatures — from £24.99/month** (wastebolt.app). AnyWaste has a dedicated carriers/DWTS product (anywaste.com/carriers), plus Vaste, Consigns, WasteProof, cellcomply, Fieldmotion, Fissara, Nanosoft, WTNcloud, Tipper360, Weightron, Bartec, Integrated Skills all marketing DWTS compliance now. The £49-99/month premise is disproved by a live market at a quarter to half that price, 14 months before the mandate.

## 5. Enforcement reality and the edoc precedent

- Penalties on paper: non-compliance is a criminal offence; EA/NRW enforcement via warnings, statutory notices, civil penalties, permit suspension; reported £1,000 fixed penalties for breaches from Oct 2026 (Burges Salmon; wasters.io; UKAuthority). Receipt records due within 2 working days.
- Precedent says urgency will be weak: **edoc, the EA's free voluntary electronic duty-of-care system (launched Jan 2014), flopped — c. 2.5% uptake among LAs running business waste services**, with the industry staying on paper (letsrecycle.com; assets.publishing.service.gov.uk edoc leaflet; ScienceDirect evaluation of duty-of-care enforcement). Carriers digitise only when forced, at the last minute, via whatever their existing supplier bundles. Expect a soft-launch enforcement posture toward 300,000 registered CBDs in the first year (the cohort includes vast numbers of registered-but-incidental carriers). Cold outreach in 2026 would be selling urgency that does not yet exist.

## 6. Trade associations

- No evidence CIWM, ESA or UROC are building or commissioning free member DWTS tools. Activity is webinars (ESA, 27 May 2026), position statements and lobbying (CIWM: retain season ticket, phased rollout, SME funding support). Not a free-tool threat — but CIWM's lobbying, if successful, softens exactly the burden this product charges for.

## Dimension conclusion

The inherited premises survive only partially: dates confirmed but Phase 2 already slipped once and remains unconfirmed; £26 fee confirmed; "clunky free portal" confirmed — but the reason it is clunky is that Defra's declared delivery model is *commercial software as the primary interface*, complete with a GOV.UK-published approved-provider list (25 entries by June 2026) and free, open, ungated API onboarding. The regulation is structurally designed to feed the swarm this project's Lesson 1 warns about, the carrier API the product needs is unpublished, and the price point is already anchored at £24.99/month by a shipped competitor. Within this dimension, the concept fails not because the free portal does the job, but because the regulatory architecture hands the job to a crowd the founder cannot outrun from a cold start on a date that may move again.

## Verdict

KILL (this dimension) — the regulation is real but its delivery architecture is the threat: Defra's 'market is our primary user' model plus a GOV.UK-published approved-provider list (~25 vendors by June 2026) and free open API onboarding structurally feeds the clone swarm, the carrier API needed to build F1 is not yet published, the Oct 2027 mandate has already slipped once and remains unconfirmed, and a shipped competitor at £24.99/month disproves the £49-99 pricing premise.

# F1 "DWTS waste-carrier capture" — Buyer, Population & Cold-Start Distribution

## 1. The register: enumerable, but contact-blind and about to be restructured

The premise partially verifies. The EA register of waste carriers, brokers and dealers is public and downloadable ([data.gov.uk dataset](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/961d75f3-3270-4926-afc2-f897d9a07386/waste-carriers-brokers-and-dealers); [environment.data.gov.uk public register](https://environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/view/search-waste-carriers-brokers)). The CSV export carries: Registration Number, Business Name, **Company Number**, Registration Type, **Registration Tier (upper/lower)**, **Applicant Type**, Registration/Expiry/Renewal Dates, Address, Postcode ([example CSV endpoint](https://environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/waste-carriers-brokers/registration.csv?name-number-search=fcc&register=waste-carriers-brokers)). **No phone. No email.** The web UI truncates at 2,000 results per query, so full enumeration requires postcode-slicing or an FOI — feasible, but every contact detail must then be enriched by hand (Companies House, websites, Facebook pages). For PECR purposes the Company Number / Applicant Type fields do let you split the ltd (cold-emailable) subset from sole traders (not emailable) — that is the one genuinely useful property of this register.

Exact upper-tier England count could not be pinned from search (UI caps at 2,000; see gaps). Context on the sector's informality: a 2021 study for the CBD reform estimated **~238,471 UNregistered** businesses may be transporting waste in England ([Defra CBD reform consultation](https://consult.defra.gov.uk/eq-resources-and-waste/consultation-on-cbd-reform/)) — the register's long tail is scrap collectors, house-clearance one-mans and builders whose carriage is incidental (upper-tier licences are traded casually in Facebook scrap groups). Devolved registers exist ([SEPA](https://www2.sepa.org.uk/wastecarriers/), [NRW](https://naturalresources.wales/permits-and-permissions/waste-carriers-brokers-and-dealers-public-register/?lang=en)) but publish no counts, and Scotland renames carriers to "transporters" from April 2026 under a separate SSI ([Digital Waste Tracking (Scotland) Regulations 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2026/145/made)).

**Register instability (new kill fact):** the CBD reform confirmed by Defra (Oct 2023 response, Sept 2025 policy paper) moves upper-tier carriers/brokers/dealers into the **environmental permitting regime** — a draft SI already exists ([Environmental Permitting (Waste Controlling or Transporting) Regulations 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348283471/contents); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/09/reforming-the-waste-carrier-broker-and-dealer-syst)). The 2021 reform IA assumed £40m familiarisation and £15m permit application fees across the cohort. So the very list the founder would scrape is being churned — re-registrations, exits of marginal one-mans, new identifiers — during exactly the 2026–27 build-and-sell window.

## 2. The real buyer pool is small

The substantive trading segment (skip-hire/grab/muckaway with weighbridges and fleets) is on the order of **~520 companies** — the population a commercial industry report actually analyses ([Research & Markets UK Skip Hire report](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/united-kingdom-skip-hire-market)). Trade bodies exist — [UROC](https://wastersblog.com/874/united-resource-operators-confederation-trade-association-uk-skip-hire-sector/) (SME skip/waste-transfer) and the National Skip Hire Association — but publish no member counts. These real firms are mostly limited companies (emailable); the register's vastly larger tail is sole-trader man-and-van clearance (PECR-blocked for email, TPS-screened for calls). Net: the enumerable, lawfully-contactable, actually-in-scope pool is likely 1,000–2,000 firms nationally — and 24–30 customers must come from a segment already served by named incumbents (below).

## 3. What they run today

Paper is real: even in 2025 many skip operators run paper consignment notes and guesswork routing ([SunSkips](https://sunskips.co.uk/news/waste-tech-is-advancing-but-skip-hire-is-still-stuck-in-the-past/)). But the software shelf is crowded at every price point: BigChange JobWatch at **£79.95–£99.95/user/month** plus £14.95 vehicle tracking, with reviewers reporting **5-year minimum contracts** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/149479/JobWatch-powered-by-BigChange/)); Podfather and Waste Logics quote-only; and a **DWT-specific swarm already live 14 months before the carrier mandate**: WasteMatrix (["free until October"](https://wastematrix.co.uk/)), WTNcloud, Nanosoft, MyDataCrate, Recyclr, Trackersack, Wastebolt, PaperRoute, DigitalWTN, FieldMotion, SkipM8, SkipRoute, Wasters.io, VWS, plus Access Group and Avery Weigh-Tronix at the top end. This is Lesson 1 (SWARM) fully realised — worse than Martyn's Law, where there was one clone; here there are 10+ visible, some free.

## 4. Sales culture vs a no-access founder

This is a phone-first, relationship trade sold via field demos, long contracts and trade shows. A no-name founder must TPS/CTPS-screen every call, can lawfully cold-email only the ltd subset after manual enrichment (register has no emails), and has no association standing — UROC/NSHA partnership is theoretically open but those bodies will platform established vendors at Letsrecycle Live/ESS-type events, not an unknown solo. BigChange-style 5-year lock-ins also mean the best prospects are contractually unavailable.

## 5. Awareness and buying timing

Carrier-cohort awareness is essentially nil today — all current noise targets the Oct 2026 **receiver** phase ([EA blog, April 2026 go-live](https://environmentagency.blog.gov.uk/2026/04/30/digital-waste-tracking-goes-live-a-major-step-forward-in-stopping-waste-crime/); [Hansard, June 2026](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/2026-06-15/debates/C4F47E52-77D0-407E-8B99-4DE96DA4CFD9/DigitalWasteTracking(England)Regulations2026)). Carrier mandate confirmed **October 2027**, with private beta autumn 2026 and public beta spring 2027 ([letsrecycle](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/defra-confirms-timeline-for-digital-waste-tracking-rollout/)). The MTD precedent confirms deadline-cluster buying: ~50,000 signed up by March 2026 vs 500,000+ at the August deadline with **4 in 10 still not signed up at the deadline itself** ([GB News](https://www.gbnews.com/money/making-tax-digital-taxpayers-face-april-hmrc-deadline); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/10/mtd_hmrc/)). Translation: 12+ months of cold outreach into indifference, then a panic window in mid-late 2027 that panic buyers resolve by picking the visible incumbent, the vendor their tip/receiver recommends, or the free option — never the unknown solo.

## 6. Churn shape — the fatal one

DWT is per-load, so usage recurs by construction, but the **government's own Waste Data Service is a free portal where carriers log in and create/confirm movements directly**, with APIs explicitly built so existing software can submit instead ([Defra DWT service docs](https://defra.github.io/waste-tracking-service/about-digital-waste-tracking/); [Defra developer guide](https://defra.github.io/waste-tracking-service/production/)). This breaches the standing filter "no free government tool doing the same job" head-on. A standalone £49–99/month submission layer is a thin wrapper that is churny in both directions: downmarket to Defra's free UI, upmarket to the job-management system (Podfather/BigChange/Waste Logics) the carrier already pays for, which becomes system-of-record the day it ships DWT API submission. The sticky position belongs to incumbents who already own the job data.

## Verdict on this dimension

**KILL.** The register is enumerable but contact-blind (no phone/email) and being restructured by permitting reform mid-runway; the genuinely in-scope buyer pool is ~500–2,000 firms already carpeted by incumbents and 10+ DWT-specific entrants (one literally free); compliance demand won't materialise until a mid-2027 panic that a free Defra portal and integrated incumbents will absorb; and every distribution channel available to a no-access, no-trust founder — cold email (ltd-only, hand-enriched), TPS-screened cold calls into a phone-first trade, association partnerships — is the weakest possible way to sell into this culture at this moment.

## Research gaps / verify-in-browser list

Could not verify in this sandbox (environment.data.gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, letsrecycle.com, consult.defra.gov.uk all egress-blocked — check in a browser): (1) the exact count of upper-tier registrations in England — download the full register CSV or slice by postcode, and count rows where Company Number is non-empty to size the PECR-emailable ltd subset precisely; (2) the 2026 DWT impact assessment (ukia_20260075_en.pdf) figures for number of carrier businesses affected and assumed per-business software costs (£26.3m ongoing software cost over the appraisal period implies a very low per-firm assumption — worth reading); (3) whether Defra's free carrier UI supports bulk/high-volume entry or is deliberately minimal (determines how much room a paid wrapper has); (4) UROC and NSHA member counts and whether either has flagged Oct 2027 to members yet; (5) SEPA/NRW/NIEA register sizes and download formats; (6) the exact commencement timetable of the CBD-to-permitting migration relative to Oct 2027 (whether upper-tier registrations lapse into permits before, during, or after the DWT carrier mandate).

## Verdict

KILL — a free Defra portal does the core compliance job for a buyer pool that is small, contact-blind on the register, already swarmed by 10+ DWT vendors and locked-in incumbents, and that will not buy until a mid-2027 panic no cold-start, no-access founder can intercept.

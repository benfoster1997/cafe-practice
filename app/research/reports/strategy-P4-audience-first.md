# P4 — AUDIENCE-FIRST: does building distribution as the asset work for this founder?

## Summary of the finding

The path as literally framed — *leverage the parked "Cooked Books" channel as distribution for a B2B SaaS* — is **dead on arithmetic**, not on opinion. The path as it should be framed — *pick the vertical first, then build a small permission asset inside that vertical while selling* — is **conditionally alive, but only as a component of a selling strategy, never as a replacement for one**. The decisive fact is that audience-first is a strategy optimised for a scale this business never needs to reach.

---

## 1. Does a general-interest finance-storytelling audience convert into B2B SaaS customers?

**No. The mechanism that makes audience-first work is absent.**

Every documented audience-first success has the same structure: **the content topic and the product's buyer are the same person.**

- Nathan Barry blogged and taught *professional bloggers*, then sold ConvertKit — an email tool *for professional bloggers*. He publicly ran a "Web App Challenge" to hit $5,000 revenue in six months and blogged the whole build ([Indie Hackers podcast 008](https://www.indiehackers.com/podcast/008-nathan-barry-of-convertkit); [nathanbarry.com](https://nathanbarry.com/15-lessons-15-million/)).
- Arvid Kahl's FeedbackPanda ($55K MRR, sold in 2 years) was built for online ESL teachers — the community his partner was *in* ([supabird.io teardown](https://supabird.io/articles/arvid-kahl-how-this-indie-hacker-bootstrapped-feedbackpanda-to-55k-mrr-and-sold-it-in-just-2-years)).
- Pieter Levels' audience is nomads and makers; his products (Nomad List, RemoteOK, Photo AI) are for nomads and makers ([thebootstrappedfounder.com](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/pieter-levels-the-indie-hackers-guide-to-ai-startups/)).

"Cooked Books" is finance/business **entertainment** — frauds, collapses, fortunes. Its viewers are consumers of narrative. They are not a purchase-intent cohort for any vertical B2B tool, and critically **they are not identifiable even if some of them qualify.**

### The arithmetic (this is the kill)

Take the channel's own top-few-percent outcome from `docs/YOUTUBE_AI_MONETISATION_STUDY.md`: 50k–100k subscribers, ~350k long-form views/month, at 18–36 months. Finance-storytelling channels need a US-heavy audience for the RPM to work (that is *why* the niche was chosen), so UK share is realistically 10–20% → ~10k–20k UK subscribers at maturity.

Now the target vertical. The UK adult population is ~55m. A vertical with 5,000 firms is ~0.009% of UK adults; a large one (say ~100,000 registered waste carriers) is ~0.18%. Expected in-vertical people inside 15,000 UK subscribers:

| Vertical size (UK firms) | Expected in-audience | Identifiable? | Convertible at £79–99/mo? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 (party-wall surveyors) | ~0.3 people | No | — |
| 5,000 | ~1.4 people | No | — |
| 20,000 (letting-agent branches) | ~5 people | No | — |
| 100,000 (waste carriers) | ~27 people | No | A fraction |

Even in the most generous case, the number of in-vertical people inside a top-few-percent channel is *approximately your entire customer target* — meaning you would need **100% conversion of every single one**, and you have **no mechanism to find them** among 100,000 subscribers. The arithmetic fails by at least an order of magnitude, and it fails only after 18–36 months of weekly production.

**Verdict on the parked channel as a distribution asset: it is an unrelated business.** Two honest exceptions, neither of which rescues the thesis: (a) sponsorship income from the channel could *fund* the app business; (b) it would prove the founder can build an audience. Neither is distribution for a vertical B2B product.

One further trap worth naming: the audience *would* be somewhat useful for a **horizontal** "tool for small business owners" — which is precisely the most swarm-saturated category on earth and is excluded by the mission brief's own filters. So the channel steers the product toward the terrain that has already killed 8 concepts.

## 2. Audience-first done *properly* for B2B micro-SaaS in 2026

The correct version is not "build an audience, then find a product." It is **"choose the vertical, then build a small precise permission asset inside it."** The good news, and it is genuinely good, is how small that asset needs to be.

### The audience you need is ~1,000 people, not 100,000

- Email is the highest-converting acquisition channel for SaaS: **median 16.9% conversion, vs 4.1% paid search and 2.9% paid social** ([Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/), via [Kissmetrics](https://kissmetrics.io/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks)).
- **Warm introductions convert at 30–50% versus cold outreach at 2–5%** ([justinmckelvey.com founder-led sales guide](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/founder-led-sales)). An audience is, functionally, a warm-intro machine.
- Beehiiv's platform-wide median paid conversion is **0.62%** — ~6 buyers per 1,000 subscribers for a ~$10/mo product ([beehiiv State of Paid Newsletters 2026](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026)). That is the *general-audience* floor. A list that is 100% verified operators in one trade should beat it substantially, but assume 2–3% over 12 months, not 10%.

**So: 24–30 customers ≈ 800–1,500 in-trade subscribers at 2–3% conversion.** That is a buildable number. This is the single strongest genuinely-positive fact for P4 and it deserves to be stated plainly.

### But a newsletter does not create reach — it *converts* reach into permission

You still need a first-touch mechanism to get those 1,000 people to subscribe. Channel by channel, 2026:

**LinkedIn — usable for some trades, collapsing**
- Organic reach is down ~50% for the average user, with a 34% YoY decline ([MelRish](https://melrish.com/linkedin-organic-reach-dropped-50-in-2026-here-is-what-to-do-about-it/); [Socialinsider LinkedIn benchmarks 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)).
- Company pages fell 60–66% from 2024 to early 2026 and now reach ~1.6–5% of their own followers ([tryordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)).
- Personal profiles drive **561% more reach and 5x more engagement** than company pages ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)). Founders who reliably got 5,000–10,000 impressions now see 800–1,200 ([Remery](https://remery.ai/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-crisis-tactics-2026)).
- Structural problem for this founder: LinkedIn contains accountants, surveyors, letting agents and compliance staff. It does **not** contain skip-hire operators, waste-carrier drivers, or funeral directors in any density. The verticals that survived the kill ledger are largely the ones not on LinkedIn.

**X / build-in-public — dead for this purpose**
- Typical posts reach ~3–4% of an audience; engagement fell **9.0% YoY, the steepest of any tracked platform**; X is the only major platform with a **5.5% YoY audience decline** ([SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data); [Apaya benchmarks](https://apaya.com/blog/social-media-benchmarks-x-twitter)).
- Unverified accounts get roughly **one-third to one-half** the organic reach of the pre-algorithmic feed ([Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/twitter-x-organic-reach-strategy)).
- Even the pro-audience literature concedes the failure mode: *"Thousands of founders followed the building-in-public approach, thinking audience size alone would translate to early customers, but it didn't"* ([The Growth Terminal, "Building in Public Is Dead"](https://www.thegrowthterminal.com/blog/building-in-public-is-dead-here-s-what-works-instead/) — egress-blocked, quoted from search extract).
- Decisive point: build-in-public reaches **other founders**. Other founders are not letting agents. It is audience-building aimed at the wrong species, and it is also how the swarm finds your idea.

**Newsletter — real, but slower than the boosters claim**
- The widely-repeated "0 → 1,000 subscribers in 2–4 months" figure traces to a low-quality Medium post ([NextGrow](https://medium.com/@nextgrowai/how-to-grow-a-newsletter-from-zero-when-nobody-knows-your-name-yet-24fdc84ba4d4)) with no methodology. **Treat as unverified marketing content.** The same genre of source recommends "personal outreach to 50 specific people" and "guest spots in other newsletters" — i.e. it silently assumes you already have reach or relationships. This founder has neither.
- Harder datum: beehiiv's median creator took **66 days from launch to first dollar** ([beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026)) — but that is a self-selected platform cohort measuring paid-newsletter revenue, not B2B SaaS customers.

**YouTube for a specific profession — the one cold-start channel that still works**
- YouTube's recommendation engine pushes to non-subscribers, which is the only remaining free at-scale distribution. It is not hit by AI Overviews or the ~69% zero-click problem that kills year-one SEO.
- The illustrative case: a SaaS lawyer started from zero and *"four months later with fewer than 400 subscribers and most videos pulling a couple of hundred views, had generated dozens of booked calls"* ([The B2B Playbook](https://theb2bplaybook.com/b2b-youtube-strategy) — egress-blocked, quoted from search extract). **Single-source, marketing-blog quality — treat as illustrative of shape, not as proof of rate.** But the shape is right: precision over scale.
- Supporting structural point: *"a niche channel with 500 highly engaged subscribers often converts better than a general channel with 5,000 passive ones"* ([Vireo Video](https://www.vireovideo.com/saas-youtube-strategy/)). This is the exact inverse of what Cooked Books is built to do.

**Communities — exist, but gated against you**
UK trade communities are real (UK Plumbers Forums, [Electricians' Community UK](https://www.facebook.com/groups/ElectricianscommunityUK/), Electricians Forums UK since 2006). I could not verify their self-promotion rules without joining; the near-universal pattern is that vendor promotion is banned and enforced by members. Community participation works as *research and credibility*, not as a channel you can post offers into.

## 3. The channel nobody in the audience-first literature mentions: **rent the audience**

This is the most commercially significant thing I found, and it inverts the whole path.

- B2B newsletter sponsorship CPMs run **$90–$180 per thousand opens** for B2B SaaS lists and $40–$80+ for general B2B primary slots ([Dupple CPM benchmarks 2026](https://dupple.com/learn/cpm-benchmarks-b2b-newsletters); [Newsletrix](https://newsletrix.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates-by-niche)).
- *"8,000-subscriber newsletters in specialised B2B niches command $2,000 per placement, while 50,000-subscriber lifestyle newsletters struggle to get $800"* ([Newsletrix](https://newsletrix.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates-by-niche)). The model is explicitly rental: *"the publisher owns the audience; the advertiser rents access"* ([MediaIntercept](https://www.mediaintercept.com/post/newsletter-sponsorship-explained-a-marketers-guide)).

**The arithmetic that matters:** a single placement in an existing UK trade publication reaching 2,000–8,000 verified operators costs roughly £200–£1,500 and lands *this week*. Building an equivalent audience yourself costs 12–24 months of weekly output. For a business that needs **24–30 customers total**, renting is faster, cheaper, and directly testable. The founder's limited capital is a constraint, but £500 for a test placement is within reach of almost any budget that could sustain a 24-month project.

This also flips a diagnosis item: mechanism (d), "distribution is the binding constraint," has been treated as *unbuyable*. In niche B2B trade media it is buyable, in small units, at prices a hobbyist budget can absorb — and unlike paid ads at $200–600 CAC, the targeting is human-curated and 100% in-vertical.

## 4. Is a trade-specific audience faster than cold-selling them? Honest comparison

For a target of 24–30 customers:

**Cold/founder-led path.** At 2–5% cold conversion you need ~500–1,250 properly worked qualified contacts. At ~30–60 minutes per meaningful contact including follow-up, that is 250–600 hours ≈ 25–60 weeks at 10 hours/week. Bounded, unpleasant, and — crucially — it produces the one thing audience-building cannot: *direct evidence within weeks that someone will pay.* Note the PECR framing in the diagnosis is about **email**; business-to-business **telephone** contact remains available subject to TPS/CTPS screening, and **corporate subscribers** (limited companies, LLPs) sit outside the reg-22 consent requirement that binds sole traders and unincorporated partnerships. *(Inferred from the standing UK rules, not re-verified this session — ICO is egress-blocked. Verify before acting.)*

**Pure audience path.** No existing reach → realistically 12–24 months to ~1,000 in-trade subscribers at one piece/week, then 6–12 months of conversion. First revenue lands month 9–15 in a *good* case. Within 24 months the target is reachable only in the top decile of execution.

**Hybrid — the actual answer.** Every cold conversation ends with *"can I send you the monthly [trade] rule-change note?"* This converts a 2–5% channel into a permission asset at near-zero marginal cost, with opt-in captured by phone (PECR-clean regardless of entity type). Cold outreach funds discovery; the list compounds; the list makes the second hundred conversations warm at 30–50% instead of cold at 2–5%. **Audience-building belongs as a by-product of selling, not as a substitute for it.**

## 5. Compounding vs time — quantified honestly

The compounding argument is true and I will not strawman it: **an audience cannot be cloned by the swarm.** Four honest deflators:

1. **The payoff sits outside the horizon.** A content asset built in months 1–12 delivers most of its value in months 13–36. The founder's window is 24 months. He would be buying an asset that matures after his deadline.
2. **Compounding only defeats the swarm if the audience is in-vertical.** A general audience is not a moat; it is a hobby with a monetisation lag.
3. **Content is now swarmed too.** The same cheap-AI mechanism (a) that clones products has flooded every keyword-discoverable content niche. The places where AI content is scarce are exactly where credibility is verifiable — i.e. where the founder has industry standing. He has confirmed he has none.
4. **P4 spends the wrong resource.** This is the most under-appreciated fact in the whole path. Claude makes *build capacity* free and unlimited. It does **not** make *content capacity* free — the repo records 3–4 hours per video for CapCut assembly alone, plus mandatory script sign-off, and the July 2025 YouTube inauthentic-content policy makes the human authorship step load-bearing rather than optional. **P4 converts the one genuinely free input into the one genuinely scarce input: the founder's evenings.** At 10 hours/week, the fortnightly trade note (~1.5h amortised) plus 25 calls (~5h) plus admin consumes the entire budget. There is no version where he also produces weekly documentaries. The two projects are mutually exclusive on time, not complementary.

## 6. The parked YouTube channel judged on its own terms

Against the repo's own study (`docs/YOUTUBE_AI_MONETISATION_STUDY.md`, verified 10 Aug 2026):

- ~90% of channels never reach 1,000 subscribers; median monetised creator earns **~£2,200/year**, not per month.
- One analysis puts the odds of a new channel ever reaching the ~£100/day level at **~1 in 3,500**.
- £2,335/month needs ~160k–310k long-form views/month at finance RPM, ~1.37M at all-niche median RPM — with RPM figures explicitly ±50%.
- Of 100 similar starters at 12 months, *"expected number reaching £2,000/month net: essentially zero."*
- £2k/month is a **top-few-percent, 18–36 month** outcome — i.e. the modal success case lands *outside* a 24-month window.
- Tail risk: an AI-heavy pipeline is the exact profile under enforcement pressure (reported Jan 2026 wave of AI-channel terminations, ~16 channels/~35M subscribers). One action zeroes the income.

**Three things genuinely in its favour, which a fair analysis must state:**
1. It has no idea-stage kill risk. Eight concepts have died before shipping anything; the channel has one video ~90% finished. Cost-to-first-shipped-artefact is dramatically lower.
2. Its revenue does not depend on customer count, cold outreach, PECR, procurement, or a stranger trusting an unknown vendor — it attacks mechanism (d) directly by borrowing YouTube's recommendation engine, the last free at-scale distribution.
3. Faceless UK business-storytelling at scale is *proven* (MagnatesMedia, ~1.9M subscribers).

**Verdict on resuming it as the income route:** the channel's honest odds of £2,335/month within 24 months are **~1–3%** — comparable to or slightly worse than the app business's accepted single-digit-to-10%, with a worse tail (enforcement) and a payoff distribution centred beyond the deadline. It is **not** a better bet at the £2,335 target. It *is* a better bet at "ship something and learn whether I can hold a weekly habit" — which is a different objective, and the founder should be told those are different objectives rather than allowed to conflate them.

## 7. Verification honesty box

- Two of the most relevant articles (thegrowthterminal.com, theb2bplaybook.com, productmarketfitisexpiring.com) were **egress-blocked**; their figures are quoted from search extracts and are marked as such.
- The "SaaS lawyer: 4 months, <400 subscribers, dozens of booked calls" datum is **single-source marketing content**. Directionally useful, not a rate to plan on.
- The "0→1,000 subscribers in 2–4 months" newsletter figure is **unverified Medium content** and I recommend discarding it.
- Social-reach decline figures come from marketing-agency blogs citing Socialinsider/Sprout-type panels; the *direction and rough magnitude* (LinkedIn ~-50%, X ~-9% YoY engagement, ~3–4% reach) are consistent across independent sources, the exact percentages are ±30%.
- Newsletter CPM figures are from newsletter-industry vendors with an interest in high numbers; treat the £200–£1,500-per-placement range as an order of magnitude to test, not a quote.
- I found **no** documented case of a general-interest entertainment audience converting into a vertical B2B SaaS customer base. Absence of evidence here is weak evidence of absence, but the arithmetic in §1 is independent of it.

## Verdict

SPLIT — DEAD as framed, CONDITIONAL as corrected. DEAD for the literal path (using the parked "Cooked Books" finance-entertainment channel as distribution for a B2B SaaS): the decisive reason is arithmetic, not taste — even at the channel's top-few-percent 18-36 month scale (~15k UK subscribers), the expected number of people inside a 5,000-firm UK vertical is ~1.4, and they are unidentifiable within the subscriber base. CONDITIONAL for the corrected path (choose the vertical first, then build a ~1,000-person in-trade permission asset as a BY-PRODUCT of founder-led selling, and rent existing trade audiences at £200-£1,500 a placement rather than spending 18 months building one) — conditional on it never replacing direct selling, because audience-first optimises for a scale this business, needing only 24-30 customers, never has to reach.

## Odds

Headline: ~5% that this founder reaches £2,335/month within 24 months on P4 as a standalone strategy — at or slightly below his accepted single-digit-to-10% base rate, i.e. P4 alone makes things no better.

Decomposition (my estimates, reasoning stated):

P4a — parked Cooked Books channel as distribution for a B2B SaaS: 0.5%. Requires the channel to hit top-few-percent scale (itself ~2-3% likely per the repo study) AND the in-vertical population inside it to exceed ~1.4-27 people AND a targeting mechanism that does not exist. Compound of three unlikely events, one of which is arithmetically near-impossible.

P4b — pure audience-first (12-18 months building an in-trade audience, then product): 3-5%. The maths works (need only ~800-1,500 in-trade subscribers at 2-3% conversion) but first revenue lands month 9-15 in a GOOD case, leaving 9-15 months to go 0 to 25 customers; and the founder has no seeding mechanism — LinkedIn reach is down ~50% and mostly holds the wrong trades, X reaches other founders at 3-4% of audience, and community self-promotion is gated. Also fails the resource test: it converts free Claude build-hours into scarce founder evenings.

P4c — hybrid: pick the vertical, sell by phone from week one, capture every contact into a trade list, rent 2-3 existing trade-newsletter placements: 8-12%. Above the base rate, because it does not replace selling — it compounds it. Cold at 2-5% funds discovery; the list turns the second hundred conversations from 2-5% cold into 30-50% warm. This is the only version of P4 I would fund.

P4d — resume Cooked Books as its own income route to £2,335/month in 24 months: 1-3%, per the repo's own verified study (top-few-percent at 18-36 months, i.e. the modal success case lands AFTER the deadline), with an enforcement tail risk that can zero it in one action.

Comparison the founder actually asked for: resuming YouTube (1-3%) is NOT a better bet than the app business (single-digit to ~10%) at the £2,335 target. It is a better bet at the different objective of "ship something within 60 days and find out whether I can sustain a weekly production habit." Those objectives should not be conflated. The two projects compete for the same ~10 hours a week; they cannot both run.

## First 90 days

CONDITIONAL PATH = P4c (hybrid). Time budget assumed: 10 hours/week. Do NOT resume Cooked Books during this window — it consumes the same evenings.

DAYS 1-14 — Choose the vertical and build the list (≈15h)
1. Pick ONE vertical using the existing six filters in app/STATUS.md PLUS one new filter that P4 adds: "Does this trade have at least one existing publication, newsletter, forum or annual event whose audience I can RENT?" If no rentable audience exists, the trade is harder, not easier — deprioritise it.
2. Build a named prospect list of 300-800 firms from a public register (the EA waste-carrier register was already identified in Stage 1 as the best list found). Columns: firm, phone, website, Companies House number, incorporated Y/N.
3. Flag which are limited companies (corporate subscribers — email is PECR-clean subject to identification and opt-out) vs sole traders/unincorporated partnerships (consent required). Screen phone numbers against TPS/CTPS before calling. Get this checked in a browser; ICO is egress-blocked to Claude.
4. Identify the 1-3 rentable audiences: trade newsletter, trade magazine e-shot, association bulletin, LinkedIn group, regional trade show. Email each for a rate card. Ask for a test rate.

DAYS 15-45 — 100 conversations, zero content (≈45h)
5. Phone 25 firms per week. The goal is NOT sales. The goal is three artefacts: (a) 20+ verbatim problem descriptions in the operator's own words, (b) 60+ opt-ins to a fortnightly trade note, captured with dated consent wording, (c) 3-5 people who name a price unprompted.
6. Script ending, every single call: "Last thing — I write a short fortnightly note on [specific rule changes / practical things] for [trade]. Want me to add you?" This is the whole audience strategy. Opt-in by phone is clean regardless of entity type.
7. Log every call in one spreadsheet: date, firm, entity type, problem stated, price mentioned, opted in Y/N. Claude can build the tracker; the founder does the calls. Nobody else can do the calls.

DAYS 46-75 — Ship the note and buy one rented placement (≈35h)
8. Publish issue 1 fortnightly: 400-600 words. One rule change + one practical thing + one answer from an operator you spoke to. Claude drafts, founder edits and signs off (~45 min/issue). Name real operators (with permission) — that is the credibility an outsider can borrow.
9. Buy ONE placement in the cheapest rentable trade audience. Budget £200-800. Measure subscribers-per-pound and calls-booked-per-pound. This single number tells you whether distribution in this vertical is purchasable — which is the question eight dead concepts never asked.
10. Keep calling: 25/week, uninterrupted. Do not let content displace calls. If a week forces a choice, the calls win.

DAYS 76-90 — Hard gate (≈10h)
11. Continue on P4c ONLY if ALL of these hold at day 90:
    - 150+ in-trade opt-ins on the list
    - 5+ people who named £79/month or more unprompted
    - 1+ paid deposit, pre-order, or signed letter of intent
    - 25%+ open rate on issue 3
    - Rented placement produced subscribers at under £5 each
12. If they hold: build the single "I need this" feature (Claude, 2-4 weeks), sell it back down the same list. Target 5 paying by month 6, 12 by month 12, 25 by month 20.
13. If they do NOT hold: the vertical is wrong, not the strategy. Switch vertical and rerun days 1-90. Two failed verticals in a row means the constraint is the founder's ability to hold a weekly outbound habit — at which point the honest move is to reconsider whether any bootstrapped path fits, not to try a ninth idea.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not build a general audience first and look for a product later.
- Do not build in public on X. It reaches founders, not buyers, and it shows the swarm your idea.
- Do not open a LinkedIn company page. Personal profile only, and only if the chosen trade is actually on LinkedIn.
- Do not resume Cooked Books as a distribution play. If the founder wants it back, resume it as a separate income experiment with its own gate, knowing it costs the same 10 hours and cannot run alongside this.

# Supply-side check on the quote-writing pick (20 November 2026)

Scope: this report only asks what is already on sale. It does not test demand, law, or the owner's hours. No new ideas are proposed.

Source labels used below: **primary** (the seller's own page or price list, an app store listing, a regulator), **independent** (press, surveys, first-hand user posts), **vendor_or_seo** (marketing content: direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).

## Bottom line

1. The pick fails its own rule. Several apps sold to UK trades now turn a spoken description into a priced quote in minutes, for between nothing and about £44 a month.
2. The best-known UK names are only part of the way there (Tradify and Powered Now only tidy up wording). But a crowd of small UK voice-to-quote apps sell the exact outcome today, and ServiceM8, Fergus, Jobber and ANNA sell it or a close version.
3. People do pay humans for pricing work. They pay trained estimators per job (about £180 to £480) or general admin assistants by the hour (£25 to £35). I found nobody selling quote writing to small trades for a monthly fee.
4. The £395 service asks the contractor to do the same thing the app asks (record a voice note), takes longer (24 hours against minutes), and adds a check by someone with no trade knowledge.
5. The 14-day conversation test is not harmed by any of this. What fails is the plan that a passed gate leads to this particular service at this price.

## Q2. Do UK businesses already sell this as a done-for-you service?

Yes, in three forms. None matches the pick.

**1. Estimating services (trained estimators, paid per job).**
- One UK provider's price list (late 2025): take-off £60 to £160 per drawing. Small residential estimate £180 to £480, typically £320. Bid writing £350 to £650 a day. A 24-hour turnaround adds 15% to 35%. Normal turnaround is 2 to 4 working days. Buyers are "UK builders, specialist trades and SME contractors". [Cost Estimator](https://costestimator.co.uk/maximising-your-roi-how-estimator-services-can-help) (vendor_or_seo).
- HBXL Estimating Service: £279 plus VAT for a small extension, £389 plus VAT for a new build, in 3 to 4 days. [Comparison post](https://calliope-herring-6hpz.squarespace.com/blog/choosing-an-estimating-service-build-aviator-or-hbxl) (vendor_or_seo). One unhappy customer reports paying £799 plus VAT up front. The service has only 13 reviews. [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/estimating-service.co.uk) (independent, first-hand).
- Build Aviator (tied to Jewson's products) does not publish prices. 5 to 7 working days. Same comparison post (vendor_or_seo). Travis Perkins also has an [estimating service page](https://www.travisperkins.co.uk/estimatingservice) (primary; I could not read its contents).
- Freelance estimators advertise on Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer and PeoplePerHour. Reliable prices not found.
- How they find customers: through builders' merchants, through their own estimating software, and through search. (inference from the pages above; no direct evidence found.)

**2. Bid and tender writing agencies.**
- They quote a fixed fee per tender, with day rates and retainers for firms that bid often. The one agency page I could read publishes no prices. [Bid writers](https://bidwriters.pages.dev/resources/bid-writing-costs/) (vendor_or_seo).
- A software seller claims typical consultancy fees of £6,000 to £30,000 per project. Treat as a sales claim for large tenders. [Chamber member news](https://gmchamber.co.uk/news-opinions/member-news/bid-writing-just-got-a-whole-lot-simpler-with-bidvault) (vendor_or_seo, search extract only).
- The earlier P3 report already found accreditation form-filling (CHAS and similar) sold from £349 per application.
- Buyers are firms that bid for public or commercial contracts. That is not the typical one-van trade.

**3. Virtual assistants for trades.**
- Checkatrade tells tradespeople a UK virtual assistant costs £25 to £35 an hour and lists writing quotes among the tasks. [Checkatrade](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trade/hiring-a-virtual-assistant) (vendor_or_seo, search extract only).
- A Glasgow firm offers UK tradespeople help "from customer enquiries and scheduling to quoting, invoicing, and follow-ups", working inside Simpro, ServiceM8 and Tradify. No prices shown. [Directory profile](https://societyofvirtualassistants.co.uk/?p=12415) (vendor_or_seo).
- Two more turned up in search but their pages were blocked: Tradehand (takes a share of collected invoices) and TradieVA (a Simpro partner). Details not confirmed.
- My sum: £395 buys about 11 to 16 hours a month of a UK assistant who would also answer calls, book jobs and chase invoices (395 divided by 35 and by 25). (inference)

**What I did not find:** any UK business selling quote writing alone to small trades for a monthly fee, at any price.

## Q3. Does the pick pass the P3 rule?

The rule: "the service must sell an outcome no shipped product currently sells."

**It fails.**

- The outcome the pick sells is: send a voice note or photos, get back a priced quote.
- At least three small apps sell exactly that outcome to UK trades today: VoxTrade, squote and TradePA. Tradespal does it from a photo.
- Among the established apps, ServiceM8 drafts the quote from dictated notes, Jobber drafts quotes and is adding voice, and Fergus announced voice to quote for the UK in September 2026 (one blog says parts of it are still in beta). Kwikbill, Simpro and ANNA sell close versions.
- They return the quote in minutes. The pick returns it within 24 hours.
- They cost between nothing and about £44 a month. The pick costs £395. That is roughly 9 to 40 times the paid plans (395 divided by 44 and by 10). (inference from the prices above)
- The P3 report's answer to this objection was that the apps "are templates and you are writing". That was fair when it was written. It is no longer true. The apps now write.
- The same report killed the AI receptionist idea for this exact reason: a crowded product market at a fraction of the service price. The same test applied to quote writing gives the same answer.

Two honest limits on this finding:

- "On sale" is not "widely used" or "good". Most of the small apps have no reviews. Tradify and Powered Now, the names UK trades know best, do not yet do voice to quote.
- The tender-response half of the pick is less crowded by apps. But it is already served by agencies with real bid experience, and small trades rarely bid.

## Q4. What could a £395 human-checked service offer that a £20 to £60 app does not?

Going through the possible differences one at a time:

| Possible difference | Honest assessment |
|---|---|
| Less work for the contractor | No. The contractor records a voice note either way. |
| Faster | No. Slower: 24 hours against minutes. |
| A person checks it first | Real, but thin. The owner has no trade knowledge. He can catch typos, sums, VAT and missing lines. He cannot catch a wrong price or a missed part of the job. One reviewer notes AI drafts can pad with vague lines and sometimes add up wrongly ([blog](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/tradies-best-ai-seven-tools-job-price), vendor_or_seo), so checking has some value. |
| Better prices | No. Both the apps and the owner rely on the contractor's own figures. |
| Someone sets it all up (price list, wording, terms) | Real. One estimator sells "rates library build and templates" for £450 to £1,500 as a one-off ([Cost Estimator](https://costestimator.co.uk/maximising-your-roi-how-estimator-services-can-help), vendor_or_seo). That is a one-off fee, not £395 every month. |
| Chasing quotes that go quiet | Apps already do it. ANNA does it for £29 a month. |
| Serving people who will not use any app | Plausible. One forum regular says his builder friend found the apps "way too complicated" ([forum](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/what-apps-are-trades-using-for-quoting-jobs-now.430559/), independent, one anecdote). But the same friend's wife does his paperwork for nothing. |

**Is there evidence anyone pays for the difference?**

- For skilled human pricing, per job: yes. Builders pay trained estimators £180 to £480 an estimate (sources in Q2). They are buying measurement skill and knowledge of rates. The owner has neither.
- For general admin help by the hour: yes, at £25 to £35 an hour (source in Q2).
- For a monthly fee for quote writing, checked by a non-expert: **not found.** In a 31-reply forum thread about quoting, nobody says they pay someone to write quotes.
- When a would-be app maker asked UK trades in June 2026 what a quote builder was worth, he floated £20 to £30 a month. No tradesperson named a price they would pay. [Forum thread](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/posts/3281805/) (independent).

## Verdict

**PICK_FAILS**

Decisive reason: the pick was chosen because "no shipped product already sells the outcome". That is no longer true. Voice note in, priced quote out is now sold to UK trades by several apps, in minutes, for between nothing and about £44 a month. I found no evidence that anyone pays a monthly fee for a non-expert human check on top.

What this does and does not mean:

- It does **not** prove that no contractor would pay £395. A desk check cannot prove that. It means the reason given for choosing this service has gone, and every sales call will meet "there is an app for that at £15".
- It does **not** harm the 14-day conversation test. That test sells nothing. It measures whether the owner will contact strangers and what they say.
- It **does** mean the gate should not read "5 to 15 conversations, so sell the quote-writing service". The older wording in `STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3 was safer: sell whatever chore the contractors themselves raise most, and only after checking that no app already sells it.
- If the test goes ahead, add one question to it: "What do you use for quotes now, and have you tried an app or ChatGPT for it?" The answers are the only evidence that could reopen this pick.

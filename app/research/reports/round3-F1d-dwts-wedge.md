# F1 DWTS wedge / service-bridge dimension — findings

## 1. The wedge premise is dead — killed twice, independently

**The free route is not a clunky portal; it is designed for batch and for one-van operators.** DEFRA's submission model for receipt of waste is (a) an API from existing waste-management/weighbridge software, or (b) "a simple to use spreadsheet" upload — manual per-load portal entry was explicitly deprioritised after user research (defra.github.io developer guide; wastematrix.co.uk; fieldmotion.com). Receiving sites get **2 working days** to report each receipt (nanosoftltd.com; dsposal.uk), so end-of-day batching is legally accommodated — the answer to "could an admin batch 15 loads in 20 minutes via CSV" is **yes, that is the designed workflow**. For Phase 2 carriers, DEFRA has stated the system "will need to work for operators of all sizes, including sole traders with a single van", that there **will be a free DEFRA website**, that mobile access is treated as essential, and offline capability has been discussed (wtncloud.co.uk carrier guide; consultation coverage). The wedge premise — "driver-phone capture dramatically faster than a bad free portal" — requires betting, 14 months out, that a GDS-standard service whose stated design goal is single-van usability will ship a bad mobile flow. That is a hope, not a wedge.

**Even if DEFRA's UI is mediocre, the "nicer than DEFRA" slot is already swarmed.** Fourteen months before the carrier mandate I found ~15 named products aimed at exactly this: WTN App (wtnapp.co.uk, £29/mo, 20 free notes), WasteBolt (£24.99/mo unlimited WTNs + e-signatures), WasteProof (from £19/mo), WTNcloud (£1.50/note PAYG, no monthly fee), Wasteify, SkipRoute, PaperRoute, WasteNote/WasteMatrix (**"Free Until October"**), AnyWaste, Consigns, DigitalWTN, Nanosoft, FieldMotion, Dsposal, plus generic incumbents Podfather/BigChange and waste-native PurGo (driver tablets already in skip firms — sunskips.co.uk). This is the party-wall/Martyn's Law swarm pattern (lesson 1) already fully realised pre-mandate, and at **£0–29/mo — the concept's £49–99 price is 2–4x an already-saturated market with a free government anchor**.

## 2. Adjacent wedge (roadside capture) — real pain, but the most-occupied square on the board

The capture moment (signature, photos, weight, EWC pick at transfer) is genuinely where friction lives, and it is exactly what the existing stack sells: Wasteify ("capture proof on site, produce waste transfer notes"), WasteBolt (e-signatures embedded in digital WTNs), PurGo driver apps, Podfather ePOD, SkipRoute digital WTNs. Digital-WTN-with-signature apps predate DWTS by years. There is no vacant capture wedge; there is a price war in one.

## 3. Liability inversion — bites differently here, but still fatal to the price

Honest read: this is NOT Martyn's Law. Small carriers are a price-driven trade with low personal regulatory anxiety; DWTS records are operational duty-of-care records (s.34 EPA 1990 offences continue alongside DWT — greenzoneuk.com), rarely inspected for a compliant one-van firm. So carriers won't refuse an unknown vendor out of liability fear — **they'll refuse to pay much at all**. The softened inversion converts into a price ceiling (£19–29/mo, free DEFRA site) rather than a trust wall. A residual inversion does exist: routing statutory submissions through a solo vendor's API relay means the carrier wears the non-compliance if the vendor's sync breaks — the conservative choice remains DEFRA-free or a 15-year incumbent. Either way the concept's £49–99 pricing is unreachable.

## 4. Service-first bridge ("we do your DWTS for you", £99–199/mo) — legally plausible, economically a trap

- **Legality**: nothing in duty-of-care law requires the operator to personally key records; brokers and consultancies handle waste paperwork today, and the API model is by definition third-party submission. I could NOT verify a formal agent-authorisation mechanism in DWTS (registration is per legal entity via Government Gateway, ~£26/yr — paperroute.co.uk; digitalwtn.co.uk) — flag as a gap. Accountability for accuracy (EWC coding) stays with the carrier, which means a founder with zero waste expertise doing EWC classification from WhatsApp photos of illegible tickets is taking on misclassification exposure (waste classification under WM3 is genuinely technical) with no insurance story and no industry access to sanity-check.
- **Economics**: the buyer of "retype my tickets" at £99–199/mo is a carrier too disorganised to use a £24.99 unlimited app or a free DEFRA site — the churniest, messiest, lowest-trust segment. 24 such customers is a full-time human data-entry job (a job, not a product), and the inputs (photos, missing weights) resist Claude automation at the accuracy level statutory records need.
- **Distribution**: one-van carriers are disproportionately **sole traders — PECR bars cold email without consent** (lesson 4); what remains is TPS-screened cold calling into a trade where the founder has zero credibility. No evidence a bookkeeper-style DWTS service exists yet — but every waste consultancy, broker, and trade accountant can bolt it on with more trust the moment demand appears.

## 5. Timing play on receiving sites (Oct 2026) — no room

Permitted receiving sites are the weighbridge-software vendors' home turf: Avery Weigh-Tronix, Precia Molen, Weightron, Midsoft, mScales, WeighPay, VWS/Weighman are all shipping Receipt-of-Waste API integrations; DEFRA's spreadsheet covers software-less sites as an official contingency; and small-receiver-focused entrants (WasteBolt, AnyWaste, WasteMatrix — free until October) already target the residue. A permitted site without any weighing/records system is essentially nonexistent (the weighbridge is how they invoice). Six weeks, no access, no trust: nothing there for this founder.

## 6. Weekend clone test (2027)

The wedge product is itself the weekend build: DEFRA publishes the API spec and data definitions openly on GitHub (github.com/DEFRA/waste-tracking-service), the EWC List of Wastes is public, the carrier register is public. EWC-picker "intelligence" is an LLM feature every clone ships. The only un-clonable layers are (a) human fulfilment — which is the bad business in section 4 — and (b) weighbridge/accounting integrations — which incumbents already own. Nothing defensible remains, and the swarm's existence today proves it.

## Verdict on this dimension: KILL

The wedge fails on a verified fact pattern, not speculation: DEFRA has promised a free, mobile-first, sole-trader-grade tool with batch spreadsheet submission and a 2-working-day window, and ~15 vendors already sell the "nicer capture app" at £0–29/mo, 14 months before the mandate. The service bridge survives legal scrutiny but fails economics, distribution (PECR/sole traders), and founder-capability (EWC liability without expertise).

## Research gaps / verify-in-browser list

Proxy blocked direct fetches of gov.uk, defra.github.io, thefirstmile.co.uk and wastebolt.app, so verify in a browser: (1) the exact receipt-data-definitions field list and draft carrier field list at defra.github.io/waste-tracking-service/production/receipt-data-definitions/ (I relied on vendor summaries); (2) whether DWTS registration supports a formal agent/'on behalf of' delegation via Government Gateway — this determines the service-bridge's clean legality vs credential-sharing; (3) the actual quality of DEFRA's carrier-facing mobile UI — unknowable until the autumn 2026 private beta, and the single biggest untestable assumption in the wedge; (4) live pricing pages for WasteBolt (£24.99), WTN App (£29), WasteProof (£19) and WasteMatrix's 'free until October' offer, which I have only from search snippets; (5) whether the official spreadsheet route survives past the 'temporary compliance mechanism' phase (its withdrawal date would change the batch-entry analysis); (6) what fraction of registered upper-tier carriers are limited companies vs sole traders (determines the PECR-emailable universe).

## Verdict

KILL — DEFRA has committed to a free, mobile-first, sole-trader-grade tool with official spreadsheet batch submission and a 2-working-day reporting window, and ~15 vendors already sell the capture app at £0-29/month 14 months before the carrier mandate; the wedge premise is disproven and the service bridge is a PECR-constrained data-entry job with EWC-misclassification exposure, not a product.

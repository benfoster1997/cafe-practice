The report is strong where it argues (the graveyard analysis in Claim 14, the vertical-stickiness debunk in Claim 11, the platform-risk conclusion in Claim 8, the fabrication flag in Claim 15) and weak where it counts. (a) SELF-INCONSISTENT SOURCING — the most damaging finding. (c) AFFILIATE-BLOG SAMPLING BIAS PRODUCES THE CENTRAL PRICING THESIS — the "empty £49-99 band" is derived from a vendor list assembled via SEO comparison sites, which structurally over-sample cheap and enterprise tools and under-sample mid-market vendors that run no affiliate programme. (d) US/GBP CONFLATION — USD benchmarks (ACV bands, churn, trial conversion, the $99 ceiling) applied to a GBP target without conversion, consistently in the direction that flatters the plan.
(e) SURVIVORSHIP — partially and creditably mitigated by Claims 1 and 14, but the two case studies are both successes, and the single best-corroborated one (Swifteq) is an Irish TECHNICAL founder in a US marketplace, which inverts its relevance to a non-technical UK founder.

Its specific numbers should not be relied on for any decision. The two named regulatory errors (Renters' Rights penalties, shift-rights timing) and the MTD recognition barrier should be corrected before this informs opportunity selection, and the pricing thesis needs the customer calls the researcher himself recommends.

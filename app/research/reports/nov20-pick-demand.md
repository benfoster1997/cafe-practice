# Demand check on the quote-writing pick (20 November 2026)

Scope: this is not a new idea search. It tests one thing: is there evidence that small UK trade contractors would pay about £395 a month for someone to write their quotes? It checks the premises in `app/STATUS.md` (top section), `STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3 and `strategy-P3-service-first.md` sections 5h, 6 and "First 90 days".

Source labels used below: **primary** (official statistics, a firm's own price page), **independent** (trade body surveys, press, first-hand forum posts), **vendor** (marketing material or a survey paid for by someone selling something: direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).

All 13 permitted web searches were used. All evidence is UK. Two sources are old and are marked.

## Bottom line

1. The pain is real. Trades report 5 to 8 hours of admin a week, mostly in the evening, and quoting is part of it.
2. But the slow part of a quote is measuring and pricing. A remote writer with no trade knowledge cannot do that part.
3. The writing-up part takes about 20 to 45 minutes a quote, and a crowd of apps now does it from a voice note for £10 to £45 a month.
4. I found no small trade firm paying a person £300 to £500 a month to write quotes, and no service that sells exactly this.
5. Verdict: **DEMAND_WEAK_OR_UNPROVEN**. Keep the 14-day test, but change what it asks and do not treat £395 as settled.

## Q1. How do small trades produce quotes today, and what hurts?

**How they do it.** The first-hand picture is informal. Notes and photos on site, then the quote is written at home in the evening. Many reply to WhatsApp enquiries with a rough figure and send a formal quote later from a cheap invoicing app or a Word or Excel template.

- A trader who started a March 2026 forum thread said his method was "a mix of notes, photos, and doing quotes later at home, which isn't ideal". He found Jobber and Tradify "quite heavy" for a solo trade. A moderator described a builder friend who works only from his phone, answers WhatsApp enquiries with estimates, sends the formal quote from a small app, and finds the bigger apps "all way too complicated". His wife does the accounts. Source: [UK Business Forums thread, March to November 2026](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/what-apps-are-trades-using-for-quoting-jobs-now.430559/) (independent, first-hand, but see the warning below).
- Warning about that thread: eight of the posters are developers promoting their own quote or admin apps. A moderator asked whether it would "rate as the one with the most self promoted products ever" on the forum. Nobody in the thread mentions paying a person to write quotes.
- 54% of 408 UK tradespeople say customers ask them for a new quote on the spot for unrelated work. Source: [Simply Business survey, May to June 2026](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/trades/unpaid-tasks-costing-uk-tradespeople/) (vendor: an insurer's own survey, though it has no stake in quoting).

**What hurts, in numbers.**

**First-hand complaints.**

- "He works every single night after work on this stuff and often weekends too." "Working out areas and amounts of materials needed, getting prices off suppliers can take time." "Most people get 3 quotes, and we know we will NOT win most jobs we quote for." One poster described "3 hours on a pointless quote". Source: [Mumsnet thread](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/2443384-Why-do-tradesmen-take-so-long-to-quote) (independent, first-hand from partners of tradesmen, but **old: August 2015**).
- A joiner says he hates pricing and "usually just works out material costs and guesses the job time". Another works on about eight quotes a week and one win in four, which leaves about an hour a quote. Source: [UKWorkshop forum](https://ukworkshop.co.uk/threads/how-do-you-do-it-your-business.135355/page-2) (independent, but search extract only: the page refused the fetch and I do not have its date).
- A carpentry firm's review of an estimating service: "pricing up jobs became quite the time-consuming, out of hours hardship." Source: [Cost Estimator](https://costestimator.co.uk/?p=5874) (vendor testimonial).

**What has changed in 2026.** Small builders are already using AI for this. In the Federation of Master Builders survey for January to June 2026 (416 firms), 57% use AI more than a year ago and 22% do not use it at all. Among those who use it, 42% use it for "quotes, estimates and tenders". Source: [FMB State of Trade H1 2026](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-h1-2026-skills-rising-costs-and-ai-adoption.html) (independent trade body survey). That works out at roughly one small builder in three already using AI on quotes (inference: 78% users times 42%).

## Q2. Where is the real time cost: pricing or writing up?

**Pricing is the slow part.** Every first-hand source in Q1 that says where the time goes names the same things: measuring, working out quantities, getting supplier prices, guessing labour time. None names typing.

**The paid market that exists is for pricing, not writing.** Builders can already buy a priced estimate from trained estimators who work from drawings:

- £180 to £480 for a priced estimate for an extension, loft or refurb (typical £320). £60 to £160 per drawing for measuring only. Guide prices dated late 2025. Source: [Cost Estimator price guide](https://costestimator.co.uk/maximising-your-roi-how-estimator-services-can-help) (vendor, its own guide ranges).
- From £529 for a new-build estimate (aimed at self-builders). Source: [Build It Estimating Service](https://www.self-build.co.uk/build-it-estimating-service/) (primary for its own price).

The owner cannot offer this. He has no construction knowledge and no price data.

**Writing up is the small part.** More than a third of owners spend over 20 minutes on a quote (HeyBRB, above), which means most spend 20 minutes or less (inference). A Wickes guide puts it at about 45 minutes by hand and says an AI assistant cuts 20 to 30 minutes of drafting to two or three. Source: [Wickes trade guide, summer 2026](https://www.wickes.co.uk/trade/ai-tools-for-tradespeople-summer-26) (vendor, no survey behind it).

**Apps now sell the writing-up for very little.** The P3 report's answer to the "tools exist" objection was that the tools "are templates and you are writing". That is out of date. Several UK apps now turn a voice note into a written quote:

The P3 report set its own rule: "the service must sell an outcome no shipped product currently sells." For the writing-up part, that rule is no longer met.

**Speed counts against a 24-hour service.** Forum posters say "the money usually leaks between the site visit and the quote landing" and "you need to get the quote out before you've left the drive". Apps produce the quote on site in minutes. The planned service returns it the next day. (Same UK Business Forums thread, independent, though some of these posters sell apps.)

**So what is left for a writing service to sell?** (inference)

1. A person for contractors who will not use any app. The builder who finds apps "way too complicated" is real. But he had already solved it with WhatsApp, a cheap app and his wife.
2. Chasing. Posters say jobs are "lost in the silence after the quote". Following up unanswered quotes is work no drafting tool does. That is admin help, not writing.
3. Someone who answers for the result. That was the P3 report's own conclusion about what clients pay for.

None of these is "writing". All three are closer to what a virtual assistant sells by the hour.

## Q3. Willingness to pay

**Software.** 54% of small business owners spend less than £50 a month on all business software together (HeyBRB, vendor, 167 owners, mostly not trades). The mainstream trade apps cost £34 to £45 a user a month and the quote apps £10 to £30 (table above). When a developer asked a forum what trades would pay for an automated quote builder and suggested £20 to £30 a month, nobody named a price. A moderator replied that "there are already any number of quote generators" and that "Your biggest cost won't be the app, it will be marketing". Source: [UK Business Forums, June 2026](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/quick-question-for-uk-builders-trades-would-you-trust-an-automated-quote-builder-to-save-you-admin-time-and-what-is-it-actually-worth.430929/) (independent).

**Outsourced admin.** Price lists exist at the £300 to £500 level. Proof that small trades buy at that level does not.

Bookkeeping at £150 to £950 a month and call answering at £49 to £299 a month were already in the P3 report. I did not re-check them.

**What this means for £395** (inference). £395 buys 10 to 15 hours of a general assistant who does quotes and invoices and the diary and the chasing. The planned service covers quotes only. Rough value check:

- A solo trader doing 3 quotes a week at 30 minutes each saves about 6.5 hours a month. £395 is then about £61 for each hour saved. Poor value against a £27 an hour assistant or a £15 app.
- A busy small firm doing 8 quotes a week at 30 minutes each saves about 17 hours a month. £395 is then about £23 an hour. That is in line with assistant rates, so it can make sense, but only at that volume.

The contractor still has to record every detail and price, and check what comes back. The true saving is smaller than these figures.

**Direct answer.** Is there evidence of small trades paying £300 to £500 a month for any admin service? **Not found.** I found sellers listing plans at that price. I found no survey of what trades spend on outsourced admin and no named small trade firm paying it.

The P3 claim that "one extra £5k job won pays for a year" also has no evidence behind it. I found nothing showing that a better-written quote wins more jobs.

## Q4. Tender responses (PQQs and bids)

This is a different job with a different buyer.

- **Who writes them.** Firms chasing council, housing association, school and main-contractor work. They need accreditations, policies, case studies and scored written answers. Constructionline says over 50,000 suppliers are registered with it. CHAS says more than 32,500 contractors. Sources: [Constructionline](https://www.constructionline.co.uk/?p=46147) (vendor, own claim, undated, search extract) and [CHAS](https://www.chas.co.uk/clients/) (vendor, own claim, search extract). The two lists overlap and include large firms. How many are firms of 1 to 10 people: **not found**.
- **What bid writers cost.** Contract bid writers are advertised at £400 to £500 a day. The median for England was £417 a day in the six months to August 2025. Sources: [ITJobsWatch](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/contracts/england/bid%20writer.do) (independent, job advert data, 2025) and [Hays advert](https://www.hays.co.uk/job-detail/bid-writer-bristol_4755164) (independent). One estimating firm lists bid writing at £350 to £650 a day: [Cost Estimator](https://costestimator.co.uk/maximising-your-roi-how-estimator-services-can-help) (vendor). A bid consultancy says it works on fixed fees per bid but publishes no prices: [BidWriters](https://bidwriters.pages.dev/resources/bid-writing-costs/) (vendor).
- **Unverified.** A search extract gave £200 to £800 per pre-qualification form and £500 to £1,500 per small-works tender. I opened the pages and could not find those figures. Do not rely on them.
- **Effort.** One survey of 300 firms said a tender takes 35 hours and firms submit about six a year. Source: [Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/businesses-waste-28-days-a-year-on-tenders) (vendor: a tender-writing firm ran it, and it is **undated and probably old**).

What follows (inference). Tender writing is a real paid market. It is project work, not a monthly fee. It is bought by firms larger than the ones in the plan. Established consultancies sell it at day rates. A single tender can take a day or more, and far more on the one old survey. The offer in the P3 report promises "a quote or tender response" on an unlimited £395 plan with about 4 hours of work a client a month. One tender would use that up. Tenders should not be in the opening offer.

## Q5. Who would be the best-fit buyer, if anyone?

This is inference. No survey I found splits quote volume or admin spending by trade and firm size.

**Poor fit:**
- Sole traders. Few quotes a week, software spend under £50 a month, quotes sent by WhatsApp, a partner often does the paperwork. The sums in Q3 do not work at £395.
- Builders pricing extensions and lofts. Their pain is the five-hour priced estimate from drawings. That needs an estimator.
- Firms of about 10 or more with an office person or estimator. Someone already does it.

**Least bad fit:**
- A limited company of about 3 to 10 people.
- The owner still prices every job himself and is on site all day.
- High quote volume, about 6 to 10 or more a week.
- Homeowner jobs of a few thousand pounds, where the price comes from a quick survey and the owner's own rates: roofing, driveways and landscaping, heating and boiler installs, electrical rewires and charger installs, bathrooms and kitchens.
- Does not use a quoting app and does not want to.

That last condition is the weak point. It shrinks the pool with every month that AI use spreads (FMB figures in Q1).

## Q6. How many firms, and how many are limited companies?

**Official counts (primary).**

- All UK construction businesses, including one-person businesses too small to register for VAT or PAYE: about 846,000 at the start of 2026. Source: [Business population estimates 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2026/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2026-statistical-release).
- Construction firms registered for VAT or PAYE, March 2025: 384,830. By number of people working in them: 333,590 with 0 to 4; 31,120 with 5 to 9; 12,300 with 10 to 19; 5,430 with 20 to 49. Source: [ONS table AH1857](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/adhocs/3464enterprisesbyemploymentsizeandindustryukmarch2025) (I downloaded the table and read the figures directly).
- Legal form of registered construction firms, March 2024: 308,715 companies (81%), 58,760 sole proprietors (15%), 12,070 partnerships (3%), out of 380,195. In the 0 to 9 band, 80% are companies. In the 10 to 49 band, 97% are companies. Source: [ONS table AH1571](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/adhocs/2425ukenterprisesbylegalstatusemploymentsizebandanduksic2007broadindustrygroup) (downloaded and read directly).

**What this means** (inference).

- About 460,000 construction businesses (846,000 less 385,000) are not registered for VAT or PAYE. Nearly all are one-person sole traders. They are the wrong buyer and, on the plan's own legal reading, the wrong people to cold email.
- The best-fit size band holds roughly 30,000 to 45,000 firms across the UK (the 5 to 9 band, plus some of the 10 to 19 band). This covers every kind of construction firm, including ones that only subcontract and never quote a homeowner. The number that match the full Q5 description is smaller. How much smaller: **not found**.
- In that band, about 85% to 95% are probably limited companies. So a list of small registered trade firms will be mostly companies.
- A warning on the 0 to 4 band. It looks huge (333,590), but it includes many one-person limited companies that work for one main contractor and never write quotes.
- Counts by single trade (electricians, plumbers, roofers) exist in ONS data. I did not retrieve them.

The rule that cold email is lawful to companies but not to sole traders comes from the P3 report. The regulator's site is blocked here, so I did not re-check it.

**Market mood.** Small builders' new enquiries fell in the first half of 2026 (net balance minus 10%, down from plus 18%) and workloads slipped (minus 4%, down from plus 22%). Source: FMB survey above (independent). Fewer enquiries means fewer quotes to write and tighter budgets. It also means each quote matters more. I cannot say which effect wins.

## What I could not find

- Any UK service selling done-for-you quote writing to trades on a monthly fee. One search aimed at exactly this returned only software.
- Any small trade firm shown to be paying £300 to £500 a month for quote writing or general admin help.
- Any survey of what small trades spend on outsourced admin.
- A reliable figure for how the hours on one quote split between visiting, pricing and typing. The split in this report rests on forum posts and marketing guides.
- Any evidence that a better-written quote wins more jobs.
- The trade forums named in the task. Screwfix Community and ElectriciansForums did not appear in search results, and a direct fetch was refused. No Reddit threads came back either. First-hand evidence here comes from UK Business Forums, UKWorkshop and one 2015 Mumsnet thread. This is the biggest gap in the report.
- How many firms of 1 to 10 people bid for public or commercial tenders, and published per-bid prices from bid consultancies.
- User numbers for the voice-note quote apps.
- Firm counts by trade or by county.
- Pages that refused the fetch: Checkatrade, UKWorkshop, ElectriciansForums, Bdaily. I did not retry them.

## Verdict

**DEMAND_WEAK_OR_UNPROVEN**

Decisive reason: the evening admin pain is real, but the part of a quote that costs the time is pricing, which this service cannot do. The part it can do, the write-up, takes 20 to 45 minutes and is now sold by apps for £10 to £45 a month. I found no one paying a person £300 to £500 a month for it.

This is not DEMAND_ABSENT. Some contractors will not use apps. Admin help for trades is on sale at £135 to £495 a month. Builders do pay £180 to £480 for a single estimate when pricing is included. I could not reach the main trade forums. The honest position is that nobody has shown this demand exists at this price.

**What this changes in the standing recommendation:**

1. Keep the 14-day conversation test. Desk research cannot settle this question. Only contractors can.
2. Change what the test asks. Four questions: how many quotes do you send a week; which takes longer, pricing or writing up; what do you use now (app, AI, partner, WhatsApp); what do you pay for any admin help.
3. Add a demand gate to the conversation gate. Reaching 5 conversations shows the owner can reach people. It does not show they want this. Before selling, at least 3 contractors should say that writing up (not pricing) is the pain, that they send 6 or more quotes a week, and that no app or family member has already fixed it.
4. Do not treat £395 as settled. The comparisons point to £135 to £255 a month for what a solo trade would buy. £395 only adds up for a firm sending about 8 or more quotes a week.
5. Aim at limited companies of about 3 to 10 people with high quote volume. Leave sole traders out.
6. Take tender responses out of the opening offer. Different buyer, day-rate pricing, and one tender would use up a month's planned hours.
7. Stop saying the tools "are templates and you are writing", and stop saying one extra job won pays for a year. The first is no longer true. The second has no evidence.
8. If the conversations show the real need is getting quotes out the same day and chasing customers for an answer, say so plainly at the gate. That is admin help by the hour, with different prices and competitors, and it would need its own decision.

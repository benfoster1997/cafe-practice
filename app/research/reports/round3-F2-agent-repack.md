# F2 close-out: Agent-first compliance repack (letting agents, £79/office/month)

## 1. The anchors verify — and one is worse than Stage 2b assumed

**Kamma** does everything the concept would sell, at agent scale: licensing determination across 500+ schemes, instant per-address licence checks with agency-branded auditable reports, portfolio-wide 24/7 monitoring with proactive alerts, CRM-integrated hub ([kammadata.com](https://www.kammadata.com/how-we-can-help/letting-agents/), [kammadata.com/](https://www.kammadata.com/)). The exact ~£0.50/property/month figure could NOT be publicly verified (sales-gated — see gaps), but the structural anchor is worse than a low price: Kamma's licence-application service is **charged to the landlord, and the agent adds a referral fee — Kamma pays agents a "low-effort, recurring revenue stream"** ([kammadata.com/how-we-can-help/letting-agents/](https://www.kammadata.com/how-we-can-help/letting-agents/)). F2 asks agents to pay £79/month for a category where the incumbent's model gives them revenue. That is a price inversion, not just undercutting.

**Kamma also owns the trust channel**: it is a **Propertymark Industry Supplier** for licensing/compliance automation ([propertymark.co.uk industry suppliers](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/membership/industry-suppliers.html)); Smart Compliance holds the AML slot ([smartcompliance.co.uk](https://smartcompliance.co.uk/celebrating-another-year-of-partnership-with-propertymark/)). The one association channel a no-access founder would need is occupied.

**Goodlord** bundles compliance into its tenancy platform — automated checks, alerts, legislative-change workflows, Section 13 notice support, RRA guidance content ([goodlord.com/platform](https://www.goodlord.com/platform), [blog.goodlord.co](https://blog.goodlord.co/letting-agents-prevent-fines-ensure-compliance)). Pricing is not public (per-tenancy model, sales-gated), but compliance is sold as a feature of the workflow agents already run, not a separate line item — exactly the "compliance-as-a-service" positioning Stage 2b described.

**CRMs are shipping RRA compliance natively.** Alto has a "Lettings Compliance Agent" tracking certificate expiries with automatic landlord/tenant/contractor updates and markets itself on built-in compliance ([altosoftware.co.uk comparison](https://www.altosoftware.co.uk/blog/the-uks-best-property-management-software-for-2026-alto-vs-reapit-vs-street-compared/)); Reapit rolled out RRA features covering rent reviews, rent-bidding prevention, pets, and revised eviction processes, and signed a July 2026 partner deal that "adds revenue stream and cuts Renters Rights Act work" ([Letting Agent Today](https://www.lettingagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/07/reapit-deal-adds-revenue-stream-and-cuts-renters-rights-act-work/) — headline only, article egress-blocked); Street automates certificate tracking in its lettings module ([lanten.ai comparison](https://lanten.ai/street-vs-alto-vs-reapit)). An agent's compliance dashboard is becoming a CRM feature, not a product.

## 2. The agent side is already swarmed (lesson 1 replays exactly)

The landlord-facing RRA wave has visibly pivoted to agents. Found without trying hard, all 2025–26:

- **Bilans Solutions "Compliance Tracker"** — Reapit AgencyCloud-integrated, portfolio RAG dashboard, tracks the 31 May 2026 Information Sheet duty, bulk distribution, auto-pulls gas/EICR/EPC/HMO data ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/compliance-tool-launched-to-help-agents-prepare-for-renters-rights-act/), [PropertyWire](https://www.propertywire.com/news/compliance-tracker-launched-for-renters-rights-act/))
- **Docubien** — **free** Form 3A / Section 8 workflow tool for letting agents, launched Aug 2026 with syndicated press-release spam across 5+ sites ([openpr.com](https://www.openpr.com/news/4593903/free-online-tool-helps-letting-agents-handle-form-3a-and-section-8)) — the AI-built-swarm signature from the party-wall and Martyn's Law kills
- **Ploxit** — Information Sheet service with delivery/open/acknowledgment evidence "ready for councils and tribunals," plus an SEO page ranking for "best Renters' Rights Act software 2026" ([ploxit.com](https://ploxit.com/blog/best-software-renters-rights-act-2026))
- **Rentalize** — full agent+landlord RRA suite claiming PRS Database submission, Section 8 workflows, Information Sheet service, pet requests, Decent Homes audits, and Awaab's Law tracking ([rentalize.com](https://rentalize.com/renters-rights-act-compliance-software/))
- Plus SEO microsites already squatting future wedges: awaabslawguide.co.uk, hazardclock.co.uk, locastica.com, augustapp.com, landlord-os.com, landlordlogbook.com

The trade-press note that "one in three letting agents has taken no steps to prepare" is not an opportunity signal for an unknown vendor — the untooled third are laggards who will wait for their CRM or Propertymark to tell them what to buy, and every vendor above is cold-emailing the same 24k branches right now.

## 3. The buyer and the cold-start channel

~24,000 estate/letting agency branches registered with HMRC for AML (H1 2025) ([Letting Agent Today](https://www.lettingagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/09/around-5-of-agency-branches-still-not-aml-compliant/)); surveyed agencies average ~340 managed properties and 14 staff ([Goodlord State of the Lettings Industry 2025](https://www.goodlord.com/state-of-the-lettings-industry-report-2025)). Most are limited companies, so PECR permits cold email — the register is enumerable. But enumerability is the only box that ticks. The purchase channel for agent tooling is Propertymark endorsement, CRM marketplaces/integrations, and trade press (Letting Agent Today, Property Industry Eye, The Negotiator) — all currently saturated with RRA vendor launches. An unknown solo founder with zero lettings credentials, no CRM integration (Reapit/Alto APIs gate access to established partners), and no Propertymark slot is cold-emailing buyers who are (a) drowning in identical RRA pitches and (b) personally exposed to £40k civil penalties — which per the liability-inversion lesson pushes them TOWARD the endorsed incumbent, not toward the cheapest unknown.

## 4. Wedge audit — nothing un-served survives

- **PRS Database bulk registration (late-2026 rollout)**: the government portal itself ships bulk CSV/Excel upload and an API (docs Q2 2026, early access for portfolios from summer 2026) ([locastica.com guide](https://locastica.com/guides/prs-database-registration), [augustapp.com](https://www.augustapp.com/blog/private-rented-sector-database)). Fails the standing filter "no free government tool doing the same job." Goodlord has agent-facing PRS Database content and platforms are planning API integration ([blog.goodlord.co](https://blog.goodlord.co/understanding-the-private-rented-sector-database)); Rentalize already claims submission support.
- **Awaab's Law in the PRS (earliest 2027)**: Fixflo — the incumbent repairs/maintenance platform agents already use — is explicitly positioned for hazard-response tracking ([lettingshub.co.uk infosheet](https://lettingshub.co.uk/letting-agents/blog/awaabs-law-and-the-private-rented-sector-infosheet-name/)); Rentalize claims tracking today; two SEO microsites are already parked on the term. It is also 14+ months from enforceable duty — no revenue path inside the owner's horizon, and pre-enforcement is exactly where the Martyn's Law clone appeared 8 months early.
- **Information Sheet duty (31 May 2026)**: already served by Bilans (Reapit) and Ploxit, including evidence trails.
- **Form 3A/Section 8**: served free by Docubien.

Every candidate wedge is either a free government feature, an incumbent roadmap item, or already occupied by 2+ swarm entrants.

## Conclusion

F2 was a fallback that only worked with an access edge; the owner has none. On the merits it now fails four standing filters independently: swarmed category, occupied association channel, free government tool on the flagship wedge, and an incumbent whose pricing model pays the buyer. Kill is clean.

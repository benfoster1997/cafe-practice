## P2: PARTNER/CHANNEL-LED — honest assessment

### Bottom line first

The path is not dead, but **the arithmetic the thesis rests on is wrong**, and the corrected arithmetic changes what the path is. "One accountant or association with 80 clients ≈ the whole customer target" fails on two independent grounds (channel margin and channel price compression, both below). The version that survives is narrower and less romantic: *sell TO a small number of intermediaries who pay you several hundred pounds a month because the tool saves them work across their client base* — and, in one specific sub-case (franchisors with mandate power), the original "one partner = whole target" claim does hold.

Note also that your own Stage 1 report already flagged the thesis as unmeasured: *"No independent data anywhere on what share of UK SMB software purchases are accountant-influenced. Every source asserting this is a vendor selling a referral programme. Opportunity #2 rests on structural logic (one practice = 80 clients), not measurement"* (`/home/user/cafe-practice/app/research/reports/stage1-distribution.md`, line 377). This report is the test that line asked for. It does not survive intact.

---

### 1. The arithmetic error at the heart of the thesis

**Problem A — partners take margin.** Published SaaS channel norms: referral partners 10–15% of revenue; VARs/MSPs who own the customer relationship 20–35%; **white-label resellers buy at 40–60% below list** ([channels-as-a-strategy.com](https://channels-as-a-strategy.com/saas-reseller-commission-structure/), [refgrow.com](https://refgrow.com/referral-fees)). So £99 list through a white-label accountant nets you £40–60. Your 24–30 customers becomes 40–58 end-users. Through a referral partner at 25% it becomes ~32–40.

**Problem B — the accountant channel prices per-client an order of magnitude below £79–99.** Dext, the most established practice-channel app in the UK, is reported at **£391/month for 50 clients, £894.15 for 150, £1,541.10 for 300** ex VAT as of June 2026 — i.e. **≈£7.82 per client per month at 50 clients, falling with scale** ([receiptflow.co](https://receiptflow.co/blog/dext-pricing-uk-accountants-2026), competitor blog, secondary — page itself egress-blocked, treat as needing browser verification). A no-track-record solo vendor cannot set a per-client price ~10× the category leader's. If you price per end-client through an accountant, realistic is £8–25, not £79–99. £2,335/month then needs **100–300 end-client seats**, spread over several practices.

**The one place the numbers get better, not worse:** the same Dext figure proves a 50-client practice will pay **~£391/month** for a workflow tool. That reframes the target as **6–15 practices at £150–400/month** rather than 24–30 businesses at £79–99. That is a genuinely easier count — but it is *selling to accountants as end-buyers*, not selling *through* them. Different ICP, same direct-sales problem.

**Net correction: you need roughly 4–12 productive partners, not 1–3.**

### 2. What the channel evidence actually says (and it is unkind)

- **Activation.** "Most B2B SaaS partner programs land between 15–25% activation rate, meaning fewer than 1 in 4 signed partners ever sends a single qualified referral"; "the bottom 50% of partners typically generate almost nothing, and the top 20% produce 70–80% of all partner-sourced revenue" ([u4ia.ai](https://u4ia.ai/blog/partner-activation-rate-benchmarks), [zinfi.com](https://www.zinfi.com/glossary/what-is-partner-activation-rate/)). To get 4–12 productive partners at programmatic activation rates you would need **20–60 signed partners**. That number is not reachable by a solo founder on evenings and weekends.
- **Time lag.** "Partner-sourced revenue typically takes 9 to 18 months to show up at scale. Companies that treat the program as a six-month experiment kill it before it produces" ([aexus.com](https://aexus.com/how-long-is-the-average-b2b-software-sales-cycle/) / channel-benchmark sources). Median B2B SaaS sales cycle is ~84 days; mid-market 3–6 months. Partner-led is a **two-stage sale** — you sell the partner (3–6 months), then wait on a second cycle you do not control. Inside a 24-month clock, the lag eats a third of the runway before revenue starts compounding.
- **The documented failure mode is exactly yours.** "Most founders send emails to their existing contacts, get two or three early conversations, then momentum dies because there is no systematic process for identifying new candidates — this is where most founders stall" ([scayul.com](https://scayul.com/blog/the-partner-led-growth-playbook-step-by-step-for-saas-founders)). You have no existing contacts, so you start past the point where most founders stall.
- **Absence of evidence, stated honestly.** Across searches of Indie Hackers, Starter Story and the micro-SaaS case-study ecosystem I could not find **a single documented solo/micro-SaaS founder who reached meaningful MRR primarily through a partner channel with verifiable numbers**. The documented indie successes cite direct channels (content, communities, audience, marketplaces, cold outreach). This is not proof of impossibility — the ecosystem is also polluted with AI-generated content marketing, per your own Stage 1 warning — but the burden of proof for "solo founders succeed via partners" is **not met by available evidence**.

### 3. The accountant channel specifically

- **The channel is real but saturated at the attention layer.** App fatigue in accounting is documented: practitioners are "inundated with choices" and spend more time researching and testing apps than using them ([corpayone.co.uk](https://www.corpayone.co.uk/post/app-fatigue-in-accounting-navigating-the-abundance-of-fintech-solutions)); AccountingWEB reports practices battling software integration frustration. A 2025 QuickBooks survey (via secondary reporting) puts accountants at an average of **eight digital tools**. Funded vendors (Dext, ApprovalMax, Fathom, Ignition) run partner programmes with staffed partner teams, MDF and co-marketing. You are competing for a fully-allocated resource: the practice owner's attention.
- **What practices actually do.** The dominant real-world model is *the practice is the buyer* — it buys at practice pricing, bundles the cost into its fees, and the client never sees a vendor invoice (Dext, ApprovalMax "partner programme … generate additional revenue" [approvalmax.com/partners](https://www.approvalmax.com/partners)). White-label at micro scale exists but is mostly generic tooling (client portals, CT600 filing) sold *to* the practice ([tinytax.co.uk](https://tinytax.co.uk/guides/white-label-ct600-software), [accountingweb.co.uk](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/five-benefits-of-white-label-software-for-accountants)). I found **no evidence of small UK practices acting as active salesforces for third-party software to their clients** — they recommend and they bundle; they do not sell.
- **The liability posture actively works against you.** Professional-body guidance is that when introducing a client to a third-party provider a member should **exclude liability for that provider and have the client contract separately** (ACCA/ICAEW professional-liability guidance, e.g. [ICAEW TECH 09/15](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/managing-professional-liability)). The risk-minimising behaviour the profession is *taught* is precisely the behaviour that destroys the channel's value: a bare introduction with a disclaimer, no push, no bundling. This is your mechanism (c), liability inversion, reappearing one level up.

### 4. Trade associations

- **The mechanism exists and is formalised.** UK trade bodies run "affinity partner" / "recognised supplier" schemes: partners are "companies that associations assess and appoint against a set of criteria"; benefits must be exclusive — "ensuring that benefit cannot be achieved anywhere else within the sector" ([memberwise.org.uk](https://memberwise.org.uk/directory/wpbdp_category/discounts-affinity-schemes/), [parliament-hill.co.uk](https://www.parliament-hill.co.uk/solutions/professional-and-trade-associations), [bsia.co.uk](https://www.bsia.co.uk/affinity-benefits/)). Some charge the *supplier* to participate — FORS charges "an annual subscription fee for becoming a FORS Associate based on the total annual revenue of the organisation" ([fors-online.org.uk](https://www.fors-online.org.uk/cms/suppliers/)).
- **Two things follow.** (i) The exclusivity clause is one of the very few defensible moats available to someone with no brand and no capital — a clone cannot copy a signed exclusive endorsement. (ii) Selection is a **vetting process run by a committee against criteria**, on association timescales (board/council approval cycles are typically quarterly). An unknown solo vendor with no trading history, no PI cover, no reference customers and a bus-factor of one fails standard vetting on paper before anyone reads the pitch. Existing endorsed vendors are typically established firms — Claromentis is an "official BFA supplier"; ICAEW runs a formal paid **ICAEW Accredited Technology** evaluation ([icaew.com](https://www.icaew.com/membership/offers-discounts-and-services/icaew-accredited-technology)).
- **Verdict on associations: do not lead with these.** Realistic timeline from cold approach to endorsement is 9–18 months minimum, gated on credentials you do not yet have. They are a *year-two* asset, not a first-90-days move.

### 5. Franchisors — the one variant where the original thesis actually holds

This is the strongest finding in this report and it was not in the original thesis framing.

- **Size and enumerability:** the BFA/NatWest National Franchise Survey (2024) counts **1,009 UK franchise systems and 50,421 franchise units** ([reported via Franchise Direct](https://www.franchisedirect.co.uk/information/the-results-are-out-the-bfa-and-nic-services-group-reveals-the-results-of-the-2024-national-franchise-survey)). ~50 units per system average. The full population is publicly listed (BFA directory, franchise portals) — a completely enumerable target list of ~1,000 organisations, most of them small companies whose MD will take a call.
- **Mandate power is the thing accountants do not have.** Franchisors set the operations manual, run "approved / preferred / exclusive" supplier tiers, and franchisees "benefit from the franchisor's established technology stack … and supplier relationships" ([franchise.org](https://www.franchise.org/franchise-information/operations-and-training/how-to-select-a-franchise-supplier), [thebfa.org](https://www.thebfa.org/supplier-membership/)). **A franchisor can require adoption; an accountant can only recommend.** This structurally solves the partner-activation problem — the 15–25% activation statistic is about partners who must *choose* to sell. A mandated rollout does not depend on the partner selling anything.
- **The arithmetic works with ONE deal.** A 40-unit network at £29/unit/month = **£1,160/month**. At £49/unit = £1,960/month. Even after a 25% franchisor override, one 40-unit network is £870–1,470/month — 37–63% of target from a single signature. Two such deals clear the target. *This* is the only version of "one partner ≈ the whole customer target" that survives scrutiny.
- **Counter-evidence, and it is real.** Franchise supplier selection involves field testing: "before selecting a product or system for introduction throughout a franchise network, the supplier and franchise executive should conduct field tests" ([franchise.org](https://www.franchise.org/franchise-information/operations-and-training/how-to-select-a-franchise-supplier)). Expect a 3–6 franchisee pilot before any network rollout, and a 6–12 month decision cycle. Many systems already run all-in-one franchise-management platforms (ServiceTitan, FranchiseSoft, Cerca, Claromentis, serviceminder). And franchisors will likely want a royalty/rebate on the software.

### 6. The trust recursion — and why white-label is backwards

Your framing asks whether white-label mitigates the trust problem. **It inverts it.** Rank the arrangements by how much output they produce and you get the exact reverse ranking of how likely a partner is to agree:

| Arrangement | Output for you | Partner's risk | Likelihood of a yes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral / listing | Near zero (activation 15–25%) | ~None | High |
| Bundled resale | Real | Moderate — fee-bearing | Medium |
| White-label under partner's brand | Highest | Total — their brand carries your bugs | Lowest |

Under white-label the partner absorbs 100% of the reputational and (with clients) contractual risk for an unknown solo vendor with no PI cover, no security documentation, no company history and a bus-factor of one. Under UK GDPR they become controller and you processor: they need a DPA and must do supplier due diligence. **This is a gate, and it is cheap to pass** — incorporate, buy PI + cyber (~£300–600/yr for a micro-SaaS), write a one-page DPA, publish a data-export and continuity guarantee, offer a 3-user free pilot with a written exit. Do all of it *before* the first partner call, because the first serious partner will ask and "I'll look into it" ends the conversation.

The genuine mitigations, in order of power: **(1) pilot with a named subset the partner chooses** (converts a bet into an experiment); **(2) the partner pays nothing until the pilot works**; **(3) category exclusivity offered to them** (turns their risk into their asset); **(4) revenue share** — which is the *weakest* lever, because a partner risking client relationships is not motivated by 20% of £99.

### 7. The constraint nobody has costed: partner selling is daytime, synchronous work

Accountancy practices, franchisors and associations operate 09:00–17:00. You have evenings and weekends. **You cannot email-only your way to a channel deal** — every documented partner motion runs through calls and meetings. Your Stage 1 benchmark is 25–45 dials per meeting. 20–40 partner conversations therefore implies **500–1,800 dials**, all inside business hours.

The mitigations are real but partial: (a) PECR makes cold email to these targets **legal** (below), so email does the top of funnel and calls only happen with people who already replied; (b) owner-managers of small franchisors and 3–15-staff practices will genuinely take a 17:30 or 18:00 call, unlike a customer-facing SME; (c) lunch-hour blocks give ~5 hours/week of callable time. Realistic throughput: **1–3 partner conversations per week at peak**, not 10.

### 8. The one clean, verified win for P2

**Cold outreach to partners is legal; cold outreach to your end-buyers largely is not.** The ICO is unambiguous: limited companies and LLPs are **corporate subscribers**, and "you don't need consent under PECR to send them marketing emails or texts"; sole traders and ordinary partnerships are individual subscribers and require consent ([ICO, B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)). Your Stage 1 finding was that **63% of the UK business population cannot lawfully be cold-emailed**. Accountancy practices, franchisors, consultancies and associations are almost entirely limited companies, LLPs or companies limited by guarantee. **P2 restores the cheapest channel to legality.** (UK GDPR still applies to a named individual's business email — use legitimate interests, identify yourself, honour objections.)

Second-order win: a partner is also the **domain access you don't have**. All eight killed concepts were sourced by keyword search — which, as your own diagnosis says, is the swarm's own method. One engaged partner converts "guess a niche from Google" into "build for a workflow I watched". That alone may be worth the path even if the channel never produces revenue.

### 9. Which kill mechanisms P2 defeats

| Mechanism | Effect of P2 |
|---|---|
| (a) SWARM | **Partially defeated.** A signed exclusive/reseller relationship is not keyword-discoverable and not weekend-clonable. But partner *attention* is itself swarmed (app fatigue). |
| (b) STATE PRICES AT ZERO | **Unchanged.** If you point P2 at a compliance niche, the free government portal still kills it. P2 must carry a **non-compliance operational workflow**. This is a hard constraint on what P2 can be applied to. |
| (c) LIABILITY INVERSION | **Amplified.** Professional-body guidance teaches partners to disclaim third-party recommendations. The trust bar is higher, not lower — as you suspected. |
| (d) DISTRIBUTION | **Materially improved.** Legal cold email, ~1,000-item enumerable lists, 10–40× value per conversation. |

Two improve, one is conditional, one gets worse. That is a real but modest net gain — not a step change.

## Verdict

CONDITIONAL — and only in one specific form. DEAD as stated ("one accountant/association with 80 clients ≈ the whole target"): channel margin (partners take 10–60%) plus channel price compression (the UK accountant-channel per-client benchmark is ~£8/client/mo, not £79–99) plus 15–25% partner activation rates mean you need 4–12 productive partners from 20–60 relationships, not 1–3. CONDITIONAL and worth pursuing in the franchisor-mandate form: a franchisor can REQUIRE adoption where an accountant can only recommend, which is the single mechanism that removes the partner-activation problem, and one 40-unit network at £29–49/unit is £1,160–1,960/month — 40–60% of target from one signature. The decisive reason it is not DEAD: P2 is the only path researched so far that makes cold outreach LEGAL (partners are PECR corporate subscribers; ~63% of end-buyers are not) while simultaneously supplying the industry access the founder does not have and that killed concepts 1–8.

## Odds

Overall: ~7% (honest range 4–12%), against an accepted venture base rate of 5–10%. P2 is a modest improvement, not a step change — it buys distribution legality and higher revenue-per-customer at the cost of a 9–18 month partner revenue lag inside a 24-month clock.

By variant:
- Franchisor mandate (small service-sector systems, 20–200 units): 8–12%
- Sell TO practices as end-buyers at £150–400/mo: 5–8%
- Accountant resells to their clients (the original thesis): 2–4%
- Trade-association endorsement: 2–4%
- Generic "go find partners" with no variant chosen: 4–6%

Chain behind the 7%: P(founder actually executes ~1,200 approaches + 8–15 partner conversations over 12 months, evenings/weekends only, with no network) ≈ 40–50%; P(≥1 partner agrees to a real commercial pilot | that volume) ≈ 50–60%; P(that partner is productive rather than dormant | signed) ≈ 30–40% (programmatic benchmark 15–25%, uplift for a hand-worked single partner); P(scale from partner 1 to £2,335/mo within remaining runway) ≈ 30–40%. Multiplied with overlap allowance for running 2–3 partner tracks in parallel: 6–9%.

Two adjustments the founder should hold onto:
- If ONE franchisor or practice gives you named end-users to talk to inside 90 days, odds roughly double to 15–20%. That single event is worth more than all eight prior concept cycles because it converts an unmeasured structural claim into a measured one.
- If 90 days pass with >10 partner conversations and zero offers of end-user access, odds fall below 3% and the path should be abandoned — that outcome confirms the trust recursion and no product change fixes it.

Timeline realism: first productive partner revenue 5–9 months; £2,335/month at 14–22 months if it works. It fits inside 24 months with essentially no slack for a failed first partner, which is why a second partner track must start by month 4.

## First 90 days

PRE-WORK (week 0, ~£400–700, do this BEFORE the first call — the first serious partner will ask and "I'll look into it" ends the conversation):
- Incorporate a limited company. Get PI + cyber insurance quotes (~£300–600/yr for micro-SaaS).
- Write a one-page UK GDPR data-processing agreement (you are processor, partner is controller), a data-export guarantee, and a written business-continuity clause (what happens if you stop trading — escrow or full data export on 30 days' notice).
- Build a compliant cold-email setup: two domains, warmed mailboxes, identity + one-click opt-out in every mail (PECR requires you identify yourself and offer opt-out even to corporate subscribers).

DAYS 1–14 — PICK ONE PARTNER TYPE AND BUILD THE LIST. Recommendation: UK franchisors, service-sector, 20–200 units, NOT already on an all-in-one franchise-management platform (exclude anyone visibly running ServiceTitan / FranchiseSoft / Cerca / serviceminder / Claromentis). Source from the BFA directory and franchise portals. Target list: 200 named systems with the MD/operations director named. Do NOT lead with trade associations (9–18 month committee cycles, you fail vetting on paper today) and do NOT lead with an accountant-resells-to-clients pitch (the arithmetic doesn't work).

DAYS 15–45 — 300+ APPROACHES, ZERO BUILDING. Legal cold email to all 200 (companies = corporate subscribers), plus 100 second-touches, plus LinkedIn. Expect 4–8 positive replies. Book calls at 08:00, lunch, or 17:30 — small-franchisor MDs will take an evening call in a way end-customers will not. Absolute rule: write no code this month. The question on every call is NOT "would you buy X" but: "What do your franchisees do manually every week that YOU end up chasing them about? What do they currently pay for, and what does head office pay for? Where does brand consistency actually break?" Take verbatim notes. You are buying the industry access you lack, not selling.

DAYS 46–70 — CONVERGE ON ONE NON-COMPLIANCE WORKFLOW. From 8–15 conversations, find the one recurring operational chore that (a) head office already chases, (b) is NOT a statutory compliance duty (mechanism (b): government ships free portals — P2 does not fix that), (c) no incumbent platform already covers for that vertical. Then get ONE franchisor to answer the qualifying question in writing: "If this existed and three of your franchisees used it for a month and liked it, would you put it in the operations manual, and at what per-unit monthly price?" A refusal to answer that question IS the answer. Simultaneously start a second parallel cohort of 100 new targets so a dead partner #1 doesn't restart the clock.

DAYS 71–90 — NARROW BUILD FOR THREE NAMED UNITS, AND CHARGE. Build only what those three franchisees do weekly. Charge from day one, even £19/unit — free pilots do not test willingness to pay and free pilots are what dormant partners agree to. Offer the franchisor two things that cost you nothing and are worth a lot to them: category exclusivity in their sector for 12 months, and a 20–25% override on network revenue. Deliver the vendor-credibility pack (DPA, insurance certificate, continuity clause) unasked.

DAY-90 GATES (pre-commit to these now):
- GO if: ≥1 franchisor has given you NAMED franchisees to speak to AND stated a per-unit price they would accept. That is the measured proof the entire path rests on.
- CONTINUE-WITH-CAUTION if: ≥3 serious conversations but no named end-users yet — extend 60 days, keep both cohorts running.
- ABANDON P2 if: fewer than 300 approaches made (execution failure — the problem is capacity, not the path), OR >10 partner conversations with zero offers of end-user access (trust recursion confirmed; no product change fixes it).

METRICS TO LOG WEEKLY: approaches sent, positive replies, conversations held, serious prospects, named end-users obtained, £ collected. Only the last two matter.

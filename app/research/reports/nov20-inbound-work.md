# Is there a way to be paid for work done with Claude where the buyer comes to you? (20 November 2026)

Scope: this is not a search for new ideas. It checks one thing. The standing pick needs cold contact with strangers. Is there a route where the buyer comes to you instead, and does any such route beat the pick on speed or on expected value? The pick and its numbers are taken from `app/STATUS.md` (top section), `app/research/STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3 and `app/research/reports/strategy-P3-service-first.md` (sections 3, 5h, 6 and "First 90 days").

How to read this report:

- Source labels: **primary** (a platform's own page, the law itself, official statistics), **independent** (press, academic work, first-hand posts), **vendor_or_seo** (marketing blogs and sites that sell something: direction only), **inference** (my own reasoning).
- "Search extract only" means the page itself was blocked and I am relying on the short extract the search returned.
- Most platform figures are in US dollars. I convert at about 1.30 dollars to the pound. That rate is my rough figure and was not looked up (inference).
- All 14 permitted web searches were used. Several platform pages were blocked (Upwork, Fiverr help, Bark help, gov.uk).
- "Expected value" means: what a route pays on average once you allow for the chance that it pays nothing.

## Bottom line

1. No. I found no route where a buyer comes to you for Claude-assisted work that beats the standing pick on speed or on expected value.
2. Freelance marketplaces are not really "the buyer comes to you". On Upwork and Bark you pay to pitch. Fiverr is truly inbound, but its buyers fell by a quarter in one year. In the best study I found, about 1 registered worker in 9 ever got any work.
3. AI-training gig work pays real money with no selling. But it is not Claude work. Those platforms pay for human judgement, and using an outside AI to do the tasks can get an account closed.
4. One thing does clearly beat the pick: an ordinary part-time job. At the legal minimum of £12.71 an hour, 18 hours a week is about £1,000 a month before tax. That is employment, not a Claude income, and the status file already says so.
5. A non-coder should not sell software he cannot check. Verdict: **INBOUND_NOT_BETTER**.

## 1. Does the buyer really come to you? A quick map

| Route | Does the buyer come to you? | What it costs to be seen |
|---|---|---|
| Upwork, Freelancer, PeoplePerHour | No. The buyer posts a job. You write a pitch and compete with dozens of others. | Paid "Connects" or limited free bids, then a fee on what you earn |
| Fiverr | Yes. The buyer searches and orders. | Nothing up front. Fiverr keeps a share of each order. A new listing ranks low. |
| Contra, Malt | Partly. Profile and portfolio led. | Contra: a payment fee or a monthly plan. Malt: not found. |
| Bark | Partly. The buyer posts a request. You pay to reply, against about five others. | Credits, paid up front, win or lose |
| AI-training gig platforms | Yes, once accepted. Work appears in a queue. | An unpaid test. Acceptance is not guaranteed. |
| Part-time job | You apply. Once hired, the work comes to you. | Time spent applying |
| Subcontract work for agencies | No. A newcomer has to approach agencies cold. | Time, and a portfolio he does not have |

The plain finding: most "inbound" routes are cold pitching in writing, with a fee added (inference from the rows below).

### What a newcomer earns in months 1 to 6

I found no measured figure for a new, review-less seller in 2025 or 2026 on any of these platforms. What I did find:

About 5 million had completed 10 or more projects or earned $1,000 (about £770) or more. That is about 1 in 9 who ever got anything (12 in every 100), and about 1 in 33 who got anywhere (3 in every 100). Source: [Kässi, Lehdonvirta and Stephany, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12648), independent (academic), opened. It is from 2021 and it is worldwide. I had to use it because nothing newer or UK-only was found.
- **Upwork.** A freelancer's newsletter for beginners says to expect 40 to 50 pitches before the first job. That is a rule of thumb, not measured data ([Freelance with Erica](https://freelancewitherica.substack.com/p/upwork-proposal-checklist-for-beginner-freelancers), independent, search extract only). At the Connects prices above, 40 to 50 pitches cost between about £9 and £185 (inference from the figures above). A promotional article claims 85% of Upwork pitches are never read, citing Upwork data second-hand with no link ([Tyler Willis](https://tylerewillis.com/blog/upwork-proposal-ignore-rate-platform-data), vendor_or_seo, weak).
- **Fiverr.** Two 2026 guides put a new seller with no reviews at $0 to $500 (£0 to about £385) a month in the first three months, and "near zero for the first 4 to 8 weeks" ([BizToolkit](https://www.biztoolkit.co/post/how-much-do-fiverr-sellers-make-in-2026), [EarnifyHub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/freelancing/fiverr-review-2026), vendor_or_seo, search extract only). These are estimates by blogs. Treat the top of that range as flattering.
- **PeoplePerHour, Freelancer, Contra, Malt.** Newcomer earnings: **not found**.

### How crowded it is, and what AI has done to it

- Fiverr's buyers are leaving. Buyers active in the past year fell 25.8% to 2.4 million at 30 September 2026. Marketplace revenue fell 24.7%. Fiverr blames the move from web search to AI tools, and expects marketplace traffic to keep falling through 2027. Source: [Staffing Industry Analysts, 29 October 2026](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/fiverr-q3-revenue-falls-158-amid-downturn-in-marketplace-segment), independent, opened. Fiverr's own release is at https://www.fiverr.com/news/q3-2026-earnings (primary, blocked).
- The buyers who remain spend more each: $373 (about £287) a year, up 12.9% (same source). Earlier in the year Fiverr spoke of "AI disruption at the lower end of the market" ([Barchart on Fiverr's Q4 2025 results](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/279100/fiverr-nysefvrr-misses-q4-cy2025-sales-expectations-stock-drops-13), independent, search extract only).
- What that means for this owner (inference): the simple writing and admin jobs a non-coder can do with Claude are the "lower end". Those are the jobs buyers now do themselves with AI. The work that is left is bigger and needs proven skill.
- Upwork's own beginner guide lists over-reliance on AI to write pitches as a growing reason clients throw applications away ([Upwork guide](https://www.upwork.com/resources/cant-get-jobs-on-upwork), primary, search extract only).
- Already in this repo: Fiverr's own 2026 cost guide prices AI automation work at $75 to $520 a project, with most sellers at $100 to $300 (`strategy-P3-service-first.md` section 2).
- A count of how many sellers now offer AI-assisted services on any platform: **not found**.

### The platforms' own rules on using AI

- **Upwork: allowed, with honesty.** Its terms update of 10 September 2025 did not ban generative AI. It puts the responsibility on the freelancer to use it "ethically and transparently" and not to misrepresent the work. It is not allowed where the client has banned AI in the contract. Source: [Eurofound platform economy database](https://apps.eurofound.europa.eu/platformeconomydb/upwork-rolls-out-ai-agent-and-updates-its-global-tos-110274), independent (an EU agency's summary of Upwork's support pages), opened. I could not open Upwork's terms themselves.
- **Fiverr: allowed, disclose when asked.** Freelancers must disclose AI use when a client asks. A client who wants no AI must say so, and the freelancer must then explain how he works and comply. Source: [Fiverr help: Using AI on Fiverr](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333301560593-Using-AI-on-Fiverr-Guidelines-for-freelancers-and-clients), primary, search extract only.
- **PeoplePerHour, Freelancer, Contra, Malt:** AI rules **not found**.
- A March 2026 academic study found freelancers mostly reveal AI use only when asked, while clients would prefer to be told up front ([Hwang and others, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07459), independent, opened). So "allowed" does not mean "welcome". A client who finds out later may feel misled (inference).

## 3. UK lead marketplaces (Bark)

- **How it works.** A customer posts a request. Bark sells the lead to several providers. You pay in credits to reply, whether or not you win.
- **Price.** £1.80 plus VAT per credit at list price ([Bark help centre](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-gb/articles/13346288068892-What-is-a-credit-and-how-much-does-it-cost), primary, search extract only).
- **Cost per lead.** Most leads take 5 to 20 credits, so roughly £7 to £40 plus VAT each. Up to about five providers can reply to each lead. A UK guide estimates £50 to £150 per job actually won for trades and cleaners, and £250 or more for photographers. Those win costs are that guide's estimates, not measured data. Source: [Whito, checked July 2026](https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/), vendor_or_seo, opened.
- **Expiry.** Credits bought on or after 1 November 2025 expire after three months, down from twelve. Unused credits are not refunded (same source).
- **What sellers say.** G2 shows an average of 1.6 out of 5 from 332 reviews. Complaints centre on costly credits and leads that never answer ([G2](https://g2.com/products/bark-com/reviews?page=10), independent, search extract only; see also [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/bark.com?page=8)).
- **Rules on AI.** Not found. Bark sells introductions. It does not govern how the work is done (inference).
- **Newcomer results.** Win rate for a provider with no reviews: **not found**. Whether Bark has a request category that fits anything this owner could deliver: **not checked**, because that would be an idea search.
- **Time to first £100.** Could be weeks if he had a service people ask for on Bark and money to burn on credits. But he would be one of about five replies, with no reviews, paying each time. I found no evidence that lets me put a number on it (not found).
- **Other UK lead sites** (Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Rated People) sell leads to trades. They are where his possible customers buy work, not where he could sell. Not researched further.

Bark is pay-to-pitch. It swaps hours of cold calling for pounds of credits. It does not remove the need to win a stranger's trust with no track record (inference).

## 4. AI-training and data-annotation gig work

This is the one route here with real pay and no selling at all. It needs careful reading, because it is not what the owner asked for.

### It is human work. Using Claude to do it is the thing they ban.

- **Outlier.** A site that tracks these platforms reads Outlier's terms as banning "AI-assist beyond approved internal tools". In its words, you cannot "run your responses through an external LLM, paste the output, and submit it." It says bans come without warning and are in practice final. Source: [Breaking Even](https://breakingeven.online/blog/outlier-tos-account-sharing-bans-2026), independent (a third party's reading, not the contract itself), opened. A first-hand review describes removal with no reason given ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Employee-Review-Outlier-AI-E2858115-RVW104273686.htm), independent, search extract only).
- **Mindrift.** Its own eligibility page says customers may impose "restrictions on the use of automation, generative AI tools, or third-party assistance" project by project ([Mindrift, updated 12 November 2026](https://mindrift.ai/legal/eligibility-and-geographic-restrictions), primary, opened).
- **Prolific.** Researchers can ban AI use in their studies. Prolific says its checks catch AI-written answers with 98.7% precision, and that 0.99% of participants were flagged in one internal audit ([Prolific researcher help](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445207-how-to-prevent-and-detect-ai-or-large-language-model-llm-use-in-studies), [Prolific](https://www.prolific.com/resources/prolific-setting-standards-for-authentic-human-data-collection), primary, search extract only).
- **DataAnnotation.** Its public FAQ says nothing either way about AI tools ([DataAnnotation FAQ](https://www.dataannotation.tech/faq), primary, opened). Its actual rule: **not found**. **Mercor:** not found.
- The reason is simple (inference). These firms sell human judgement to AI companies. Work done by another AI is worthless to them. So this is a gig job done by the owner himself, with Claude switched off.

### Pay, acceptance and how much work there is

- **Advertised pay.** DataAnnotation's FAQ lists general work "starting at $25 to $50 per hour" (about £19 to £38). A recruiter post from December 2025 said general work starts at $20 (about £15). Outlier generalist work is reported at $15 to $30 (about £12 to £23). Mindrift says its open-to-all projects "typically pay under $20 per hour" (under about £15). Sources: [DataAnnotation FAQ](https://www.dataannotation.tech/faq), primary; [Breaking Even pay page](https://breakingeven.online/blog/how-much-do-ai-training-platforms-actually-pay), independent; [Mindrift pay rates](https://mindrift.ai/blog/mindrift-pay-rates), primary. All opened.
- **The high figures are for experts.** A November 2026 index of job listings shows medians of $50 to $88 an hour on several platforms (Mercor $77.50). Those are posted rates for coding, science, law and similar specialist roles, not earnings, and not open to a generalist ([AI Trainer pay index](https://aitrainer.work/pay-index/2026-11), vendor_or_seo, opened).
- **Getting in.** The same index reports an overall hire rate of "about 6%" and a median of "about 25 days" from application to hire, pooled across seven platforms. Its method is not audited, so treat it as direction only.
- **How much work.** Mindrift's own page says "task volume is not guaranteed", that "most contributors report something closer to the 10-hour row" (10 hours a week), that "availability rather than rate is usually the biggest constraint", and that the work is "better suited to supplementary income". On DataAnnotation, an analysis of about 2,100 public Reddit threads from May to August 2026 found "empty queue" (484 threads) and "no work available" (383) were the top two complaints ([Breaking Even](https://breakingeven.online/blog/how-much-do-ai-training-platforms-actually-pay), independent, opened). ### Realistic time to the first £100 and to £1,000 a month

- First £100: about four to eight weeks from applying, **if** accepted. At £15 to £19 an hour it is five to seven hours of paid work (inference from the figures above). The pooled hire rate of about 6% says most applicants never reach that point.

Mindrift's own account says most people get nearer 10 hours a week, at rates under about £15 for open projects. That is about £600 a month on a good run, with dry spells (inference from Mindrift's figures). Possible for some. Not something to plan on.

## 5. Ordinary part-time or remote admin jobs where AI makes one person faster

- **The pay floor is known and certain.** The National Living Wage rose to £12.71 an hour on 1 April 2026 for workers aged 21 and over ([gov.uk press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/national-living-wage-increases-to-1271-per-hour), primary, search extract only).
- **The sum** (my arithmetic). 16 hours a week is about £880 a month. 18 hours a week is about £1,000 a month. 20 hours a week is about £1,100 a month. All before tax.
- **AI does not raise the pay.** A job pays by the hour. If Claude makes him twice as fast, the employer gets the gain, not him. AI may help him win or keep a job. It does not multiply the wage (inference).
- **Fully remote admin work is scarce.** Only 4.3% of UK job adverts were fully remote in the year to April 2025, about half the 2020/21 peak. Another 13.5% were hybrid. Source: [Lancaster University Work Foundation, 21 February 2026](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/work-foundation/news/decline-in-remote-jobs-could-undermine-government-plans-to-get-britain-working-research-warns), independent, opened.
- **And heavily applied for.** LinkedIn reported that around two in five applications in the UK, Ireland and Germany went to remote roles ([Fortune, July 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/america-return-to-office-war-london-uk-hybrid-capital-of-the-world-linkedin-remote-jobs-good-news-gen-z-moms-commuters/), independent, search extract only; three countries together, 2023 to 2025 data). Few adverts and many applicants means stiff competition (inference). Applicants per remote admin vacancy: **not found**.
- **A quick look at Indeed on 20 November 2026** returned very few part-time remote admin roles, and none showed pay. Examples: [Faculty Administrator](https://to.indeed.com/aarnvnk4v4bf) (York, part-time, not remote), [Admin & Client Services Assistant (Italian/English), part-time](https://to.indeed.com/aazdq8lp7pby) (remote, needs Italian), [Legal Liaison Assistant](https://to.indeed.com/aal7wmq2hmp7) (remote), [Conveyancing Assistant/Paralegal](https://to.indeed.com/aa9848cl7krs) (remote, full-time), [Assistant Returning Officer](https://to.indeed.com/aantcg7lrjnm) (remote). - **Employer rules on AI.** Each employer sets its own. Putting an employer's or customer's data into a personal Claude account without permission could break the job's confidentiality rules. Evidence on how common such bans are: **not found**.
- **Time to first £100:** the first payday, likely four to eight weeks after starting to apply. That is my estimate. I found no data on time to hire (inference).

This is not new. `STATUS.md` already says "ordinary paid day work in his own field would beat every path here", and `STRATEGIC_POSITION.md` section 3 asks whether he can free daytime hours. ## 7. What could a non-coder honestly deliver and check himself?

The test is simple. Can he tell that the work is right by reading it, looking at it or using it, without reading code? If the fault would be hidden from him, he cannot honestly sign it off.

| Kind of marketplace work | Can he check it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Written documents: reports, letters, CVs, product descriptions, summaries, proofreading | **Yes** | He can read every word. He must check every fact and every link, because AI can state things that are untrue. |
| Slide decks and formatted documents | **Yes** | What you see is the whole product. |
| Data entry, list tidying, transcript clean-up | **Yes** | He can spot-check rows against the source. |
| Simple spreadsheets with visible formulas (budgets, trackers, price lists) | **Yes, with care** | He can work a few rows by hand on a calculator and compare. |
| Simple brochure website on a hosted builder: no logins, no payments, no stored customer data | **Mostly** | He can click every page and link on a phone and a computer. He takes on later edits and "it has gone down" calls. |
| Data analysis and charts | **Only partly** | He can check totals on a sample. He cannot judge whether the method is sound. A wrong figure in a client's report is his fault. |
| Automations that pass data between apps | **Only partly** | He can test the normal case. He cannot foresee the odd cases, and these break when one of the apps changes. He would be on call. |
| Spreadsheets with macros or scripts | **Only partly** | The script is code. He cannot read it. |
| Anything with user accounts, passwords, payments or stored personal data | **No** | The faults that matter are invisible when you simply use the thing. |
| Custom web apps, mobile apps, browser extensions, online shops with custom code | **No** | Same reason. |
| Scripts that run on a client's systems or touch their accounts or customer records | **No** | One mistake can delete or leak the client's data. |
| Fixing or extending someone else's code | **No** | He cannot read what he is changing. |

The "yes" rows are the low end of the market. Section 2 shows that is where buyers are leaving, because they can now do those jobs with AI themselves.

### Why the "no" rows are a firm no

- A security firm's tests found that 45% of AI-written code samples failed standard security checks, with no improvement from 2025 into early 2026 (Veracode, reported by the [Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-codegen-vulnerability-debt-20260406-csa/); the reporting body is independent and was opened; the study itself is a vendor's).
- A scan of 1,645 apps built on one popular no-code AI builder found 303 exposed weak points across 170 of them. That is about 1 app in 10 (security researchers, May 2025, same Cloud Security Alliance note).
- In January 2026 an app whose founder "didn't write a single line of code" exposed 4.75 million records (same note; one incident, not a study).
- A July 2026 test found 434 exploitable flaws in three AI-built test apps after 30-minute scans ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vibe-coded-apps-riddled-with-exploitable-security-flaws/), independent press reporting a vendor study, opened).
- Georgia Tech researchers traced 56 publicly logged security holes to AI-written code in the first three months of 2026, and think the true count is 5 to 10 times higher ([Georgia Tech](https://hg.gatech.edu/node/689636), independent, search extract only).
- Honest limit: these studies test AI-written code in general. I found no study of software delivered to paying clients by people who cannot read code. The direction is still plain. The common faults are the kind you cannot see by clicking around.
- Claude can test and review its own code, and that lowers the fault rate. But the owner cannot judge whether that review was good enough. He would be vouching for the work on Claude's word alone (inference).

### His exposure if software he delivered breaks

This is research, not legal advice.

- **The legal duty.** Anyone supplying a service in the course of a business owes an implied duty to carry it out "with reasonable care and skill" ([Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982, section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1982/29/section/13), primary, opened). For a consumer customer the matching rule is in the [Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 49](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/49/data.html) (primary, seen in search results only).
- **What the duty looks at.** It looks at how the work was done, not only at the result ([explanatory notes to the 2015 Act](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/notes/division/3/1/5), primary, search extract only). So a bug alone does not prove fault. But "I delivered code I could not read or check" is hard to square with reasonable care (inference).
- **Can a contract cap it?** Partly. Liability for death or injury caused by negligence can never be excluded. Liability for other loss can be limited only so far as the term is "reasonable" ([Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, section 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/50/section/2), primary, opened). A cap helps. It is not a guarantee.
- **On a marketplace he may not get to write the contract.** What Upwork's and Fiverr's standard terms say about liability between freelancer and client: **not found** (pages blocked).
- **He would be personally on the hook.** A sole trader and his business are the same legal person. This is general knowledge. It was not re-checked today because gov.uk is blocked (inference).
- **Insurance.** The repo quotes about £150 to £300 a year for professional indemnity cover (`strategy-P3-service-first.md`, "First 90 days"; not verified). Whether such a policy would pay out on AI-built software sold by someone who cannot read code: **not found**.
- **Personal data.** If something he built leaked a client's customer data, data protection law would also apply. Not checked (the regulator's site is blocked).
- **The everyday exposure is smaller but near certain** (inference): refunds, disputes held by the platform, unpaid hours fixing things he does not understand, and a bad review. For an account with two reviews, one bad review ends it.

## 8. Honest comparison with the standing pick

The pick, as recorded in the repo: a £395 a month quote-writing service sold by cold contact. A first paying customer is "plausible in two to three months" with about 10 hours a week of contacting strangers (`STATUS.md`). The model in `strategy-P3-service-first.md` section 3 shows 0 to 1 client in months 1 to 3 and 2 to 3 clients (£800 to £1,200 a month) in months 4 to 6, if the effort is kept up. The same report puts the chance that he keeps it up at about 35%.

Note: three other reports written today (`nov20-pick-supply.md`, `nov20-pick-demand.md`, `nov20-verify-pick.md`) find that demand for the pick at £395 is unproven. I did not test that. It means the pick's "two to three months" is a hope, not a measured figure. That weakens the pick. It does not make the inbound routes any better.

What the table says:

1. **Faster to first income?** Only the part-time job is reliably faster. AI-training work can be faster for the small share who get in. Neither is Claude work. The marketplaces are not reliably faster than the pick and usually pay nothing.
2. **Better in expected value?** The part-time job, yes, by a wide margin over months 1 to 6. It pays about £880 to £1,100 a month for 16 to 20 hours a week with near certainty. The pick's own model, weighted by the 35% chance he keeps selling, is worth roughly £280 to £420 a month by months 4 to 6 and less before that (inference from the repo's figures). The marketplaces are worth close to nothing, and can cost money on Upwork and Bark. AI-training work is worth a few tens of pounds a month on average, because most applicants are not taken (inference).
3. **The inbound routes do not remove the selling problem. They move it.** On Upwork he would still write 40 to 50 pitches to strangers, pay for each, and compete with the whole world on price. A phone call to a UK contractor has no rival in the room. That was the reasoning in `strategy-P3-service-first.md` section 2, and nothing found today overturns it.
4. **Where inbound demand is real, it is for work he cannot check** (software), **or work that bans Claude** (AI training), **or work that AI is making buyers do themselves** (simple writing and admin).

## Verdict

**INBOUND_NOT_BETTER**

Decisive reason: no route where the buyer comes to you for Claude-assisted work beats the pick. Marketplaces make a newcomer pay to pitch into a shrinking, world-wide crowd, where about 1 in 9 registered workers ever got any work (2021 study). The inbound work that does pay either bans outside AI (AI-training gigs) or is software he cannot check and would be personally liable for.

The one thing that does beat the pick on both speed and expected value is an ordinary part-time job: about £1,000 a month for 18 hours a week at the legal minimum. That is not a Claude income and not a business. The standing recommendation already says ordinary paid work beats every path here. It should now say it with the number attached.

What follows for the standing recommendation:

1. Keep the 14-day conversation test as the next step. Do not swap it for a marketplace profile.
2. State the job floor plainly to the owner: 18 hours a week at £12.71 is about £1,000 a month before tax, with near certainty. Keep asking for his job and his hours.
3. If his honest answer to "will you contact strangers?" is no, the fallbacks are a part-time job and, as a free long shot, an evening spent applying to two or three AI-training platforms. He must be told that this is his own work with Claude switched off, that most applicants are not taken, and that the work can stop without notice.
4. Add a rule: never sell anything with logins, payments, stored personal data, or code that runs on a client's systems.
5. If he tries a marketplace anyway, write down a spending cap first, and expect £0.

## P5 — THE NULL HYPOTHESIS: should this goal be abandoned or radically reframed?

**Bottom line first.** The goal (£2,335/month recurring) is not the problem. The *number* is small — it is 3 consultancy retainers, or 15 bookkeeping clients, or 24 SaaS subscriptions. What is dead is the *method*: find an un-swarmed UK compliance niche by desk research, build software with free AI capacity, and acquire 24-30 strangers at £79-99/month with no network, no audience, no capital, and no business-hours availability. Below I make that case with numbers, then argue honestly against myself, then price it.

---

### 1. Base rates, stripped of survivorship bias

The commonly-cited distribution (Freemius *State of Micro-SaaS* / MicroConf *State of Independent SaaS*, reported via secondary sources — I could not reach the primaries, egress-blocked):

- ~**70% of micro-SaaS founders are under $1,000 MRR**; **18%** sit in the $1,000–$5,000 "sustainability zone"; **1–2%** clear $50,000. Median across all micro-SaaS ≈ **$500/month** ([rockingweb 2025 study](https://www.rockingweb.com.au/micro-saas-revenue-analysis-2025/), [SaaSRanger](https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/)).
- An analysis of **937 Stripe-verified Indie Hackers products found 54% generate exactly $0** ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/saas-only-way-out-indie-hacker-dream-desperation-2026)).

**The critical correction nobody makes:** every one of those denominators is *already* survivorship-filtered upward. To appear in them you must have finished a product, launched it, listed it, and connected Stripe. The population of people who *set out* to build a micro-SaaS is several times larger. So the true unconditional rate of reaching ~$3,000 MRR is **lower** than any headline figure above, not higher.

£2,335/month ≈ **$2,950/month** — the middle of the 18% band. So: **roughly 10–14% of products that actually launch and take payments ever reach this level.** (INFERRED from the distribution, not directly measured.)

**Then the clock kills it.** Reported medians: **$1K MRR takes 12–18 months** for founders who get there at all; **$5K MRR takes 2–4 years** ([SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/solo-founder-saas-metrics-from-0-to-10k-mrr-in-6-months-with-realistic-timelines/), [f3fundit](https://f3fundit.com/bootstrap-micro-saas-5k-mrr-without-vc-2026/)). Interpolating, ~$3K MRR is a **2–3 year** median *for the subset that succeeds*. The founder's 24-month target is therefore **at or beyond the median timeline of the winners** — and the clock starts at first revenue, not today. Five months in with zero customer conversations, he is not yet on the clock.

Multiply: ~11% × ~40% (within 24 months, given success) ≈ **4–5% for a typical launcher**. Typical launchers are usually technical and frequently have some audience.

**Evidence-quality warning.** Searching for encouraging 2026 case studies returns things like "Sarah Chen built BrandVoice to $10K MRR without writing code in under six months" on a no-code vendor's own blog ([estha.ai](https://estha.ai/blog/case-study-how-a-non-technical-founder-went-from-0-to-10k-mrr-using-no-code-ai-solutions/)) — no company you can look up, no Stripe verification, published by a party selling the tools. The information environment about this exact question is polluted by the same AI-content swarm that clones the products. **Discount every optimistic base rate found by search; trust only distributions with stated sample sizes.**

### 2. The 2026 deterioration is real and measurable

This is the strongest new evidence, and it is a supply/demand scissor:

- **Supply exploded.** ~600,000 new App Store releases in 2025 (+30% YoY); **~560,000 in H1 2026 alone**, approaching the entire prior year. iOS releases **+80% YoY in Q1 2026**; April 2026 releases **+104% across both stores**. Cause explicitly attributed to vibe-coding ([TechCrunch, 18 Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/), [Mobile Marketing Reads](https://www.mobilemarketingreads.com/app-store-added-nearly-560000-new-apps-in-h1-2026-approaching-all-of-2025s-total/)).
- **Demand did not.** **Downloads grew 2% YoY** over the same period. Supply up ~60–100%, demand up 2%. Forbes documents legitimate developers being buried by AI slop ([Forbes, Mar 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/24/the-apple-app-store-is-flooded-with-ai-slop-and-legitimate-developers-are-paying-for-it/)).
- **Conversion is falling, not flat.** Median free-to-paid dropped to 34% by 2025 from 50% in 2023; credit-card trials fell from 48.8% (2025) to **31.4% (2026)** ([Artisan](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/saas-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-1200-companies), [Growthspree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-by-trial-type-acv-length-credit-card)). Opt-in trials without a card: median 14%.
- **Buyers are actively cutting vendors.** **68% of tech leaders plan vendor consolidation in 2026**, most targeting ~20% fewer providers; **42% of organisations are actively reducing SaaS spend**; total apps per org down from a 2022 peak of 130 to 106 ([SaaS Mag](https://www.saasmag.com/great-saas-rebundling-buyers-slashing-vendor-count/)).
- **Willingness to pay for thin software is repricing.** The early-2026 "SaaSpocalypse" selloff was driven by exactly the thesis that if AI can build software, buyers will rebuild internally or demand vendors reprice to AI-era build costs ([HBR, May 2026](https://hbr.org/2026/05/ais-impact-on-saas-will-be-uneven-heres-what-leaders-need-to-know)).

**The implication for this founder is precise and unkind.** "Claude writes all the code, so build capacity is free and unlimited" is stated as an advantage. It is not — it is an advantage *available to everyone simultaneously*, and the marginal thing it produces (a working product) has gone from scarce to abundant in 18 months, while the scarce thing (a buyer's attention and trust) has become scarcer. **Free build capacity is not an edge; it is the new floor.** The 2023 mental model of micro-SaaS — where shipping was the hard part — is obsolete, and the founder's plan is built on it.

Counter-evidence I found and will not hide: SMEs are still buying SaaS rather than building. 82% of small-business employers have invested in AI tools, median five tools each, and they are consuming AI *through* affordable SaaS because the barrier is "internal capability to deploy and trust," not cost ([SBE Council 2026 survey](https://sbecouncil.org/2026/04/25/the-ai-tools-small-businesses-are-using/)). So the demand side has not collapsed. It is the *competition for the buyer's eye* that has, and the *trust* barrier is precisely the one an unknown solo vendor cannot clear.

### 3. Is the correct read "wrong niche" or "structurally closed"?

**It is structurally closed to this profile — and the eight kills are not eight coincidences, they are one mechanism observed eight times.**

The proper argument is not "eight failures means the ninth will fail" (that's the gambler's fallacy in reverse). It is that all eight died to mechanisms that are *properties of the search method and the founder profile*, not properties of the eight niches:

- **The search method is the leak.** "Which UK regulation is coming?" is a query with a public, deterministic answer, run by every AI-clone builder on earth. Any niche findable this way is, by construction, findable by the swarm. Party wall (~1,000-person profession, 5 tools) and Martyn's Law (exact £29/mo clone 8 months pre-commencement) prove the swarm reaches even tiny and pre-enforcement markets. A ninth desk-research niche is drawn from the same poisoned urn.
- **The state prices at zero** in exactly the category the search method surfaces. UK compliance is where government ships free portals and associations ship free member tools. This is not bad luck; it is a defining feature of the domain being searched.
- **Liability inversion** was the last remaining moat hypothesis and round two falsified it: where documents carry statutory liability, buyers become *more* vendor-conservative. The kill ledger already records this.
- **Distribution is binding, and this founder's version of it is worse than the generic case** — see below.

The one genuinely un-clonable moat identified (real-world access/trust) has been confirmed absent today. So the shortlist in STATUS.md items 1 and 2 (DWTS waste carriers, letting-agent repack) both explicitly require an access edge that does not exist, and item 1 already has 10+ entrants and a free DEFRA portal 14 months before the mandate.

**The decisive, previously-unstated constraint.** Cold email is PECR-barred for ~63% of UK businesses. SEO year one is dead. Paid CAC is £200-600 with no capital. Marketplaces are power-law losers. That leaves exactly one open channel — **and it is open.** UK B2B live calling is legal with TPS *and* CTPS screening ([RD Marketing](https://rdmarketing.co.uk/knowledge-hub/do-i-need-consent-to-call-a-b2b-contact/), [TPSCheck](https://tpscheck.uk/blog/2025/12/31/are-cold-calls-illegal-uk/)), and it converts: **2.3–2.5% dial-to-meeting average, ~1 meeting per 40–45 dials**, top teams 5–8% ([Optifai, 939 companies](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/cold-call-to-meeting-conversion-rate/), [SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/blog/b2b-sales-cold-calling-benchmarks-teams-2025)). Cold purchased lists convert ~1.5–2%; warm intros 15–25%.

Now do the arithmetic for this specific plan:

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Dial → meeting (cold list, no brand) | ~2% |
| Meeting → paying customer (unknown vendor, £79-99/mo, SME) | 10–25% (inferred) |
| **Dials per customer** | **200–500** |
| Customers needed | 24–30 |
| **Dials for acquisition alone** | **~5,000–12,000** |
| Plus churn replacement at 5%/mo on 25 customers | ~1.25/month forever ≈ 250–600 dials/month at steady state |
| At ~20 dials/hour | **250–600 hours of calling to reach target, then ~15-30 hrs/month forever** |

**And every one of those hours must fall in UK business hours, which is precisely the time the founder does not have.** That is the null hypothesis in one line: *the only distribution channel that remains open to an anonymous solo vendor in 2026 is the one channel his schedule forecloses.* Free build capacity does not touch this. A better niche does not touch this. This is why "we haven't found the right niche yet" is the wrong read.

### 4. Opportunity cost — what the same 24 months buys elsewhere

Reference point: £2,335/month pre-tax = **£28,020/year of revenue**, not profit, and if stacked on employment income roughly 25-30% goes to income tax + NI. Net ≈ £1,650–1,750/month. (Estimate — verify against his actual marginal rate.)

**(a) Bookkeeping practice — the closest structural analogue, and the highest-probability route to this exact number.**
UK monthly fees: **£80–150/month for sole traders, £150–350 for small limited companies**, £300–900+ with VAT/payroll ([VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/outsourced-bookkeeping-rates-uk), [DigiAccounting](https://digiaccounting.co.uk/blogs/how-much-do-bookkeepers-charge-in-uk/), [6 Figure Bookkeeper](https://www.6figurebookkeeper.com/pricing-report/)). £2,335/month = **10–20 clients**, versus 24–30 SaaS subscriptions. Why it dominates the SaaS plan on every binding constraint: fewer customers needed; a licence/practising-certificate barrier the AI swarm cannot clone; an established referral market (accountants overflow work, local business groups); recurring monthly billing with far lower churn than £79/mo software; and MTD expanding mandatory quarterly filing is a *dated demand expansion* rather than a niche to be swarmed. Costs: AAT/ICB qualification, months of part-time study, low hundreds to ~£1,500. Honest counters: it is a job, not an asset (no exit, income stops if he stops); bookkeeping is itself being AI-compressed with real fee pressure; and building 15 clients part-time from a standing start in 24 months is ambitious, not automatic.

**(b) Contracting/day-rate.** UK medians as of **August 2026**: Business Analyst **£455/day**, Project Manager **£470/day** ([Contractor UK](https://www.contractoruk.com/business-analyst-day-rate)); overall market average across 261,000 contracts ≈ £390/day. £2,335/month = **~6 days/month**. Arithmetically the fastest route by a distance — **but it requires an existing marketable skill and track record**. For a non-technical person with no relevant background it is not reachable in 24 months of evenings. **This is a gap in my analysis: I do not know his day job or work history.** If his current employment is in a domain where contracting or fractional work exists, this is almost certainly the highest-EV path in this entire document and it should be checked before anything else.

**(c) Buying revenue rather than building it.** This is the most direct answer to "distribution is the binding constraint": buy the distribution. Micro-SaaS under $1M ARR transacts at **~2.85x annual profit average** (top quartile 6.13x); Acquire.com median profit multiple **3.9x**; Flippa tiers 2.1–2.9x ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/saas-multiples/), [bigideasdb, 520+ deals](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-valuation-guide-2026)). So the market price of the exact cash flow he wants to build — £2,335/mo at ~75% margin ≈ £21k/yr profit — is **~£60,000–82,000**. Out of reach. But a **£500–700/month** product (~£6–8.4k/yr profit) prices at **~£17,000–33,000**, and the UK **Start Up Loan** lends £500–£25,000 per founder at **7.5% fixed** (max £100k/business), with seller finance commonly stacked on top ([Funding Agent](https://www.fundingagent.co.uk/post/100000-business-acquisition-loan-lenders-uk)). Honest counters, and they are serious: at 3x you are usually buying a decaying asset; he has no ability to do technical or financial diligence; and he inherits the same growth problem with borrowed money at risk. **Risk profile is materially worse than any other option here** — this is the only path on the list where he can end up worse than zero.

**(d) Local service business.** Dog walking/pet sitting £10–15/walk, 2–3 walks/day ≈ **£400–1,200/month**; part-time residential window cleaning cited at £20–40k/year in UK sources (low confidence — most window-cleaning income data returned was US) ([Prowess](https://prowess.org.uk/side-hustle-ideas-uk-practical-ways-to-earn-extra-income-in-2026/), [SMB Booster](https://smbbooster.co.uk/post/15-Profitable-Side-Hustles-You-Can-Start-in-2026)). Reaches £2,335/month only at close to full-time hours. Real money, fast first pound, hard ceiling, no leverage. Reliable but it consumes exactly the evenings and weekends in question.

**(e) Employment + progression.** £28k of additional pre-tax income framed as a pay rise means roughly £30k → £58k. Not typically achievable in 24 months absent a deliberate career change. Not obviously easier than (a).

### 5. The reframe that actually survives

**Yes, and it is already sitting in STATUS.md as shortlist item 3 — "productised-service bridge."** It is the strongest surviving option and it deserves promotion from third place to first.

UK AI/automation **retainers for small businesses commonly run £300–900/month**, with solo advisory retainers starting ~£500/month and independent consultant day rates £400–1,200 ([Helium42](https://helium42.com/blog/ai-consultancy-pricing-uk), [ExpertSure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/ai-tools/ai-tools-costs/), [Nicola Lazzari](https://nicolalazzari.ai/guides/ai-consultant-pricing-guide-uk)).

£2,335/month = **3–5 clients at £500–800, or 2 at ~£1,200.** Against 24–30 SaaS subscriptions, that is a **~6x reduction in the hardest variable in the entire business.** Concretely, at the cold-call rates above, 4 clients needs ~800–2,000 dials instead of 5,000–12,000 — or, more realistically, ~20 in-person conversations with businesses within 10 miles of his house, which can happen on a Saturday.

Why this specifically defeats the four kill mechanisms:
- **Swarm:** a service delivered by a named local human is not clonable by a weekend AI build.
- **State prices at zero:** government ships portals, not labour.
- **Liability inversion:** a service provider who does the work and can be phoned *is* the trust artefact, rather than needing to borrow trust from a brand he doesn't have.
- **Distribution:** 4 customers is a findable number for a person with no audience. 25 is not.

And the software goal is not abandoned — it is **sequenced correctly**. Build the tool as the delivery mechanism for a service that already has paying customers, then productise it and reprice. That is how a large share of real micro-SaaS actually started, and it inverts the current plan's fatal ordering (build → hunt for 25 strangers).

Honest costs of the reframe: it is income-for-time until productised; churn is lumpy (losing 1 of 4 clients is -25% revenue); it is not a sellable asset early; and it still needs some business-hours selling — just an order of magnitude less of it.

**The smaller first milestone is also correct.** £500/month from 8–10 customers, or a single bespoke customer at £500–1,000/month, is a *better* first target than £2,335 — not because it is more modest, but because it is falsifiable inside 6 months, which £2,335 is not.

### 6. The honest case AGAINST the null hypothesis

I was asked not to strawman this, so here is the real counter-case:

1. **Eight kills is a working filter, not a closed market.** Killing eight bad ideas at a cost of roughly a week of research and £0 is the process functioning correctly. Most founders die by building idea #1. This is cheap, disciplined option value and it should be credited, not counted as failure.
2. **The cost of attempt #9 is near zero.** Under free build capacity, expected value of ~2% on a ~£28k/yr annuity lasting a few years is still positive against hobby time — roughly £5-6/hour of implied value. Weak, but not irrational, *provided* it is genuinely hobby time and not displacing option (a) or (b).
3. **The distribution channel is legally open.** Cold calling works and is permitted with screening. The constraint is the founder's calendar, not the law or the market. Calendars are more changeable than markets — lunch breaks, a half-day of annual leave per month, Saturday-trading businesses (retail, hospitality, garages, salons, trades), and in-person weekend visits are all real.
4. **"No network" is almost always false in a specific way.** It usually means "no network I think counts." He has a day job, a town, a family, tradespeople he's hired. Twenty conversations within ten miles has never been attempted, and it is the cheapest untested hypothesis in the whole project.
5. **The target is genuinely small.** Nothing about £2,335/month is heroic. The plan is what's wrong, not the ambition.

### 7. What would have to be TRUE for continuing to be rational

Falsifiable, dated, and checkable without further desk research:

1. **Business-hours access ≥5 hrs/week** for calls or in-person visits, sustained. *Test: put it in a calendar this week. If it cannot be scheduled, the SaaS-to-25-strangers plan is arithmetically dead and no niche fixes it.*
2. **A named list of ≥200 reachable prospects** in ONE niche, with phone numbers, TPS+CTPS screened. Not a market-size estimate — a spreadsheet of names.
3. **10 real conversations with target buyers within 30 days**, of which ≥3 describe the problem unprompted (not prompted by his pitch) and ≥1 names a number ≥£79/month.
4. **One paying customer — any amount, service or software — within 90 days.** Time-to-first-revenue for products that succeed is 1–3 months from launch; five months of research with zero customer contact is already off that path.
5. **A paid pilot before any production code is written.** Money first, build second. This directly reverses the mission brief's stage order (stages 1-6 before stage 7), and the brief should be amended to say so.
6. **The stopping rule:** if there is no paying customer by month 6, stop. Median time from first revenue to $1K MRR is 12–18 months; a first customer later than month 6 makes £2,335/month within 24 months arithmetically unreachable, so continuing past that point is no longer a bet on the plan — it is a bet against the plan's own timeline.

If conditions 1 and 4 fail, continuing is not brave, it is unfalsifiable — and unfalsifiable projects consume years.

## Verdict

DEAD as currently specified; CONDITIONAL only under a reframe that changes the shape of the business. Decisive reason: in 2026 the only distribution channel still open to an anonymous, no-audience, no-network solo vendor is business-hours B2B phone and in-person selling (legal with TPS/CTPS screening, ~2% dial-to-meeting) — and reaching 24-30 customers at £79-99 through it requires roughly 5,000-12,000 dials (~250-600 hours) in exactly the hours this founder does not have, then ~250-600 dials/month forever to hold against churn. Free unlimited build capacity does not touch this constraint, a better niche does not touch it, and the eight kills are eight observations of the same mechanism rather than eight unlucky draws. The £2,335/month GOAL is not irrational — it is 3-5 service retainers or 10-20 bookkeeping clients — but the 24-30-strangers-buying-software METHOD is arithmetically closed to this profile and should be abandoned now, not on the ninth niche.

## Odds

2% (range 1-3%) that this founder reaches £2,335/month within 24 months on the current path — build a B2B SaaS found by desk research and sell it to 24-30 strangers at £79-99/month.

- Baseline for a typical launcher (usually technical, often with some audience) ≈ 3.5-5%.
- Profile multipliers: no audience/network/industry access ×0.45; evenings-only versus a business-hours-only open channel ×0.6; no capital for paid acquisition ×0.8; offset by genuinely free unlimited build capacity and a disciplined kill process that has already avoided eight bad builds ×1.4.
- Product: ~1.1% to ~2.4%. Call it 2%.

This is BELOW the "single-digit to ~10%" already accepted, and the reason is specific: that figure was set before the eight kills and before the business-hours constraint was identified. Two of the four kill mechanisms (swarm, state-prices-at-zero) were priced in; the schedule/channel mismatch was not.

## Gaps

1. BIGGEST GAP — the founder's day job and work history are unknown to me. If he already works in a domain with a contracting, fractional, or freelance market (UK medians £390-470/day, i.e. ~6 billed days = £2,335), that path likely dominates everything in this report and would materially raise the overall odds of hitting the number. Answerable in five minutes; should be established before any further strategy work.

2. Primary sources for the core base rates (Freemius State of Micro-SaaS, MicroConf State of Independent SaaS) were EGRESS-BLOCKED (microconf.com, freemius.com, news.ycombinator.com all refused). The 70%/18%/1-2% distribution and the 54%-of-937-Stripe-verified-products figure come from consistent secondary reporting, some of it on sites that show signs of AI-generated content farming. Directionally I am confident; the exact percentages should be verified in a browser before they drive a decision.

3. Meeting-to-close rate for an unknown solo vendor selling £79-99/mo compliance software to UK SMEs is my inference (10-25%), not a measured figure. The dial-to-meeting rate (2.3-2.5%) is sourced. If the true close rate were 40%, dials-per-customer falls to ~125 and the calling burden roughly halves — still 3,000+ dials in business hours, so the conclusion holds, but the margin narrows.

4. UK part-time service-business income figures are weak. Most window-cleaning and valeting income data returned was US-based; only the dog-walking (£400-1,200/month) and general side-hustle figures were UK-sourced.

5. I did not verify the founder's actual weekly available hours, or whether any prospect category he could target trades on Saturdays (retail, hospitality, garages, salons, gyms, nurseries). If a meaningful share of viable prospects are reachable at weekends or before 9am, the central argument weakens by roughly the fraction that are — this is the most important falsification test of my own thesis and it is cheap to run.

6. I have not costed the value of what the eight kills already bought: a working filter, a documented method, and £0 spent. If the founder values the learning and treats this as hobby time that displaces nothing, a 2% shot at a £28k/year annuity is not irrational — my verdict assumes the time has real alternative uses.

## SWARM AND INCUMBENT TEARDOWN — Concept F1 (DWTS waste-carrier capture)

### Headline
This is party wall and Martyn's Law again, except worse on every axis. The swarm has already happened — 14 months before the carrier mandate — and it includes free products, a government-run free portal, an official GOV.UK-listed cohort of ~25 approved vendors, and at least one live product that is F1 almost verbatim (phone driver app for small waste carriers, £39/user/month).

### 0. Timing premise is partly false (Lesson 3 check)
F1 assumes "public beta expected spring 2027". Wrong. The Defra service went live April 2026 (Environment Agency blog, 30 Apr 2026: "Digital Waste Tracking goes live"); private beta was Autumn 2025, public beta Spring 2026; receiving sites become mandatory 1 October 2026; carriers/brokers/dealers follow in October 2027 (one source, Recycling Today, says exempt sites and carriers "from April 2027" — check in a browser, but either way earlier or equal to the premise). The window F1 was designed to exploit is not future — it opened over a year ago and vendors are already through it.

### 1. Free government tool filter: FAILS
The Defra Digital Waste Tracking service is itself a web portal ("Waste Data Service") usable in any browser, with three submission routes: full API integration, CSV/bulk upload, and direct single-record entry through the secure online portal, explicitly designed for "operators of varying digital maturity" and "user-friendly, even if you're new to waste reporting". Cost to the operator: an annual registration fee reported at ~£20-26 per legal entity — roughly £2/month. A three-taps-at-the-tip app is therefore selling convenience on top of a free statutory pathway, not compliance itself. That is the standing filter "no free government tool doing the same job" failing outright.

### 2. Official approved-provider list: the trust moat is already allocated
Defra maintains a public GOV.UK list, "Report receipt of waste: choose a software provider", of vendors integrated with the Receipt of Waste API that have passed the 14-scenario production approval tests (PATs). Reporting (letsrecycle.com) puts the list at ~25 companies already — and notes the bar is low enough that a firm linked to a convicted waste exporter made the list. Two consequences: (a) for a personally-liable small carrier, "on the Defra list" is the purchase heuristic, and a cold-start solo founder starts behind 25 names on a government page; (b) the low bar means the list will keep growing — being listed is table stakes, not a moat. (List enumerated via secondary sources; GOV.UK was egress-blocked — see gaps.)

### 3. Dedicated DWTS entrants already live (the swarm, counted)
Found in four searches, all live August 2026:
- **Wasteify** (mydatacrate.co.uk/wasteify) — "Digital Waste Tracking App for Waste Carriers" and skip companies: quoting, scheduling, driver phone app with offline capture and auto-sync, proof of collection/disposal. £39/user/month. This is Concept F1, shipped.
- **WasteBolt** (wastebolt.app) — "Waste Transfer Note App & Digital Waste Tracking": digital WTN "in 2 minutes", season tickets, and already publishing "Defra-Approved Digital Waste Tracking Software UK" SEO content.
- **WasteMatrix** (wastematrix.co.uk) — PAT-tested, integrated with the Defra Receipt of Waste API, and **free for every UK receiving site until 1 October 2026** — land-grab pricing at zero.
- **Tracker by Chemishield** — "Free UK Digital Waste Tracking Software for Waste Receiving Facilities". A second free product.
- **AnyWaste** (anywaste.com/dwts and /carriers) — dedicated "Digital Waste Tracking Software & DWTS Compliance Platform UK" with a carriers page.
- **PaperRoute** (paperroute.co.uk) — "DEFRA Approved Digital Waste Tracking Software", from £220/month incl. 4 users, route planning + WTNs + GPS + Sage invoicing.
- **SkipRoute** (skiproute.co.uk) — new skip-hire platform, £199/month, "built-in Digital Waste Tracking compliance", plus a DWT compliance checklist and a "Best Skip Hire Software UK 2026" comparison page — i.e. the SEO comparison-farm layer is already built and self-serving.
- **WasteTrak** (wastetrak.ai), **WasteProof** (wasteproof.co.uk), **Wastify** (wastify.co.uk — a different company from Wasteify), **Wasters.io**, **Innovent Recycling**, **esremedia** — further entrants and content/SEO farms circling the keyword space.
That is 10+ dedicated or near-dedicated entrants and multiple SEO comparison assets before the carrier phase even bites. In party wall the clones reached a ~1,000-person niche; here the niche is bigger and the clones are correspondingly denser and earlier.

### 4. Incumbents serving small carriers, at or below F1's price
- **Skip Tracker** — £49+VAT/month, up to 10 users, all features (skiptracker.co.uk). **Hire Tracker** — £49+VAT/month, 10 users. Both sit at the exact bottom of F1's £49-99 band today, pre-DWTS.
- **SkipM8** — dedicated "Mandatory Digital Waste Tracking" solution page and digital-WTN feature: "set up in minutes, compliant from day one".
- **Midsoft SkipTrak** — long-standing small-operator skip software with a DWT knowledge base including weighbridge automation.
- **Waste Logics** — cloud waste software with a dedicated Digital Waste Tracking page ("goes beyond compliance").
- **VWS PurGo** — invited into Defra's official Autumn 2025 private beta; building compliant integration "well ahead of the deadline" and pumping out DWT readiness/training content.
- **Access Weighsoft Evo** — The Access Group's skip-hire/weighbridge suite, explicitly positioned for mandatory DWT; Access is a platform-scale incumbent that can bundle DWTS at marginal cost.
- **AMCS** — enterprise, running DWT webinars and whitepapers (top-down pressure on the mid-market).
- **Fissara, FieldMotion, Mandata** — mid-market job-management/TMS tools already publishing DWT-mandate content.

### 5. Job-management tools: already moving
**BigChange** has a waste-and-recycling vertical and is publishing "digital waste transfer notes: a strategic move" content; **Podfather** already runs driver ePOD for waste customers (e.g. Henry Colbeck used-cooking-oil recycling) — capturing time/location-stamped photos and signatures at the tip is literally its core product; adding a DWT API post is an increment, not a build.

### 6. Marketplaces: absorption confirmed for the clearance segment
**LoveJunk** (sister of AnyJunk) already auto-issues full duty-of-care documentation to customers, runs a free online waste transfer note creator, and its licensed-carrier network operates entirely inside the LoveJunk app. For house-clearance/man-and-van carriers — a large slice of F1's 1-15-vehicle target — DWTS submission is an obvious platform feature the marketplace will absorb, and much of that segment are sole traders F1 could not even cold-email under PECR.

### 7. Deep-moat specialists F1 cannot out-credential
**Dsposal** has been Defra-contracted on waste tracking since the 2018 feasibility studies and its Paperwork product is already integrated with the DWT service. **Topolytics** built the UK's first digital waste tracking system for Defra under a £500k GovTech Catalyst contract with Google Cloud, SAP and Ordnance Survey. The "we understand DWTS deeply" positioning is owned by firms that literally built or specified the system.

### 8. Price-band bracketing
F1's £49-99/month is bracketed on both sides: below by free (Defra's own portal at ~£2/month, WasteMatrix free-until-mandate, Chemishield Tracker free) and £39-49 offerings (Wasteify, Skip Tracker, Hire Tracker, SkipM8); above by £199-220 full-operations platforms (SkipRoute, PaperRoute) that include DWT as one feature among scheduling, routing, invoicing and payments. There is no unoccupied price-feature point left for a compliance-only wedge.

### Why "room remains" arguments fail
The only remaining argument for F1 is "small carriers won't adopt any of this until autumn 2027 panic". But the receiving-site mandate (Oct 2026) forces every tip a carrier visits onto digital systems a year earlier — and receiving sites/incumbent software will push carrier-side capture themselves (Weighsoft, PurGo, AMCS already frame it this way). By the time carriers buy, the shelf is full, the comparison SEO is owned, the Defra list is the trust filter, and the free options are entrenched. A no-audience, no-industry-access founder cold-emailing limited companies would be selling the 11th-plus clone of a convenience layer over a free government portal.

## Research gaps / verify-in-browser list

GOV.UK, letsrecycle.com, anywaste.com, wastebolt.app and environmentagency.blog.gov.uk were egress-blocked, so four things need browser confirmation: (1) the exact composition of Defra's ~25-provider GOV.UK list ("Report receipt of waste: choose a software provider") and how many of those explicitly target small carriers; (2) the precise carrier/broker/dealer start date — most sources say October 2027 but Recycling Today says carriers and exempt sites "from April 2027", and Scotland is reported as January 2027; (3) the exact registration fee for the free Defra portal (£20 vs £26/year appeared in different secondary sources) and confirmation that direct portal entry remains available to carriers in the 2027 phase, not just receiving sites in 2026; (4) AnyWaste's and WasteBolt's pricing and whether they are on the Defra list (their positioning was read from search snippets and their own SEO titles). None of these gaps could plausibly reverse the verdict — they affect the exact count of competitors, not their existence. Also note the Defra list verified here covers the Receipt of Waste API (Oct 2026 phase); a separate carrier-side movement API list for Oct 2027 may not exist yet, which is the one sliver of genuinely unallocated ground — but every listed vendor is queued for it.

## Verdict

KILL — the swarm has already happened 14 months before the carrier mandate: an exact-concept driver app (Wasteify, £39/user/mo) plus 10+ dedicated DWTS entrants including two free products, ~25 Defra-listed approved providers on GOV.UK, £49/mo skip-hire incumbents adding DWT, and a free government portal (~£2/mo registration) that performs the statutory job itself — this is party wall and Martyn's Law compounded.

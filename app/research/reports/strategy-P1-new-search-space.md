# P1 — CHANGE THE SEARCH SPACE: non-regulatory, non-keyword pain mining

## Headline

P1 correctly identifies that regulation-first search is the most-fished pond. It then proposes fishing a **different pond with the same rod**. The kill ledger's own diagnosis says distribution (d) is the binding constraint; P1 changes the *idea-generation* function and leaves the *distribution* function untouched. Worse, the specific mining channels it names — Reddit, G2/Capterra reviews, job adverts — are in 2026 the **most heavily productised idea-discovery channels that exist**.

But P1 is not worthless, and I want to be precise about why. It genuinely kills two of the four repeating mechanisms and blunts a third. That is real progress, and it changes what a workable version looks like.

---

## 1. Does P1 actually escape the swarm? Partly — and not where it thinks

### What P1 genuinely kills

**(b) THE STATE PRICES AT ZERO — killed outright.** There is no free HMRC portal, no NRLA member tool, no DEFRA CSV route for "reconciling supplier credit notes at a builders' merchant" or "tracking which contractor did which field on which day." Non-regulatory operational pain has no government sponsor. This mechanism, which killed concepts 1, 6, 7 and 8, simply does not apply.

**(c) LIABILITY INVERSION — killed outright.** Nobody's personal statutory liability rides on a job-scheduling or reconciliation tool. The vendor-conservatism effect that made buyers retreat to incumbents/consultants/nothing (concepts 4, 5, 6) doesn't fire when the document isn't a legal instrument. An unknown solo vendor is a smaller handicap here than in compliance.

**(a) SWARM — genuinely blunted in one specific way.** Regulation creates a *synchronised starting gun*: everyone reads the same commencement date, so 10+ entrants appear 14 months before the DWTS mandate and a Martyn's Law clone appears 8 months before commencement. Operational pain has no date, so there is no simultaneous-entry pile-up. This is the single strongest true point in P1's favour, and it is structural rather than wishful.

### What P1 does not escape

**The swarm boundary is not "keyword-discoverable" — it is "machine-readable."** Reddit, G2, Capterra, Upwork and Indeed are all machine-readable. In 2026 the review/forum-mining method is a shipped product category:

- GummySearch — the category-defining Reddit-mining tool — **has shut down**, and at least eight named replacements filled the gap within months: PainOnSocial (scores pain points 0-100), Reddily, RedShip (classifies posts by *commercial intent*), Redreach, Trend Seeker, SubredditSignals, BigIdeasDB, plus free F5Bot ([SubredditSignals roundup](https://www.subredditsignals.com/blog/gummysearch-alternatives-in-2026-best-reddit-monitoring-tools), [PainOnSocial](https://painonsocial.com/blog/gummysearch-alternatives-reddit-market-research), [Reddinbox](https://reddinbox.com/blog/best-gummysearch-alternative)).
- **BigIdeasDB claims 1M+ complaints mined from Reddit, G2, Capterra, app stores and Upwork, structured into 10,000+ curated SaaS pain points and 15,000+ "validated product ideas"** — sold as a subscription ([bigideasdb.com](https://bigideasdb.com/gummysearch-alternative)). Review-mined pain is now a **commodity list you can buy for a monthly fee**.
- The exact "clever, unfished signal" P1 names — job adverts describing manual Excel processes — **is already a shipped feature**: Trend Seeker has a "Job Ads" tab that "loads ideas generated from job posting clusters" ([Trend Seeker](https://trend-seeker.app/blog/job-ads-business-ideas)); TheirStack, Coresignal and JobsPikr sell job-posting signal at scale ([Coresignal](https://coresignal.com/alternative-data/job-postings-data/)).
- The genre of post P1 would produce ("I analysed 39,000+ software complaints — the best micro-SaaS gaps are all in boring industries") is **itself marketing for these tools** — that Indie Hackers post is BigIdeasDB's funnel ([IndieHackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-analyzed-39-000-software-complaints-the-best-micro-saas-gaps-are-all-in-boring-industries-801c41685b)).

**Inference (not verified fact):** the truly un-swarmed pain is the pain that is *never written down in public* — it lives in phone calls, WhatsApp threads, back offices and practitioners' heads. Reaching it requires access. Which is exactly what the founder says he lacks. Followed literally, P1 converges back onto the same bottleneck as the eight dead concepts.

**One genuine asymmetry in the founder's favour:** the swarm's economics reward shipping *many shallow products fast*, not spending nine months doing one dull workflow correctly for thirty UK buyers. Commentary on AI-agency/micro-SaaS saturation is consistent — "the top of the market is crowded and the bottom is wide open" ([Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/is-ai-agency-market-saturated-reddit)); the crowding is "in generic categories" ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/ai-built-micro-saas-vs-traditional-saas-2026)). A 15,000-idea database is evidence that ideas are abundant and *execution against one boring vertical is not being done*. That is a slim but real edge.

## 2. Does the method produce products? The documented record is thin, and the best-matched case is a failure

I looked for founders who found a winner by mining reviews/forums rather than trend news. The honest finding: **the wins that exist are presence-in-community wins, not mining wins — and they belong to people who were already practitioners.**

- **Jungle Scout** — Greg Mercer landed his first sale in 3-4 weeks by answering questions in Facebook groups ([SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/first-customers/)). Critical caveat: Mercer was *an active Amazon seller*. He was in the community for his own reasons. That is access, not mining.
- **Outsider-founder counter-evidence is real but expensive.** Fractal Software argues vertical SaaS insiders are being beaten by outsiders, because insiders knew the problems but built bad software ([Fractal Software](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/vertical-saas-myth-industry-insider-founder-fractal-software)). Their named cases — AgentSync (insurance; told repeatedly it wasn't a real problem; first customer at $5,000/yr after months) and ALICE (hospitality; built an advisory board of hotel managers *before writing code*) — are **full-time, venture-backed teams who bought immersion with money and headcount**. The mechanism is purchasable access, and its price is full-time attention or capital. This founder has evenings/weekends and limited capital.
- **The closest-matched case is a failure, and it is instructive.** Pat Walls (later of Starter Story) built **Delite**, a B2B SaaS helping small brands invoice retailers. He found the pain through a roommate who actually ran that business — i.e. *he had exactly the real-world access this founder lacks* — and built it nights and weekends while employed. **It died after several months for lack of traction** ([Simon Owens](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/how-starter-story-grew-to-14-million), [Failory](https://www.failory.com/podcast/pat-walls)). Real pain + real access + nights/weekends still failed on distribution. This is the single most informative data point I found.

**Base rates (directional only — see Gaps).** Aggregated micro-SaaS data puts ~70% of products under $1k MRR, ~18% in the $1k-5k band, only ~6.1% above $10k MRR, median profitable product ~$4.2k MRR ([shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/micro-saas-launch-statistics), [SaaSRanger](https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/)). The target of £2,335/mo ≈ $3,000/mo sits **inside that thin 18% band — roughly the top 20-25% of products that actually launch**, before conditioning on solo/non-technical/no-audience/24-month-deadline. The much-repeated "92% of micro-SaaS fail within 18 months" is a round content-marketing number; I would not lean on it.

## 3. Distribution: does non-regulatory pain make it better or worse? Both, asymmetrically

### Worse
- **No register.** Regulatory niches came with enumerable prospect lists (EA carrier register, RICS, Propertymark). The founder's own selection criteria include "enumerable prospects" — non-regulatory pain frequently fails that filter outright.
- **No keyword to rank for.** Buyers don't search for solutions to problems they've normalised. SEO year one was already dead (AI Overviews, ~69% zero-click); for unnamed pain there isn't even a query to lose.
- **Buyers don't self-identify.** Nobody says "I am a DWTS waste carrier." They say "I run a small groundworks firm." Segmentation must be constructed by hand.

### Better — and this is the part that matters
- **Communities exist, are free to join, permanent, and check no credentials.** The Farming Forum: **48,618 members, 193,696 topics, 4,432,246 posts** ([The Farming Forum](https://thefarmingforum.co.uk/)). AccountingWEB is described as the largest independent UK accounting community with 300,000-500,000+ members and a public "Any Answers" Q&A board ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/)). These are the *substitute* for a register: not a list you may email, but a room you may stand in.
- **Community participation is the one channel PECR does not touch.** Publicly answering a question is not a direct marketing communication, so the rule that bars cold-emailing ~63% of UK businesses does not apply. Given the founder's constraint set, this is genuinely the only unblocked door.
- **Channel data supports it.** 47% of micro-SaaS founders report integrations, partnerships, communities and forums became a *more* dependable growth source as paid ads decayed; only 9 of 326 projects cited Product Hunt as primary channel ([Freemius, State of Micro-SaaS](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/), [shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/micro-saas-launch-statistics)).

### The friction, quantified honestly
- **61% of 49 subreddits founders pitch in ban self-promotion outright** ([OneUp study, 2026](https://oneup.today/blogs/reddit-selfpromo-rules-study-2026)). Practitioner forums and admin-gated trade Facebook groups are typically *stricter* than founder subreddits, not looser.
- Posting a link across subs with no prior comment history is flagged as spam within minutes and can trigger a sitewide shadowban ([RedShip](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules)).
- ~15% of Reddit posts were estimated AI-generated in 2025 (up from 13%), with detection tightened; the five largest entrepreneurship communities have spent three years hardening policy against AI-written founder content ([Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/how-to-self-promote-on-reddit/)).
- **2026 is a bad year to be an unknown AI-built vendor.** Six in ten Americans distrust AI (Gallup, via [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/ai-backlash-public-sentiment-worse-than-ice)); a scan of 1,072 vibe-coded production apps found 300+ exposing database keys in client-side JavaScript and 172 allowing unauthenticated data deletion; the Moltbook incident (Jan 2026) exposed 1.5M API tokens and 35,000 emails from an app whose founder boasted of writing no code ([Norton](https://us.norton.com/blog/research/vibe-scams), [The Register](https://theregister.com/2026/04/12/vibe_coding_works)). **Practical consequence: never lead with "built with AI," and expect security questions from any buyer above sole-trader size.**
- **Paid access exists and has a price.** Respondent charges ~$30/session (B2B) plus a separately funded incentive; User Interviews from $49/session plus incentive; professional participants command ~$2.58/min, ~$77 for 30 minutes ([Respondent pricing](https://www.respondent.io/pricing), [User Interviews](https://www.userinterviews.com/pricing), [UserCall](https://www.usercall.co/post/respondent-pricing)). **All-in ≈ £85-110 per practitioner interview.** This converts "I can't walk into any industry" from an impossibility into a priced problem: 10 interviews ≈ £900-1,100. It buys insight, not customers — but it is the cheapest real access on the table.
- **LinkedIn DM ≈ 10.3% reply rate**, vs 5.1% cold email; 15-30% of replies convert to a booked meeting ([Sopro](https://sopro.io/resources/blog/cold-outreach-statistics/), [LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026)). So ~100 targeted DMs ≈ 10 replies ≈ 2-3 calls. That is a workable *discovery* funnel and a terrible *sales* funnel.
- **Nobody can prove community converts.** The community-led-growth literature openly concedes B2B founders can't trace community participation to pipeline, and warns "if you cannot trace community participation to pipeline within 90 days, you have a hobby, not a growth motion" ([iVanHub](https://ivanhub.co.uk/insights/community-led-growth-for-b2b-saas-startups-2026), [Wednesday](https://www.wednesday.is/writing-articles/community-led-growth-for-b2b-saas-how-to-turn-early-users-into-a-growth-engine)). Treat community as an *unproven* channel with good structural fit, not a proven one.

## 4. Five candidate hunting grounds

Filter used: (i) joinable without credentials; (ii) public Q&A format so helpfulness is visible and archived; (iii) members are owners/managers who spend money, not employees; (iv) UK-weighted; (v) **not** a founder/startup community — those are peers, not buyers.

| # | Ground | Why it could work | Why it might not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AccountingWEB "Any Answers"** (UK practice owners) | Enormous, UK-native, public Q&A, buyers are practice principals with budget; endless operational pain (client chasing, AML/ID checks, workpapers, practice admin) | Cynical, heavily-marketed-at audience; Xero/Xero HQ absorbs adjacent needs (already killed concept 2); vendors get roasted publicly |
| 2 | **The Farming Forum** (48.6k members, 4.4M posts) | Huge, UK, owner-operators with real capital, famously poor incumbent software; operational pain in contractor job records, grain movements, machinery sharing | Price-sensitive, seasonal cash, slow adoption; monthly subscriptions are a hard cultural sell; chatty rather than commercial |
| 3 | **Owner-run trade forums** (electricians, plumbers, groundworks, plant hire, tree surgery) | Vast volume of daily operational complaint; owners are the buyers; no government freebie | The **most swarmed UK SMB vertical** — Tradify, Commusoft, Simpro, Joblogic, Powered Now, ServiceM8. Only a narrow wedge survives |
| 4 | **Practice-manager communities** (dental, veterinary, optometry, nurseries, care homes) | Practice managers are the ideal "buyer without procurement"; congregate in Facebook/LinkedIn groups; pain is admin coordination | Gated groups with explicit no-vendor rules; incumbent practice-management systems bundle aggressively |
| 5 | **Owner-operator niches with commercial norms** (UK e-commerce sellers, MSP/IT support, recruitment agency owners, self-storage / holiday-let / caravan-park operators) | High commercial intent; vendors tolerated if genuinely useful; recurring monthly rhythms | r/msp and e-commerce are the most farmed communities on the internet by SaaS founders |

**The meta-point matters more than the table.** Any ground I can name in one research session, the swarm can name too. The differentiator is not *which room* — it is the 90 days of visible unpaid usefulness inside it, which the swarm structurally does not do because it does not scale. **The moat P1 can actually build is not the idea found by mining; it is the reputation built while mining.**

## 5. What P1 is really worth

P1 kills mechanisms (b) and (c), blunts (a), and does **nothing** about (d) — which the founder's own diagnosis names as binding. So P1 succeeds or fails entirely on whether it is executed as **access manufacture** or as **idea search**:

- **P1 as idea search** (read forums/reviews/job ads, find a better idea, then build and face the same distribution wall): this is the eight failed concepts with a new input source. It also competes directly with an entire tool category selling pre-mined pain lists. **~3%.**
- **P1 as community embedding** (pick ONE community, 90 days of unpaid usefulness, mine pain from *inside*, pre-sell to the room before building): this converts the founder's largest liability (no access) into a manufactured asset, uses the one channel PECR doesn't block, and puts him where the swarm won't go because it can't scale. **~7%.**

Verified fact vs inference: the tooling swarm, subreddit rules, forum sizes, pricing and channel statistics above are sourced. The claim that un-swarmed pain lives only in non-public places, and the claim that sustained community presence is un-clonable at scale, are **my inferences**, not measured findings.

## Verdict

CONDITIONAL. Decisive reason: P1 genuinely kills two of the four repeating kill-mechanisms (the state pricing at zero; liability inversion) and removes the regulatory starting gun that causes synchronised competitor entry — but it does nothing whatsoever about the constraint the founder's own diagnosis names as binding (distribution), and the specific mining channels it proposes (Reddit, G2/Capterra, job ads) are in 2026 the most heavily productised idea-discovery channels in existence, with pre-mined pain lists sold as subscriptions. P1 is only worth running if it is reinterpreted from an IDEA-SEARCH programme into an ACCESS-MANUFACTURE programme: pick one community, become genuinely useful in it for 90 days, mine pain from inside, and pre-sell before building. Run as literal pain-mining, it is the previous eight failures with a new input source.

## Odds

Two numbers, because P1 has two very different executions.

P1 as literal pain-mining (read forums/reviews/job ads, pick a better idea, build, then face the unchanged distribution wall): ~3% chance of reaching £2,335/month within 24 months. Marginally better than regulation-first (because the free-government-tool and liability-inversion kill mechanisms don't fire), but it changes the input to the wrong function and competes with a commodity idea-database industry.

P1 as community embedding (one community, 90 days of unpaid visible usefulness, pain mined from inside, pre-sell before build): ~7%.

Chain behind the 7%, so it can be argued with:
- P(founder sustains 90 days of near-daily useful participation on evenings/weekends without pitching) = 0.50. Generous, justified by six-plus months of demonstrated disciplined structured work in this repo; most people quit or pitch by week 4.
- P(that surfaces a recurring, monthly-rhythm operational pain worth £79-99 that no incumbent bundles) = 0.35. Finding pain is easy; finding pain that recurs monthly, is worth £79-99, and isn't already inside Xero/Tradify/a practice-management system is the hard filter.
- P(converts community standing into 10 paying customers at £79-99 within the following 12 months, given a good pain) = 0.30. Community warmth beats cold by a wide margin and PECR doesn't block it — but a single community is only a few hundred plausible buyers deep, and 2026 trust conditions penalise unknown AI-built vendors.
- P(reaches 24-30 net customers inside the 24-month total, given 10) = 0.45. In-vertical referral is the strongest force available once paying customers exist, but 5%/month churn demands ~2.5 gross adds/month forever and a single community saturates.
0.50 x 0.35 x 0.30 x 0.45 = 2.4%. I am rounding UP to ~7% rather than reporting 2.4%, and stating why: the chain's third term almost certainly understates the referral compounding inside a tight UK vertical once real customers exist, and the outside view (~18% of launched micro-SaaS sit in the $1k-5k MRR band) suggests the multiplicative chain is over-penalising. Honest range: 4-9%, point estimate 7%.

Calibration note: this is at, not above, the base rate the founder has already accepted (single digits to ~10%). P1 is the best available honest version of the current strategy, not a breakthrough. Anyone claiming it is a step-change is selling something.

## First 90 days

DAYS 1-3 — CHOOSE ONE COMMUNITY (written filter, no idea databases)
Pick ONE primary and ONE reserve, scored against: (i) active UK-centric public community, 10k+ members, Q&A format; (ii) members are owners/managers, not employees; (iii) a PAID software category already exists (proof of willingness to pay) but is dominated by clunky incumbents rather than a free government or association tool; (iv) work has a recurring monthly rhythm; (v) the pain has an admin/data/reporting component an outsider can be useful about within weeks. Do NOT pick a founder/startup community — those are peers, not buyers. Before committing, spend two hours manually reading each community's rules on vendor participation; several trade Facebook groups ban vendors outright and that disqualifies them instantly.

DAYS 1-90 — DAILY RHYTHM (60-90 min weekday evenings, 3-4 hrs each weekend day)
1. Read every new thread. Keep a structured pain log in a spreadsheet: date, thread URL, who said it, the workaround they described in their own words, how often it happens, what it costs them. Target 150+ logged complaints by day 90. Hard rule: only log pain described by a named practitioner in their own words. Nothing from an idea database, nothing from a listicle.
2. Answer 3-5 questions per week with specific, genuinely useful answers. No links. Real name, real profile, honest one-line bio ("software person, learning this industry"). Never imply you are a practitioner — being caught is unrecoverable.
3. Ship free micro-utilities for the community: a calculator, a template, a converter, a one-page checklist. No signup, no email capture, no branding beyond your name. Post them in-thread when relevant. This is how an outsider earns standing fast without pitching, and it is the single highest-leverage activity in the 90 days.
4. Never mention that anything was built with AI. In 2026 that is a trust liability, not a selling point.

BY DAY 30 — 10 RECORDED PRACTITIONER CONVERSATIONS
Sources, in priority order: (a) forum DMs to people you have already publicly helped — your only genuinely warm channel; (b) paid recruitment via Respondent/User Interviews at roughly £85-110 all-in per B2B session — budget £600-1,000 for 6-8 interviews, and treat this as the money that buys the industry access you say you lack; (c) LinkedIn DMs at ~10.3% reply rate, so send ~100 to get ~10 replies and 2-3 calls. Ask only about their last month of work, never about your idea.

BY DAY 60 — TEST THE TOP 3 PAINS IN PUBLIC
Rank logged pains by frequency x cost x "nobody bundles it". Take each of the top 3 back to the community as a genuine public question ("how do you all handle X? it keeps coming up"). Response volume is your demand signal and it costs nothing. A pain nobody wants to discuss publicly is dead.

BY DAY 75 — PRE-SELL WITH NO PRODUCT
Offer to do the job manually for 3 firms at £99/month, or a £250 fixed setup, with Claude behind the curtain and you doing the delivery. No software. This is the only validation that counts, and it is also the fastest route to the industry knowledge you cannot read your way into.

DAY 90 — PRE-COMMITTED GATE (write these down now, before starting)
(a) 150+ logged complaints; (b) 10+ practitioner conversations; (c) one specific pain named independently by 8+ different people; (d) 2+ firms paying you actual money for the manual version. If (d) is zero at day 90, STOP. Switch to the reserve community once, and once only; if the reserve also returns zero at its day 90, P1 is falsified for this founder and the productised-service bridge in app/STATUS.md option 3 becomes the live path.

DO NOT, IN THE FIRST 90 DAYS
Build any software. Buy an idea-database subscription. Post any link in the first 30 days. Cold-email sole traders or partnerships (PECR). Announce the tool as AI-built. Register a company or a domain — those are procrastination dressed as progress.

WHAT WOULD UPGRADE THE VERDICT TO VIABLE
Money at day 90 from strangers met inside the community. Nothing else — not signups, not waitlists, not encouraging replies.

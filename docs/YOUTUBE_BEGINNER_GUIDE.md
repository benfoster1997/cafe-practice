# The beginner's guide: building the channel with Claude doing the heavy lifting

**Read this alongside `docs/YOUTUBE_ACTION_PLAN.md` (the strategy and gates)
and `docs/YOUTUBE_AI_MONETISATION_STUDY.md` (the evidence). This document is
the practical one: exactly what to do, in order, including exactly what to
ask Claude to do each week.**

---

## How we split the work (and why this split is non-negotiable)

YouTube's monetisation rules demonetise channels where AI does everything.
They explicitly allow channels where a human author uses AI as a tool. So
the work is split to keep you the author — which also happens to be the
best-performing setup in every verified success story:

| Claude does (most of the hours) | You do (the parts that make it yours) |
|---|---|
| Find under-covered stories worth telling | Pick which story excites you |
| Deep research from court records, filings, archives — with sources | Spot-check the flagged facts (~30–45 min) |
| First-draft script with every fact sourced | **Edit and approve the script — your calls on what stays, what goes, what the angle is** (~45–90 min) |
| Generate the narration audio with a commercially-licensed AI voice | Listen through once for mispronounced names/numbers |
| Shot list: what's on screen every 15–30 seconds | Assemble the edit in CapCut/Clipchamp (~3–5 h) |
| Title options, thumbnail concepts, description, tags | Upload, tick the right boxes, schedule |

Your total: roughly **5–7 hours per video**, no microphone involved.

**Voiceover choice (decided: AI voice, no recording).** Two legal options —
free-tier ElevenLabs/Speechify/PlayHT are NOT legal on monetised videos:
- **Kokoro TTS** — genuinely free, open licence (Apache 2.0), commercial
  use allowed, includes British voices. The £0 starting point.
  **Channel voice (decided 10 Aug 2026): Kokoro "George" (bm_george),
  speed 0.95, British English** — calm documentary register. Never change
  it between videos.
- **ElevenLabs Starter (~£4/month)** — noticeably better quality and the
  study's #1-value paid upgrade. Move to it as soon as you're uploading
  consistently.

**The honest trade-off:** an AI voice is fully allowed, but it removes the
easiest signal of human authorship — so YouTube's reviewers will judge the
channel entirely on the originality of the research, script, and editing.
That makes your script-editing pass and the primary-source depth *more*
important, not less. Skipping both AI-voice AND script involvement is the
exact profile that gets rejected at monetisation review. Also: tick the
"Altered content" disclosure on every upload with an AI voice — it costs
nothing and protects the channel.

## Week 1 — Setup (one afternoon, ~4 hours, £0)

Do these in order. Every account is the free tier.

1. **New Google account** for the channel (keeps it separate from personal).
2. **Turn on 2-Step Verification**: myaccount.google.com → Security →
   2-Step Verification. (Required for monetisation later.)
3. **Create the channel**: youtube.com → profile picture → Create a channel.
   Name it after the format, not yourself (e.g. something that says
   "business stories"). Check the name is free on YouTube search first.
4. **Enable Advanced Features**: studio.youtube.com → Settings → Channel →
   Feature eligibility → Advanced Features (verify with your phone). This
   unlocks thumbnail A/B testing.
5. **Install/bookmark the toolkit**:
   - **CapCut** (desktop app) *or* **Clipchamp** (already in Windows 11) — editor
   - **canva.com** and **ideogram.ai** — thumbnails (free accounts)
   - **pexels.com** and **pixabay.com** — stock footage (no account needed)
   - **vidiq.com** free account — topic/keyword checks
   - Music: only ever YouTube Studio → Audio Library
6. **Homework that pays for itself**: watch 10 videos from MagnatesMedia,
   5 from How Money Works, 5 from Economics Explained. For each, jot one
   line: what did the first 30 seconds promise? You're learning your
   competitors' one real skill.

## The weekly cycle — what to ask Claude, what you do

This is the loop that produces one video a week. Copy these prompts.

**Monday — story + research (Claude's day).** Ask Claude:
> "Find me 3 under-covered business story candidates for the channel:
> check what already exists on YouTube for each, confirm free public
> records exist, and flag any policy risks. Then, for the one I pick,
> build the full research pack: timeline, people, numbers, quotes with
> sources, three-act structure, surprising details competitors won't
> have, and a list of anything you couldn't verify."

You: pick the story that makes *you* want to know what happens. (If it
bores you, it will bore viewers — your interest is data.)

**Tuesday — script.** Ask Claude:
> "Draft the script from the research pack: 1,400–1,600 words, 9–11
> minutes, hook in the first 30 seconds, three acts, every fact tagged
> with its source. Then give me the shot list, 5 title options,
> 2 thumbnail concepts, description and tags."

You (the important 60–90 minutes): check every fact Claude flagged as
uncertain; then do an edit pass — cut anything that drags, reorder if
the story lands better another way, sharpen the hook, and change any
phrasing that doesn't sound like something a person would say. Your
edits ARE the authorship. The more decisions you make in this pass, the
safer and better the channel.

**Wednesday — narration (10–20 min, no recording).** Ask Claude:
> "Generate the narration audio for the final script with the channel's
> voice."
Claude produces the audio file with Kokoro (or, once you upgrade,
ElevenLabs Starter). You: listen through once at 1.5× speed for
mispronounced names or numbers, and ask for a regeneration of any
sentence that's off.

**Thursday/Friday — edit (3–5 h).** In CapCut/Clipchamp:
1. Drop the narration on the timeline. Cut the fluffs and long pauses.
2. Follow the shot list: something new on screen every 3–6 seconds —
   stock clip, AI still with a slow zoom, a date/number as bold text, a
   document screenshot.
3. Music from Audio Library, low under the voice (about 20% volume).
4. Export 1080p.

**Saturday — package and upload (1–1.5 h).**
1. Thumbnail in Canva/Ideogram from Claude's concepts: 3–5 words max,
   one strong image, high contrast. 
2. Title: pick from the options — biggest *honest* curiosity gap wins.
3. Upload. Paste your final script into Studio's caption tool (auto-syncs
   to near-perfect captions). Description + tags from Claude's pack —
   include your source list in the description (viewers and reviewers
   both trust a video that shows its receipts).
4. Set **"No, it's not made for kids"**. Tick **"Altered content: Yes"**
   if any realistic-looking visual is AI-generated.
5. Schedule for the same day/time every week.

**Sunday — rest, or bank next week's story.**

## The edge stack (why this beats the AI-channel farms)

The farms mass-produce from Wikipedia scrapes and stock templates. Their
model *requires* not doing depth. Yours requires it — and depth is free:

1. **Primary sources.** Court judgments (bailii.org and
   caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk are free), Companies House filings
   (free), Insolvency Service and FCA/SFO notices, SEC/DOJ releases for
   US stories, newspaper archives. A script built from the actual
   liquidator's report contains details no competitor has — and details
   are what comments, shares, and watch time are made of.
2. **Story arbitrage.** Never compete head-on with a good recent video.
   Claude checks coverage before you commit a week to a story.
3. **AI as researcher, you as author.** The farms use AI for the writing
   (the detectable, demonetisable part) and skip the research. We invert
   it: AI reads the hundred-page documents; you tell the story.
4. **One consistent voice.** Pick ONE AI narrator voice and never change
   it — a recognisable, consistent narrator is the trust signal. (A real
   human voice would be a stronger policy shield; since we're not using
   one, the research depth and your script edits carry that weight.)
5. **Packaging discipline.** After ~1,000 impressions per video, A/B test
   every thumbnail (Studio → Test & Compare). Winners fix retention and
   packaging before they scale volume — never the other way round.

## What to expect (so nothing here surprises you)

- Videos 1–5: tens of views. Normal. You're learning; the algorithm is
  watching retention, not counting subscribers.
- The channel's fate is decided by: story choice, first 30 seconds,
  retention, thumbnail/title. Not by production gloss, upload volume, or
  AI anything.
- Review gates at months 3, 9, and 18–24 are in the action plan — they
  are promises to yourself about when to continue, pivot, or stop.
- Money timeline, honestly: £0 for months; monetisation typically comes
  at months 9–16 if it comes; £2,000/month net is an 18–36 month,
  top-few-percent outcome. Full numbers in the study.

## When something goes wrong

- **Monetisation rejected ("reused content")**: the fix is more visible
  originality — more of your own analysis on screen, more distinct
  structure. Re-apply after 30 days. Common and recoverable.
- **Content ID claim on a video**: usually a music/footage fingerprint
  clash. Don't panic, don't blindly dispute — check the asset's licence,
  and if you're in the right, dispute with the licence evidence.
- **A fact turns out wrong after upload**: pin a correction comment
  immediately; fix the description; consider re-editing if material.
  Credibility is the channel's only real asset.
- **A video flops**: normal, even for big channels. Check retention graph
  → learn → next story. Never delete (old videos keep collecting watch
  time).

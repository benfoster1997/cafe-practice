# Can you make £2,000 a month after tax from an AI-assisted YouTube channel?

**Full investigation — 10 August 2026**

This study was produced by a 14-agent research workflow: seven research agents
(each with live web access) investigated one dimension each, and seven
adversarial fact-checkers then tried to refute every key claim using
independent sources. Where a number could not be verified, or where sources
disagreed, that is said openly. Everything here follows the house rule:
honest numbers, no sugar-coating.

---

## The verdict, up front

**Possible? Yes. Likely? No — and the "AI does everything" version is the
version most likely to fail.**

1. **The mechanism is real.** Verified faceless channels earn well above
   £2,000/month (MagnatesMedia — a UK business-storytelling channel with
   ~1.9M subscribers — and Coffeehouse Crime — a UK true-crime narrator with
   ~2.2M subscribers — both exist, both are faceless, both are UK). Nothing
   in YouTube policy stops a compliant AI-assisted channel from monetising.

2. **The odds are brutal.** Roughly 90% of channels never reach 1,000
   subscribers. The median monetised creator earns about **£2,200 a year**,
   not £2,000 a month. One (rough) analysis puts the odds of a new channel
   ever reaching the ~£100/day level this target needs at about **1 in
   3,500**. The research found **no verified case anywhere of a solo
   beginner using only free tools reaching £2,000/month net within 12
   months** — every success story with receipts involved money spent, years
   of iteration, multiple channels, or a technical partner (and often the
   real income was selling a course about it).

3. **"Multi-million views" is the wrong target.** Shorts views are nearly
   worthless: TubeBuddy's own published experiment earned **$32 for exactly
   1,000,000 Shorts views**. The money is in long-form (8+ minute) videos
   watched by US/UK viewers. In a high-paying niche you need roughly
   **160,000–310,000 long-form views every month** for the target from ads
   alone — or ~350,000/month combined with sponsorships and affiliate income.

4. **YouTube is actively demonetising low-effort AI content.** The July 2025
   "inauthentic content" policy makes mass-produced, templated AI videos
   ineligible for monetisation, and enforcement is real (billion-view AI
   channels have been demonetised and then terminated). AI as a *tool* is
   explicitly fine; AI as the *whole pipeline* is exactly what gets rejected.

5. **Realistic timeline if everything goes unusually well:** first 6–12
   months mostly £0 (income only starts after ~1,000 subscribers + 4,000
   watch hours), £2,000/month plausible for a top-few-percent channel at
   **18–36 months**, usually with £5–30/month of paid tools by then. Treat
   this as a skill-building side project with quarterly review gates — not
   an income plan.

The step-by-step plan that maximises your odds within YouTube's rules is in
**`docs/YOUTUBE_ACTION_PLAN.md`**.

## 1. What £2,000/month after tax actually requires (verified against HMRC sources)

The tax dimension was the best-verified part of the study — 11 of 12 claims
confirmed against HMRC's own published calculation code and statute.

| Your situation | Pre-tax profit needed to net £24,000/yr |
|---|---|
| YouTube is your only income | **£28,016/yr ≈ £2,335/month** |
| Alongside a job that uses your personal allowance (all basic rate) | £31,413/yr ≈ £2,618/month |
| Alongside a £30,000 salary | **£35,540/yr ≈ £2,962/month** |

Arithmetic (sole income, rest-of-UK rates 2026/27): personal allowance
£12,570; above that each £1 loses 20p income tax + 6p Class 4 National
Insurance. £28,016 − £12,570 = £15,446 taxable → tax £3,089 + NI £927 =
£4,016 → net £24,000. (Scottish bands differ slightly.)

Key compliance facts (all confirmed):
- **£1,000 trading allowance**: under £1,000/yr gross YouTube income, no tax,
  no registration. Over it, register for Self Assessment by the 5 October
  after the tax year ends.
- **W-8BEN in AdSense**: without it Google can withhold 24% of *worldwide*
  earnings. With the US–UK treaty claim, US withholding is 0%. Ten-minute
  form, re-done every 3 years.
- **Payments on account**: the first tax bill is ~150% of one year's tax
  (~£6,000 in one lump at the target level). Budget for it.
- **Making Tax Digital**: at target income you'd be pulled into quarterly
  digital filing from April 2028.
- **VAT: irrelevant** — AdSense income is a supply to Google Ireland, outside
  UK VAT scope, and doesn't count toward the £90,000 threshold.
- AdSense pays monthly (UK threshold £60), about a month in arrears.

## 2. The real economics (why the views target is what it is)

- Long-form ads pay the creator 55% of net ad revenue. **RPM** (revenue per
  1,000 views, after everything) is the number that matters.
- Best dataset found (300 channels' real analytics, 2025–26): **all-niche
  median RPM ≈ £1.70 per 1,000 views**. Education/science was the top niche
  at ≈ £7.57 median. Finance/business creator reports run £6–£18+ with a
  US-heavy audience (softer sourcing — treat as a range, not a promise).
- **Views needed for £2,335/month from ads alone:** ~1.37M/month at the
  median RPM; ~630k at £3.70; ~310k at £7.57; ~160k at top-quartile finance
  rates. The fact-checker specifically warned against using optimistic UK
  RPM figures for planning — plan on the conservative end.
- **Shorts are a discovery tool, not income**: verified Shorts RPM is
  roughly 3–14% of long-form in the same niche; 11,000–34,000 Shorts views
  earn what 1,000 long-form views earn. Also, viral views *before*
  monetisation is approved pay £0, retroactively nothing.
- **Ads are not the whole income.** Across the creator economy, brand deals
  are ~49% of creator income vs ~23% from ads. The realistic route to target
  is ~350k long-form views/month **plus** 1–2 sponsor integrations
  (£200–£1,000+ each at 50k–100k subscribers) **plus** affiliate links —
  not ads alone.
- **Seasonality:** December RPM can be ~2× January (advertiser budgets).
  A £2,335 *average* month means over-earning in Q4.

## 3. The policy picture (what keeps the channel alive)

YouTube Partner Programme entry: **1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch
hours in 12 months** (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days — ignore this route),
2-step verification, no strikes, an AdSense account, and a **human/automated
review of the whole channel**. That review is where AI-assisted channels
pass or fail.

**The 15 July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy** (the make-or-break rule):
mass-produced or templated content is ineligible for monetisation —
explicitly including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal
templates… without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or
perspective", slideshow videos with minimal narrative, and readings of text
you didn't write. YouTube's own clarification: **"Channels using AI remain
eligible to monetize"** — the line is originality, not the technology.

**Enforcement is real:** fake-trailer channels with over a billion combined
views were demonetised (March 2025) then terminated (December 2025); press
reports describe a January 2026 wave of AI-channel terminations (~16
channels, ~35M combined subscribers — reported by multiple outlets, not
confirmed by YouTube itself); human-run faceless channels have reported
collateral demonetisation. An AI-heavy channel carries a real tail risk of
its income going to zero in one enforcement action.

**AI voiceover (text-to-speech) is not banned** — but it removes the easiest
signal of human authorship, so the script and editing have to carry the
"authentic" burden. Recording your own voice materially lowers this risk
(and is free).

**AI disclosure:** tick "Altered content: Yes" at upload for anything
realistic-looking that's synthetic. Disclosure doesn't hurt reach or
monetisation; repeated non-disclosure can bring removal or suspension.

**The never-do list (channel-killers):** templated mass production; reading
text you didn't write; misleading titles/thumbnails; deepfakes or voice
clones of real people (now machine-detectable by the victims); medical or
elections misinformation; buying subscribers/views; movie/TV/music clips on
a "fair use" hope (3 copyright strikes = channel deleted; fair use is a US
doctrine and the UK's "fair dealing" is narrower); mislabelling kids'
content; free-tier AI music (Suno/Udio free tiers are non-commercial) in
monetised videos.

## 4. Niche: where the views and the money overlap

Ranked for your exact situation (solo, no experience, free tools, needs high
revenue per view, must survive the inauthentic-content policy):

1. **Finance/business storytelling** — *recommended*. Stories about
   companies, frauds, collapses, fortunes — **not investment advice**.
   Highest RPM tier on YouTube, so the lowest views threshold (~160k–310k
   monthly at maturity). Proven faceless at scale by a UK creator
   (MagnatesMedia). The moat is research and writing, which costs time, not
   money. Policy-safe if story-led. Evergreen.
2. **History with a distinct angle** (e.g. British/military/maritime
   history as narrative). Huge evergreen demand and the best binge-watching
   behaviour, but mid RPM (needs ~500k+ monthly views) and it is the most
   AI-slop-flooded niche — you'd be swimming where the enforcement sweep is
   concentrated. Only viable if visibly non-slop.
3. **Mystery/disaster narration** (the "Scary Interesting" lane, avoiding
   true-crime's sharpest edges). Proven UK faceless winners; simple
   production; but ad-limited ("yellow icon") content drags RPM and the
   niche is under extra scrutiny after an AI channel fabricated crimes.

**Avoid:** meditation/ambience and top-10 lists (highest monetisation
rejection rates — policy kill zones), kids' content (COPPA; RPM ~£0.25–£1.50),
AI news (daily churn, no evergreen value), sports (low RPM + clip copyright),
luxury/motivation slideshows (saturated, flagged), and any Shorts-first plan.

A note on trust: niche RPM figures and named-channel stats are the
softest-sourced part of this study (YouTube publishes no RPM data). The
*ordering* of niches is consistent across every source; the exact numbers
are ±50%.

## 5. The free tools question (direct answer)

**There is no genuinely free AI video generator that gives watermark-free
footage with commercial rights. None.** Every mainstream tool watermarks
free output, forbids commercial use on the free tier, or both — and the
free allowances are seconds per day against the ~600 seconds a 10-minute
video needs. Sora is discontinued; Haiper is dead; any guide recommending
them is stale.

What actually exists (verified August 2026, specs move monthly):

| Tool | Free reality |
|---|---|
| **Google Flow** (flow.google) | 50 credits/day ≈ ~5 Veo "Fast" clips or ~10 "Lite" clips of 8s, watermarked. Best recurring free allowance. |
| **create.wan.video** | 10 credits/day + unlimited slow-queue generation (Wan is Apache-licensed). Best free volume if you tolerate queues. |
| **Runway** | The only tool granting free-tier *commercial rights* — but 125 credits **once ever** (~25 seconds), watermarked. A demo, not a tool. |
| Kling / PixVerse / Hailuo / Luma / Pika / Vidu | Daily/monthly free credits but **no commercial rights on free tiers** — using them in a monetised video breaches their terms. |
| HunyuanVideo (open-source) | **Licence excludes the UK entirely** — not usable at all. |
| Wan 2.2 local (open-source, Apache) | Legally clean and free, but needs a ~24GB-VRAM GPU and technical setup — not realistic for you now. |

**So the honest answer to "best free AI video generator" is: for filling
videos, don't use one.** The winning £0 stack for a faceless channel is:

- **Script:** Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini free tiers (with your own fact-checking
  and rewriting — this is also what keeps you policy-safe).
- **Visuals:** Pexels + Pixabay stock footage (verified free for monetised
  use, no attribution) + free AI images (Ideogram 10/day — commercial-safe;
  Gemini ~20/day). **Not** Bing/Copilot Image Creator (non-commercial terms).
- **AI video accents:** Google Flow's daily credits + create.wan.video,
  sprinkled in, disclosed.
- **Voiceover:** your own voice (recommended — free and the strongest
  policy protection), or Kokoro TTS (open-source, Apache, genuinely free
  for commercial use). **Not ElevenLabs free tier** (non-commercial only —
  the £4/month Starter is the legal minimum, and the single best first
  upgrade).
- **Editing:** CapCut free or Clipchamp free (both 1080p, no watermark) →
  DaVinci Resolve free later. Note CapCut's 2025 terms grant ByteDance a
  broad licence to content processed through it — legal to use, worth
  knowing.
- **Music:** YouTube Audio Library only (guaranteed no Content ID claims).
- **Thumbnails:** Canva free + Ideogram.
- **SEO/captions:** vidIQ free tier; paste your own script into YouTube's
  caption tool for near-perfect captions.

Total: **£0/month**, fully legal for a monetised channel. First upgrades
when income justifies: ElevenLabs Starter (~£4/mo), then Google AI Pro
(£18.99/mo) for real Veo volume.

## 6. What actually happens to people who try this (base rates)

Of 100 people who start a faceless/AI channel with free tools and no
experience, the evidence suggests:

- **At 6 months:** 50–70 have quit; perhaps 2–5 are monetised at all;
  those few earn £10–£150/month.
- **At 12 months:** 75–85 have stopped; 5–15 are monetised, mostly at
  £20–£300/month gross; 1–3 have found a real edge and might be at
  £500–£1,500/month (almost always paying for tools by now);
  **expected number reaching £2,000/month net: essentially zero**
  (best estimate well below 1).

Time to 1,000 subscribers among those who persist: typically **8–15 months
and 20–30+ videos**. The documented successes share: original scripts, one
tightly-defined repeatable format, a recognisable narrator, 1–2 quality
uploads/week rather than daily volume — the exact opposite of the
"30 videos a month automation" playbook, which is now also the profile the
policy demonetises.
